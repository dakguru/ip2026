Q1. What is the maximum prescribed weight limit for an air-mail bag or air Transit Bag (TB)?
A. 10 kg
B. 14 kg
C. 28 kg
D. 30 kg
Answer: D
Explanation: Under Rule 26-A of Postal Manual Volume V, the weight of an air-mail bag or air Transit Bag (TB) should not exceed 30 kg.

Q2. Who is the designated officer in charge of a Transit Mail Office (TMO)?
A. Head Record Officer
B. Head Sorting Assistant
C. Mail Agent or Mail Guard
D. Sub Record Officer
Answer: C
Explanation: Per Rule 9-B, a TMO is a stationary branch dealing exclusively with closed bags. A Mail Agent or Mail Guard is in charge, whereas a Head Sorting Assistant heads a Sorting Mail Office.

Q3. Which distinguishing mark must precede the number on a Postal Service registered article?
A. Ins
B. V.P.
C. Cipher O
D. R.B.
Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 85 mandates distinguishing marks to prevent misidentification. Postal Service registered articles must bear the Cipher "O", distinguishing them from insured (Ins) or V.P. items.

Q11. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding late letters under the current framework?

1.The special late fee for window posting has been retained under the Post Office Regulations, 2024.
2.Any article presented at the window during prescribed hours is accepted for dispatch without a separate late fee.
3."Too Late" letters are dispatched with double the late fee.

A. 1 only  
B. 2 only  
C. 2 and 3 only  
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation: Only statement 2 is correct. There is no separate "late fee" for window posting. It was removed when G.S.R. 912(E) dated 29.12.2022 substituted Rule 196 of the Indian Post Office Rules, 1933 — the substituted rule simply provides that any article presented at the window during the hours the PMG prescribes is accepted for dispatch. The 1933 Rules were themselves superseded on 16.12.2024 by the Post Office Rules, 2024 (G.S.R. 767(E)) and Regulations, 2024 (S.O. 5440(E)), but the same position carries forward, so statement 1 (fee "retained") is wrong. Statement 3 is wrong because the "Too Late" category turned entirely on non-payment of that late fee and is now of historical interest only — there is no "double late fee.

Q13. Which of the following statements is/are not correct regarding investigation limits?
1.An Inspector of Posts can investigate loss/fraud cases involving principal amounts up to ₹2 lakh.
2.A Divisional Head investigates cases above ₹2 lakh up to ₹10 lakh.
3.All loss/fraud cases above ₹5 lakh must be reported to the Directorate.

A. 1 and 2 only 
B. 2 and 3 only 
C. 1 and 3 only 
D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: B
Explanation: Statements 2 and 3 are the incorrect ones. Under Rule 175, Vol V (limits notified by the Directorate w.e.f. 23/24.09.2021), the investigating authority by principal amount is: Inspector/ASPOs — up to ₹2 lakh; Divisional Head/Chief or Sr. PM — above ₹2 lakh to ₹5 lakh; AD/APMG (Region/Circle) — above ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh; Director Postal Services & above — above ₹10 lakh. So statement 2 is wrong (the DH is capped at ₹5 lakh; ₹5–10 lakh is the AD/APMG's range), and statement 3 is wrong (only cases exceeding ₹10 lakh, raised from the earlier ₹5 lakh, are reported by the Circle to the Directorate). Statement 1 is correct. Separately, under Rule 34, GFR 2017, all losses above ₹50,000 from suspected fire/theft/fraud are reported to the Police.
