Q1. Which of the following identity documents is explicitly required to be submitted to the Accounts Office within six months if an individual furnishes only a proof of application of enrolment at the time of opening an account?
A. Permanent Account Number
B. Aadhaar Number
C. Voter Identity Card
D. Passport
Answer: B
Explanation: Where the Aadhaar Number has not been assigned, the individual shall furnish proof of application of enrolment and must submit the Aadhaar Number within 6 months from the date of opening the account.

Q2. Consider the following statements regarding the operation of a Government Savings Bank account by an illiterate depositor:

1. An illiterate depositor is permitted to open a joint account with a literate depositor.
2. The thumb impression of the illiterate depositor must be witnessed by an independent witness on each occasion of withdrawal.
3. An illiterate depositor can operate the account through any literate individual whom they authorize for this purpose.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation: An illiterate depositor may open a joint account with a literate depositor. Thumb impressions of an illiterate depositor must be witnessed by an independent witness on each withdrawal. The rule authorizing a literate individual applies to physically infirm or differently-abled persons, not merely illiterate depositors.

Q3. An individual who has not submitted their Permanent Account Number (PAN) at the time of opening an account must submit it within two months if which of the following events occurs?
A. The aggregate of all deposits in a financial year exceeds Rs. 50,000.
B. The balance at any point of time in the account exceeds Rs. 100,000.
C. The aggregate of all withdrawals and transfers in a month exceeds Rs. 10,000.
D. The account holder applies for a partial withdrawal of Rs. 20,000.
Answer: C
Explanation: The PAN must be submitted within two months if the balance exceeds Rs. 50,000, the aggregate of all accounts in a financial year exceeds Rs. 1,00,000, or aggregate withdrawals and transfers in a month exceed Rs. 10,000.

Q4. If a depositor in a single account subsequently becomes a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) during the period the account is in operation, what shall be the statutory status of the account?
A. The account shall be closed immediately and the balance refunded without interest.
B. The account shall be deemed to be closed from the last day of the preceding month.
C. The account may be continued till maturity on a non-repatriation basis but cannot be extended.
D. The account can be extended beyond maturity but will earn interest at the Post Office Savings Account rate.
Answer: C
Explanation: If a depositor becomes an NRI, the account may be continued till its maturity on a non-repatriation basis. It shall not be allowed to be extended beyond maturity, and no interest shall be payable after its maturity date.

Q5. Consider the following statements regarding the transfer of an account as security (pledging):

1. An account may be transferred as security to a Local Authority.
2. The transfer of a minor's account requires the guardian to certify in writing that the minor is alive and the transfer is for the minor's benefit.
3. The transferee of an account under this rule shall be deemed to be the depositor until it is re-transferred back.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: D
Explanation: An account may be transferred to a local authority. For a minor's account, the guardian must certify the minor is alive and the transfer benefits the minor. The transferee is deemed the depositor until the account is re-transferred.

Q6. A depositor wishes to convert their existing Joint 'A' type account into a Single Account. Under which of the following circumstances is this conversion legally permissible?
A. When all joint account holders provide written consent.
B. Only in the case of a single surviving joint account holder.
C. After the expiry of three years from the date of opening the account.
D. Conversion from a Joint to a Single account is strictly prohibited under all circumstances.
Answer: B
Explanation: An account opened as a Single Account cannot be converted into a Joint Account or vice versa, provided that the conversion of a Joint Account into a single account shall be allowed only in the case of a single surviving Joint Account holder.

Q7. According to Schedule II of the Government Savings Promotion General Rules, 2018 (as amended with effect from 2nd April 2025), what fee is chargeable for the cancellation or change of nomination in a savings account?
A. Rs. 20
B. Rs. 50
C. Rs. 100
D. Nil
Answer: D
Explanation: The fee of Rs. 50 earlier prescribed under Schedule II for "Cancellation or change of nomination" was deleted entirely by the Government Savings Promotion General (Amendment) Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 214(E) dated 02.04.2025), circulated through SB Order 05/2025 dated 03.04.2025. No fee is now chargeable for cancellation or change of nomination; the service is free with effect from 2nd April 2025.

Q8. Which of the following statements is not correct regarding the nomination rules in a Government Savings Bank account?
A. A depositor may nominate up to four individuals as nominees.
B. An NRI can be nominated subject to the condition that payment shall be on a non-repatriation basis.
C. In an account opened on behalf of a minor, the minor can nominate an individual upon attaining the age of ten years.
D. Any nomination made shall stand cancelled upon the transfer of the account as security.
Answer: C
Explanation: In an account opened by or on behalf of a minor, the nomination shall be made by the guardian, who may nominate any individual, including himself. A minor cannot make the nomination independently.

Q9. In the event of a depositor's death without a nomination, where the eligible amount does not exceed Rs. 5 lakh, the authorized officer may pay the claimant after six months on submission of an application. Which of the following documents is not explicitly required for this claim?
A. Death certificate of the account holder
B. Letter of disclaimer in Form-14
C. Succession certificate issued by the Court
D. Bond of Indemnity in Form-15
Answer: C
Explanation: If the amount does not exceed Rs. 5 lakh and no succession certificate is produced within six months, payment can be made with a Death certificate, Passbook, Affidavit, Letter of disclaimer, Bond of Indemnity, and Identity proof. A succession certificate is only required if a dispute arises or the amount exceeds Rs. 5 lakh.

Q10. A member of the Indian Air Force dies while his single account is active. Who among the following is legally empowered to send a requisition for the payment of the eligible amount, superseding any active nomination?

Explanation: Under the Army and Air Force Act, the Commanding Officer or the Committee of Adjustment may send a requisition for payment, and the authorized officer is bound to comply even if a valid nomination is in force.

Q11. What mandatory action must the Accounts Office take upon detecting that interest has been credited erroneously to a depositor's account?

Q17. Which of the following provisions is correct regarding the recovery of an excess amount erroneously paid to a depositor or nominee?

Q20. How is interest calculated on deposits in a Post Office Savings Account?

Answer: C
Explanation: Interest is calculated at 4% per annum on the lowest balance at the credit of an account between the close of the tenth day and the end of the month.

Q21. After how many completed financial years of no deposit or withdrawal is a Post Office Savings Account officially treated as a Silent Account?

Q28. How is a fraction of a rupee treated when interest is credited to a Post Office Savings Account?

Explanation: Interest is credited in whole rupees after being rounded off to the nearest rupee. An amount of Rs. 0.50 or more is rounded up to Rs. 1, and less than Rs. 0.50 is rounded down to Rs. 0.

Q37. Consider the following statements about defaults in an RD account:

Q61. Consider the following statements about interest payment in Time Deposits:

Q71. What is the penalty for premature closure of an MIS account after the expiry of one year but before the expiry of three years?

Q72. When is the monthly interest payable if an MIS deposit is made on the 31st of a month, and the following month has only 30 days?

Q73. Consider the following statements regarding excess deposits made in an MIS account:
