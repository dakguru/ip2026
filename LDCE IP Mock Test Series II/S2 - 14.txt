Q1. Under Fundamental Rules, which of the following best defines a "Fee" as opposed to an "Honorarium"?
A. A non-recurring payment granted from the Consolidated Fund of India for occasional work.
B. A recurring payment granted from the Consolidated Fund of India for special work.
C. A recurring or non-recurring payment from a source other than the Consolidated Fund.
D. A recurring payment from a State Government for performing official duty on a tour.
Answer: C
Explanation:
As per FR 9(6-A), a "Fee" is a payment from a source other than the Consolidated Fund of India, whereas an "Honorarium" under FR 9(9) is paid from the Consolidated Fund of India.

Q2. Which of the following statements regarding a Government servant's "Lien" is/are correct?

1. It represents the right to hold a regular post.
2. It can be retained during foreign service.
3. It applies to a post on which probation is applicable.
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation:
Under FR 9(13), a lien is the title to hold a regular post on which probation is not applicable. FR 13 allows retention of lien during foreign service, holding a temporary post, or officiating in another post.



Q3. What is the maximum limit for Non-Practising Allowance (NPA) when combined with basic pay for medical posts?

Answer: B
Explanation:
According to FR 9(21), Non-Practising Allowance (NPA) to medical posts is 20% of basic pay. Basic pay plus NPA must not exceed the pay at Cabinet Secretary level, which is Rs. 2,37,500.

Q4. Which of the following periods is NOT treated as "Duty" under the Fundamental Rules?
A. Time spent by a civil servant in the Territorial Army on training.
B. The day of an obligatory departmental examination, such as the LDCE.
C. Pre-appointment training for the purpose of granting MACP benefits.

As per Government Decisions under FR 9(6), pre-appointment training is treated as duty for increments and qualifying service for pension, but NOT for MACP. Other options are explicitly treated as duty.

Q5. An official is placed under suspension on a criminal charge but is later fully exonerated. The pay computed for the past period of suspension is termed as:
A. Officiating Pay
B. Substantive Pay
C. Presumptive Pay
D. Compensatory Pay
Answer: C
Explanation:
Under FR 9(24), presumptive pay is the pay to which a Government servant would be entitled if he held the post substantively. Upon exoneration, re-computed pay for the suspension period is presumptive pay.
