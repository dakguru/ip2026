1. In the case of Delhi Special Police Establishment, not below the rank of Inspector of Police.
2. In metropolitan areas of Bombay, Calcutta, Madras, Ahmedabad and any other metropolitan area, not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner of Police.
3. Elsewhere, not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.
4. Criminal misconduct shall not be investigated without the order of a police officer not below the rank of Superintendent of Police (applicable to J&K).
Which of the above statements are correct?
A. 1, 2 and 3 only
B. 1, 3 and 4 only
C. 1, 2, 3 and 4
D. 2, 3 and 4 only

Answer: C

Explanation:
Section 17 prescribes the minimum investigating ranks: Inspector of Police for Delhi SPE (Statement 1); ACP for metropolitan areas (Statement 2); DSP or equivalent elsewhere (Statement 3). The proviso regarding J&K requires not below the rank of Superintendent of Police for criminal misconduct (Statement 4). All four statements are correct.


Q46. (Prevention of Corruption Act 1988) [Difficulty: Moderate]
Under Section 5 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, the Court of a Special Judge shall be deemed to be:
A. A Court of Magistrate
B. A Court of Session
C. A High Court
D. A District Court

Answer: B

Explanation:
Section 5(2) provides that the Court of a Special Judge shall be deemed to be a Court of Session, and the person conducting a prosecution before a Special Judge shall be deemed to be a public prosecutor.


Q47. (Prevention of Corruption Act 1988) [Difficulty: Hard]
Under Section 17A of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, regarding prior approval for investigation of offences relatable to recommendations made by public servants in discharge of official functions:
1. Prior approval of the Central Government is required for persons employed in connection with the affairs of the Union.
2. Prior approval of the State Government is required for persons employed in connection with the affairs of a State.
3. No such approval is necessary for cases involving arrest on the spot on the charge of accepting undue advantage.
4. The concerned authority shall convey its decision within three months, extendable by a further period of one month.
Which of the above statements are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1, 2 and 3 only
C. 1, 2, 3 and 4
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

Answer: C

Explanation:
Section 17A requires prior approval of the respective Government (Statements 1 and 2 correct). The proviso exempts cases involving arrest on the spot for accepting undue advantage (Statement 3). The authority must convey its decision within three months, extendable by one month (Statement 4). All four are correct.


Q48. (Prevention of Corruption Act 1988) [Difficulty: Moderate]
Under Section 13 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, criminal misconduct by a public servant includes:
1. Dishonest misappropriation of property entrusted to him.
2. Intentional illicit enrichment during the period of his office.
3. Accepting gifts from relatives during festivals.
Which of the above constitute criminal misconduct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: A

Explanation:
Section 13(1) defines criminal misconduct as: (a) dishonest or fraudulent misappropriation of property entrusted to the public servant; and (b) intentional illicit enrichment during the period of office. Accepting gifts from relatives during festivals is not specifically covered under the definition of criminal misconduct.


Q49. (Prevention of Corruption Act 1988) [Difficulty: Easy]
Under Section 6 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, in a summary trial, a Special Judge may pass a sentence of imprisonment for a term NOT exceeding:
A. Six months
B. One year
C. Two years
D. Three years

Answer: B

Explanation:
Section 6(1) provides that in the case of any conviction in a summary trial under this section, the Special Judge may pass a sentence of imprisonment for a term not exceeding one year. If the sentence exceeds one year, the case is re-heard as per the regular procedure for warrant cases.


Q50. (Central Administrative Tribunal Act 1985) [Difficulty: Hard]
A government employee, aggrieved by an order, filed a departmental appeal which was rejected. Six months later, without the final order on appeal being communicated, the employee seeks to approach the CAT. Under Section 21 of the CAT Act, the limitation period in this case would be:
A. One year from the date of the original order
B. One year from the date on which the appeal was filed
C. One year from the date of expiry of the said period of six months
D. Six months from the date the appeal was rejected

Answer: C

Explanation:
Section 21(1)(b) provides that where an appeal or representation has been made and a period of six months has expired without a final order having been made, the limitation is one year from the date of expiry of the said period of six months. This is a critical provision for scenario-based questions testing the interplay between departmental remedies and Tribunal limitation.
