Question:

Question:
When is a Money Order (MO) treated as "Void"?

Answer:
A Money Order is treated as void if it remains unpaid to either the payee or the remitter at the end of the **second month following the month of its issue**.

Explanation:
Once an MO is void, it cannot be paid in the usual course. This is distinct from "Forfeiture." A void MO requires special clearance to be re-issued or paid, whereas a *forfeited* MO (unpaid for more than 3 years) becomes the revenue of the Government and is completely lost to the remitter/payee.

2. 
**24 to 36 months:** Encashable with a **second commission** paid via postage stamps affixed to the back.


3. 
**Beyond 36 months:** The IPO is **forfeited** and will not be paid.



Explanation:
The validity is calculated from the last day of the month of issue. For example, an IPO issued in January 2024 is valid without extra charge until January 2026. Between Feb 2026 and Jan 2027, it requires a second commission. After Jan 2027, it is invalid.
