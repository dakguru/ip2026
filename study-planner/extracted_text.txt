Modern Application:   In the   APT 2.0   environment, the "Despatch" is instantaneous upon  booking. The physical forms (M.O. 8) are retained at the booking office (except for specific  audits), and the data is transmitted electronically. The   principles   of examination and  responsibility, however, r emain valid for exams.  19. Check of money orders   (1) The Money Order Assistant must examine the money  orders to see that they are correctly filled in and that the amounts of commission have  been correctly calculated. (2) He must then impress the   date - stamp   of the office on the  money orders and the receipts. (3) The signature of the Assistant on the receipt (M.O. 1)  is a guarantee that the amount of the order and commission has been brought to account.

Examination by Postmaster   (1) The Postmaster must ex amine the money orders and  the list of money orders issued (Journal) to see that the entries in the list agree with the  money orders. (2) He must verify that the total amount of the money orders and  commission has been correctly entered in the accounts. (3 ) He must then sign the money  orders and the list (Journal). (4) In Head Offices, the Postmaster may delegate this duty  to a designated Supervisor or Deputy Postmaster, but the ultimate responsibility remains  with him.  23. Despatch of money orders   (1) The   money orders, sorted according to the offices of  payment or the offices of exchange, must be entered in the   Money Order Check List  (M.O. 70) or the Registered List. (2) The money orders must be despatched to the office  of payment or the sorting office in t he manner prescribed for registered articles.  🔔   STATUTORY   UPDATE   (2025):   eMO   Transmission   (Ref:   Dept   of   Posts   IT  Handbook 2024)     Under the   eMO (Electronic Money Order)   system, the "Despatch" is electronic.     Once the counter PA commits the transaction in the   Point of Sale (POS) module,  the money order is effectively "despatched" to the central server.     The destination office (SO or HO) downloads the eMO   data for payment. Physical  M.O. forms do not travel to the destination; they are preserved at the booking  office. Payment of money orders  (1) A money order must be paid to the payee named in it or to some person authorised  in writing by the payee in this behalf. Identification of payee  (1) If the payee is not known to the postman or the officer who pays the money order, he  must be ide ntified by a person known to the postman or such officer.  (2) The identifier must sign the money order in the place provided for the purpose and  add his address.  36. Payment to illiterate payees  (1) If the payee is unable to write, he must produce a witnes s to attest his mark or thumb -  impression on the money order and on the acknowledgment.  (2) The witness must sign the money order and the acknowledgment and add his address.

Payment to purdahnashin women  (1) When a money order is payable to a purdahnashin woman who has not an agent  authorised to receive payment, the signature or mark of the payee must be attested by a  witness who is known to the postman or the officer making the payment. Payment to minors  (1) A money order payable to a minor must be paid to the father or lawful guardian of  the minor.  (2) The father   or guardian must sign the money order and the acknowledgment on behalf  of the minor.  (3) If the minor has no father or lawful guardian living with him, the money order may  be paid to the person in whose care and custody the minor is for the time being, pro vided  that such person signs an indemnity bond in the prescribed form.  39. Payment to lunatics  (1) A money order payable to a lunatic must be paid to the manager of his estate, if one  has been appointed.  (2) If no manager has been appointed, the money orde r may be paid to the person in  whose care and custody the lunatic is for the time being, on his signing an indemnity  bond in the prescribed form.

While the  core procedures (redirection, alteration) remain operational, the legal framework is now  supported by   Sec tion 4 (Postal Services)   of the   Post Office Act, 2023 , which grants the  Central Government the power to prescribe rates and conditions for these services.  41. Refusal of payment by payee  (1) If the payee refuses to take payment of a money order, the amount   of the order shall  be returned to the remitter.  (2) The commission paid on the issue of the money order shall not be refunded.  (3) The money order shall be returned to the office of issue superscribed with the word  "Refused" and the signature of the postm an or officer to whom the payment was refused.  CHAPTER V: ALTERATION OF ADDRESS OR PAYEE'S NAME  42. Alteration of address or name of payee  (1) The remitter of a money order which has not been paid may require that the address  of the payee shall be altere d or that the name of the payee shall be changed.  (2) The remitter must apply in writing to the Post office at which the order was issued,  and must produce the receipt given to him for the order.  (3) If the money order has not left the office of issue, the   alteration will be made at once  without charge.  (4) If the money order has been despatched, the application will be forwarded to the office  of payment.
