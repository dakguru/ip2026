# CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021 - High-Quality MCQs (Part 1: Q1 to Q25)

Q1. Consider the following statements regarding the applicability of the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 2021:
1. They apply to Government servants appointed on or before 31st December 2003.
2. They are applicable to railway servants and persons in casual employment.
3. They apply to civilian Government servants in the Defence Services who are paid from the Defence Services Estimates.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021 apply strictly to Government servants appointed substantively to civil services and posts before January 1, 2004. Civilian employees in Defence Services paid from Defence Estimates are covered.
Elimination: Railway servants, persons in casual and daily-rated employment, and those paid from contingencies are explicitly excluded from these rules. Thus, statement 2 is incorrect.

Q2. Under the provisions for withholding or withdrawing pension, which authority holds the absolute power to withhold a pension permanently if the pensioner is found guilty of grave misconduct?
A. The Head of the Department
B. The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)
C. The President of India
D. The Comptroller and Auditor General
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: The power to withhold or withdraw a pension, either temporarily or permanently, or to recover pecuniary loss from a pension, is vested exclusively in the President of India if, in any departmental or judicial proceedings, the pensioner is found guilty of grave misconduct or negligence during their service.
Elimination: The UPSC is consulted during the process, and the Head of the Department monitors the proceedings, but the final authority resides solely with the President.

Q3. A retired Group 'A' officer wishes to accept commercial employment immediately after retirement. Which of the following conditions correctly applies to this scenario?
A. The officer can accept employment without any prior permission.
B. The officer must obtain prior sanction from the Government if the employment is within one year of retirement.
C. The officer must obtain prior sanction from the Government if the employment is within two years of retirement.
D. Commercial employment is completely prohibited for Group 'A' officers post-retirement.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Under the rules restricting post-retirement commercial employment, a Group 'A' officer is required to obtain prior sanction from the Government to accept any commercial employment before the expiry of one year from the date of their retirement. (Note: The cooling-off period was previously two years but has been rationalized to one year).
Elimination: Complete prohibition is legally untenable, and accepting without permission leads to withholding of pension. The defined period is one year, making Option C incorrect.

Q4. With reference to the treatment of the period of suspension, consider the following statements:
1. The period of suspension unconditionally counts as qualifying service for pension.
2. If a Government servant is fully exonerated, the period of suspension is treated as duty and counts towards qualifying service.
3. If the suspension is not completely unjustified, the competent authority may direct that the period shall not count as qualifying service.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Suspension periods do not automatically count as qualifying service. The treatment of the period depends on the final outcome of the proceedings. If exonerated, it counts fully. If the authority deems the suspension was justified (even if a minor penalty is imposed), they have the discretion to direct that the period does not count as qualifying service.
Elimination: Statement 1 is false because it asserts the period "unconditionally" counts, ignoring the necessary regularization by a competent authority.

Q5. A Government servant who is dismissed or removed from service forfeits their pension and gratuity. However, the authority competent to dismiss them may grant a specific monetary relief in cases deserving special consideration. What is this allowance called?
A. Invalid Pension
B. Compensation Pension
C. Compassionate Allowance
D. Ex-gratia Pension
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Dismissal or removal entails forfeiture of past service. To prevent severe destitution in deserving cases, the competent authority may sanction a Compassionate Allowance, which cannot exceed two-thirds of the pension or gratuity (or both) that would have been admissible if the official had retired on medical invalidation.
Elimination: Invalid pension applies to medical incapacitation. Compensation pension applies upon abolition of a permanent post.

Q6. Identify the correct provision regarding "Average Emoluments" used for calculating basic pension:
A. It is determined by the average of the emoluments drawn during the last 12 months of service.
B. It is determined by the average of the emoluments drawn during the last 10 months of service.
C. It is the basic pay drawn on the exact day of retirement.
D. It includes Basic Pay, Dearness Allowance, and House Rent Allowance.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Average emoluments for the purpose of pension calculation are ascertained based on the basic pay drawn by the Government servant during the last ten months of their service.
Elimination: Emoluments for pension do not include DA or HRA; they consist primarily of basic pay. The calculation period is ten months, not twelve. Note that under current rules, pension is calculated as 50% of the last pay drawn OR 50% of average emoluments, whichever is higher, but the definition of average emoluments remains the 10-month average.

Q7. Match List I (Type of Pension) with List II (Condition for Grant) and select the correct answer:
List I

List II
1. Granted as a penalty under CCS (CCA) Rules.
2. Granted upon the abolition of a permanent post.
3. Granted upon voluntary retirement after 20 years of qualifying service.
4. Granted when a medical authority certifies incapacitation for further service.

A. P-4, Q-2, R-1, S-3
B. P-4, Q-1, R-2, S-3
C. P-2, Q-4, R-1, S-3
D. P-2, Q-4, R-3, S-1

Explanation:
Concept: Invalid Pension (4) is for medical incapacitation. Compensation Pension (2) is granted when a post is abolished and the employee cannot be accommodated elsewhere. Compulsory Retirement Pension (1) is a penalty decided by a disciplinary authority. Retiring Pension (3) applies to voluntary retirement after completing the requisite qualifying service (e.g., 20 years).

Q8. Which of the following correctly describes the "Qualifying Service" required for a Government servant to be eligible for a standard Superannuation Pension?
A. A minimum of 5 years of continued service.
B. A minimum of 10 years of continued service.
C. A minimum of 20 years of continued service.
D. A minimum of 33 years of continued service.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: A Government servant becomes eligible for a regular superannuation pension (calculated pro-rata or at 50% of last pay drawn as per current norms) upon completing a minimum of 10 years of qualifying service.
Elimination: Less than 10 years of service entitles the person to service gratuity only, not a monthly pension. 20 years is the threshold for voluntary retirement.

Q9. In the context of the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, if a Government servant is missing and their whereabouts are not known, when can the family become eligible to claim Family Pension?
A. Immediately after filing a missing person report (FIR).
B. Six months after lodging a report with the police, provided the official is not traced.
C. One year after a police report and the submission of an indemnity bond by the family.
D. Only after seven years when the person is legally presumed dead under the Indian Evidence Act.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The rules provide a welfare measure where the family of a missing Government servant can apply for family pension and death gratuity six months after lodging a Police Report (FIR), provided the police certify the person is untraceable and the family executes an indemnity bond.
Elimination: Waiting seven years (the legal presumption of death) would cause undue hardship, so the rules explicitly shorten this to six months. One year is incorrect.

Q10. An unmarried Government servant passed away during service. In what order of preference will the family pension be granted to the surviving family members?
A. Mother, followed by father, followed by unmarried dependent siblings.
B. Father, followed by mother, followed by dependent sisters.
C. Parents jointly, followed by all siblings equally.
D. The pension must be split 50-50 between the mother and the father.
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
Concept: In the absence of a spouse and children, the family pension is first payable to the dependent parents. Between the parents, the mother receives it first. Upon her death, it passes to the father. If both parents are deceased, it passes to dependent, unmarried/widowed/divorced disabled siblings.
Elimination: Options distributing it jointly or placing the father first directly contradict the explicit hierarchy established in the Pension Rules.

Q11. Consider the following statements regarding the "Retirement Gratuity":
1. It is payable to a Government servant who completes five years of qualifying service and retires.
2. The amount of retirement gratuity is equal to one-fourth of the month's basic pay for each completed six-monthly period of qualifying service.
3. The maximum limit for retirement gratuity is capped at ₹20 Lakhs, subject to periodic Dearness Allowance revisions.
Which of the statements given above is/are NOT correct?
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. 3 only
D. None of the above (All are correct)
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
Concept: To be eligible for Retirement Gratuity, a minimum of five years of qualifying service is mandatory. The formula is 1/4th of a month's emoluments for each completed six-monthly period (up to a maximum of 16.5 times the monthly emoluments). The absolute ceiling is set by the Pay Commission (currently ₹20 Lakhs, rising by 25% when DA reaches 50%).
Elimination: Since all segments accurately reflect the statutory limits and eligibility criteria, none of the statements are incorrect.

Q12. The rules stipulate enhanced family pension. For what duration is the "Enhanced Family Pension" payable to the family following the death of a Government servant in service?
A. For a period of 5 years from the date following the date of death.
B. For a period of 7 years or up to the date on which the deceased would have attained the age of 67 years, whichever is earlier.
C. For a period of 10 years from the date following the date of death, without any age constraints of the deceased.
D. For the entire lifetime of the spouse.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Under the modern CCS (Pension) Rules (and amended by recent Pay Commissions), when a Government servant dies while in service, the enhanced family pension (50% of the last pay drawn) is payable for a continuous period of 10 years from the date following the date of death.
Elimination: The previous rule of 7 years/67 years was replaced for in-service deaths to provide greater financial stability for 10 straight years.

Q13. In the event of the death of a Government servant after completing 20 years of qualifying service, how is the "Death Gratuity" calculated?
A. 12 times of monthly emoluments.
B. 20 times of monthly emoluments.
C. Half of the monthly emoluments for every completed six-monthly period of qualifying service subject to a maximum of 33 times of emoluments.
D. A flat sum of ₹20 Lakhs.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Death gratuity is calculated based on the length of service. For service of 20 years or more, the family is entitled to half of the monthly emoluments for every completed six-monthly period of qualifying service, capped at a maximum of 33 times of emoluments (or the absolute ceiling limit, e.g., ₹20 Lakhs, whichever is less).
Elimination: "12 times" and "20 times" represent lower service band brackets (e.g., less than 11 years, and 11 to 20 years).

Q14. An official takes Extraordinary Leave (EOL) without a medical certificate. How does this period impact their qualifying service for pension?
A. It unconditionally counts as qualifying service.
B. It is completely ignored and causes a break in service.
C. It does not count as qualifying service unless specifically authorized by the competent authority on grounds of inability to join duty.
D. It counts as half of the qualifying service period.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Extraordinary Leave (EOL) granted without a medical certificate does not normally count as qualifying service. However, the appointing authority can direct that it shall count if the leave was taken due to civil commotion, prosecuting higher studies useful to public service, or inability to join duty for reasons beyond the official's control.
Elimination: It does not cause a "break in service" (which would forfeit all previous service), but the period itself is typically excluded from the total tally unless specifically exempted.

Q15. As per the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, if a pensioner is convicted of a serious crime by a Court of Law, what action can the Government take regarding their pension?
A. The pension is automatically suspended for the duration of the jail term without any formal order.
B. The Government may withhold or withdraw the pension in whole or in part after serving a show-cause notice.
C. The pension remains unaffected as pension is an absolute fundamental right.
D. Only the Family Pension to dependents is stopped, while the pensioner continues to receive it in jail.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Pension is subject to future good conduct. If an individual is convicted of a serious crime, the President has the authority to withhold or withdraw the pension (wholly or partly, temporarily or permanently). The pensioner is given a show-cause notice based on the judicial conviction before the final order is passed.
Elimination: The action is not "automatic" without an order, and pension is not an unassailable right if grave crimes are proven. 

Q16. Under what circumstances can a "Provisional Pension" be sanctioned?
A. When the official is undergoing a regular transfer.
B. When departmental or judicial proceedings are pending against the Government servant at the time of retirement.
C. When the official takes voluntary retirement.
D. When the official completes 10 years of service and asks for an advance.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: If departmental or judicial proceedings are pending against a Government servant on the date of their retirement, they are authorized to receive a Provisional Pension (usually 100% of standard pension) until the conclusion of the proceedings.
Elimination: Gratuity is withheld in such cases, but provisional pension is paid to ensure survival. Options A, C, and D do not require provisional setups; regular processes apply.

Q17. Which of the following conditions is mandatory to receive an "Addition to Qualifying Service" upon voluntary retirement under Rule 43?
A. The official must have a severe disability.
B. The official must be retiring from a scientific or technical post.
C. "Addition to qualifying service" mapping to age weightage has been completely abolished and is no longer granted for voluntary retirement.
D. The official must have completed 30 years of actual service.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Previously, a weightage of up to 5 years was added to the qualifying service of an employee taking voluntary retirement. This provision was withdrawn on the recommendations of the Pay Commissions, and no such addition is admissible under current application of the rules.
Elimination: Options listing specific conditions rely on outdated clauses or represent incorrect interpretations of the current 2021 framework.

Q18. Regarding "Family Pension" eligibility for children, consider the following statements:
1. An unmarried son is eligible for family pension up to the age of 25 years or until he starts earning his livelihood, whichever is earlier.
2. A disabled child suffering from a physical or mental disability is eligible for family pension for life, provided the disability renders them incapable of earning a livelihood.
3. A divorced or widowed daughter is eligible for family pension irrespective of her age, provided she meets the income dependency criteria.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
Concept: Family pension guidelines guarantee payment to sons until age 25 or employment. For daughters, it continues until they marry or earn a livelihood. Divorced/widowed daughters are eligible irrespective of age if they meet dependency criteria. Disabled children incapable of earning are eligible for life.
Elimination: All three statements flawlessly represent the legal structure of family pension disbursement.

Q19. Under the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, if an employee resigns from Government service to take up an appointment in a Central Public Sector Enterprise (CPSE) upon his own volition (without proper permission or forwarding of application), what is the impact on his past service?
A. The past service counts for proportionate pension.
B. The employee is entitled to immediate pro-rata pension.
C. Resignation entails forfeiture of past service.
D. The service is transferred to the CPSE's EPF account seamlessly.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Resignation from a service or a post, unless it is a "technical resignation" (where the application was forwarded through proper channels to take up another government/CPSE post), entails forfeiture of all past service. Without proper forwarding, it is treated as a standard resignation, destroying pension eligibility.
Elimination: Technical resignation protects past service, but the prompt specified it was done without proper permission/forwarding. Therefore, forfeiture applies.

Q20. What is the impact of a "Dies-non" day on the pensionary benefits of a Government servant?
A. It reduces the basic pay permanently.
B. It constitutes a break in service, forfeiting all previous service.
C. It does not constitute a break in service, but the period is excluded from qualifying service.
D. It has absolutely no impact on pension calculation.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: When a period of unauthorized absence is treated as "Dies-non" (a day not counting for anything), it means the employee earns no pay or leave for that period, and it is excluded from their total qualifying service. However, it explicitly does NOT constitute a break in service, meaning service before and after the dies-non period is combined.
Elimination: Option B is wrong as it doesn't break the service chain. Option D is wrong because it reduces the total tally of qualifying service, slightly impacting the final fractional calculation.

Q21. Regarding the commutation of pension, a retiring Central Government employee can commute up to what percentage of their basic pension?
A. 30%
B. 33%
C. 40%
D. 50%
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: Under the relevant commutation rules heavily tied to the pension framework, a pensioner may opt to commute for a lump sum payment any fraction up to a maximum of 40% of their basic pension.
Elimination: 33% was a historical number (1/3rd). The 6th Pay Commission bumped this up to 40%, which has remained consistent in the 2021 updates.

Q23. If two widows survive a deceased Government servant (where personal law permits polygamy), how will the family pension be disbursed?
A. The entire family pension is given to the first wife.
B. The family pension is shared equally among the widows.
C. The first wife receives 60%, and the second wife receives 40%.
D. The government halts family pension until a Civil Court passes a succession decree.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: In cases where the personal law of the deceased permits polygamy, and two or more legally wedded wives survive the official, the family pension is explicitly meant to be shared equally (in equal proportions) among the surviving widows.
Elimination: The rules prioritize equitable division among legally recognized widows over arbitrary percentage splits or barring them completely.

Q24. Can a Government servant withdraw their notice of Voluntary Retirement after it has been accepted by the competent authority?
A. Yes, at any time before the actual date of retirement, without any condition.
B. No, a notice once accepted cannot be withdrawn under any circumstances.
C. Yes, provided the request for withdrawal is made before the intended date of retirement and the competent authority explicitly approves the withdrawal.
D. Yes, but only if they agree to forfeit their accrued seniority.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: A Government servant may withdraw the notice of voluntary retirement, but they have no absolute right to do so. The request must be submitted before the effective date of retirement, and it requires the specific approval/sanction of the competent authority, who evaluates if the administrative situation permits the withdrawal.
Elimination: The approval is highly conditional, making Option A false. Option B is false because withdrawal is administratively possible with consent.

Q25. Which of the following allowances is/are taken into account when calculating "Emoluments" for Retirement Gratuity?
1. Basic Pay
2. Dearness Allowance (DA)
3. House Rent Allowance (HRA)
4. Non-Practicing Allowance (NPA) to Medical Officers
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1, 2, and 4 only
C. 1 and 4 only
D. 1, 2, 3, and 4
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: For the calculation of All forms of Gratuity (Retirement and Death), "Emoluments" are defined as Basic Pay (including NPA for doctors) PLUS Dearness Allowance admissible on the date of retirement/death.
Elimination: Emoluments for monthly pension calculate ONLY Basic Pay + NPA. However, for Gratuity, DA is uniquely included. HRA is never included in either calculation. Therefore, 1, 2, and 4 are correct.
