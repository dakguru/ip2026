# CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021 - High-Quality MCQs (Part 3: Q51 to Q75)

Q51. Consider the following regarding the "Pension Process and Verification":
1. The Head of Office must start the preparation of pension papers one year before the date of superannuation of the Government servant.
2. The verification of qualifying service must be formally communicated to the employee on completing 18 years of service.
3. The Government servant bears the ultimate responsibility of calculating their exact admissible pension amount.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
Concept: The rules mandate that the Head of Office initiates the pension process exactly one year before the date of retirement to prevent delays. Similarly, upon completing 18 years of service (or 5 years before retirement), the office must verify the service and communicate the qualifying service tally to the employee to rectify discrepancies.
Elimination: The responsibility of calculating and authorizing the exact pension lies with the Accounts Officer based on the Head of Office's assessment, never the employee themselves.

Q52. A Government servant who takes "Absence Without Leave" (AWOL) has this period regularized retrospectively as Extraordinary Leave (EOL) without Medical Certificate. Does this period count as qualifying service?
A. Yes, all leave automatically counts as service.
B. No, EOL without a medical certificate does not count unless specifically ordered otherwise by the appointing authority.
C. Yes, AWOL is treated as standard Earned Leave after retirement.
D. No, it constitutes a technical break in service, forfeiting all previous service.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: By rule, EOL granted without a medical certificate is excluded from qualifying service unless the competent authority explicitly regularizes it on specific grounds (e.g., higher studies or civil commotion).
Elimination: It does not break the continuity of service (forfeiting past service), but the specific period is deducted.

Q53. Under the revised family pension rules, what is the sequence of defining the "Eligible Children" for Family Pension?
A. All children share the pension equally simultaneously.
B. The eldest eligible child is paid 100% of the pension, and only passes to the next upon losing eligibility (attaining 25 years/marriage).
C. The pension is split equally between the eldest son and eldest daughter.
D. Only sons are eligible; daughters receive only a lump sum gratuity.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: In the absence of a surviving spouse, the family pension is paid to the eligible children in the order of their birth. The eldest eligible child receives the full family pension. When they become ineligible (e.g., turning 25, marrying, or getting employed), it automatically passes to the next eligible younger child.
Elimination: Shared/split formats apply only to widows, not children (unless dealing with children from different wives, which has distinct sub-rules).

Q54. Which of the following defines the "Residual Pension" after Commutation?
A. The pension amount after deducting income tax at source.
B. The 60% of basic pension that remains payable monthly after commuting the maximum admissible 40%.
C. The remaining amount paid to the family after the pensioner's death.
D. The amount paid during suspension.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: When a pensioner commutes a fraction of their pension (up to 40%), the remaining amount payable to them every month (which is Basic Pension minus the Commuted Portion) is termed the Residual Pension.
Elimination: Dearness Relief, however, is calculated on the *original* full Basic Pension, not the residual pension.

Q55. In cases where a disciplinary authority issues an order of "Compulsory Retirement" as a penalty, what amount of pension can be granted?
A. The official forfeits all pension and gratuity.
B. The official gets the standard superannuation pension without deductions.
C. A compulsory retirement pension at a rate not less than two-thirds of the compensation pension or gratuity that would be admissible on the date of compulsory retirement.
D. Only half of the normal family pension scale.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: The competent authority has the discretion to set the quantum of Compulsory Retirement Pension, but it cannot be less than two-thirds (66.6%) of the normal pension/gratuity admissibility, preventing absolute destitution while still enforcing the penalty.
Elimination: Compulsory retirement differs from dismissal (which causes total forfeiture). It ensures a baseline pension.

Q56. Regarding the calculation of "Average Emoluments", what happens if an employee was on Extraordinary Leave (EOL) for the last three months of their mandatory ten-month assessment period?
A. The 10-month period is calculated excluding the three months of EOL, drawing back an additional three months of earlier service.
B. The period of EOL is treated as zero pay, lowering their average significantly.
C. The EOL period is assumed to be fully paid at the last drawn rate.
D. The employee is entirely denied their pension until they resume duty.
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
Concept: For determining average emoluments over the last 10 months, periods of leave without pay (like EOL) or suspension not treated as duty are explicitly ignored. The 10-month block is shifted backwards by an equal duration to capture actual active months of pay.
Elimination: Including zeros would unfairly penalize the retirement calculation.

Q57. Is "Stagnation Increment" treated as part of the Emoluments for the purpose of Gratuity and Pension?
A. No, it is a separate allowance and completely excluded.
B. Yes, it is treated as pay for all purposes, including Pension and Gratuity.
C. It is included for Gratuity but not for Pension.
D. It is only included if the official explicitly requests it in Form 7.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: A stagnation increment, granted when an employee reaches the maximum of their pay scale but continues to serve without promotion, is treated identically to basic pay and is fully included in the "Emoluments" for calculating all retirement benefits.
Elimination: Allowances like HRA/CCA are excluded, but anything designated as increment/pay is inherently incorporated.

Q58. Can an employee who has opted for the National Pension System (NPS) switch to the Old Pension Scheme governed by the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021?
A. Yes, any employee can transition randomly at any point in their career.
B. No, the schemes are mutually exclusive, except in specific historical anomaly cases (like appointments finalized before 01.01.2004 but joined later).
C. Yes, provided they transfer their entire NPS corpus to the General Provident Fund (GPF).
D. The CCS (Pension) Rules 2021 automatically converted all NPS subscribers back to the old scheme.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The application of the rules strictly bars those appointed on or after January 1, 2004, who are governed by NPS. The only exceptions are specific transitional anomalies resolved by the Department of Pension and Pensioners' Welfare (DoPPW), allowing a one-time option for those whose recruitment was finalized before 2004 but joined after.
Elimination: Generalized voluntary switching is not permitted under the 2021 statutory framework.

Q59. If a Government servant desires to alter their nomination for Death Gratuity, they can do so by submitting a cancellation in writing. Is the prior consent of the existing nominee required?
A. Yes, a legally binding no-objection certificate must be procured.
B. No, the Government servant can revise the nomination at any time without the nominee's consent.
C. Yes, but only if the nominee is the legal spouse.
D. No, unless the original nomination was witnessed by a gazetted officer.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: A nomination for death gratuity is a dynamic, revocable instrument. The employee retains the absolute right to cancel or revise their nomination at any point during their service by sending a formal notice to the Head of Office, and no consent from the current nominee is legally required.
Elimination: Requiring consent would defeat the purpose of protective nominations (e.g., removing an estranged spouse after divorce).

Q60. A retired official passed away leaving a blind daughter who is 30 years old. Under the CCS (Pension) Rules, is she eligible for Family Pension?
A. No, daughters become ineligible upon reaching 25 years.
B. Yes, visually impaired children who cannot earn a livelihood are eligible for family pension for life.
C. No, visual impairment does not qualify under "disability" for pension purposes.
D. Yes, but only for a maximum of 5 years.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The rules provide sweeping protections for dependent children suffering from severe physical or mental disabilities (including blindness) that render them incapable of earning a living. They are granted Family Pension for life, bypassing standard age restrictions.
Elimination: Age limits (25 years) apply strictly to able-bodied children.

Q61. What is the rule concerning the "Simultaneous payment of Family Pension" to twins?
A. The eldest twin receives the full amount.
B. The pension is divided equally between the twins until they become ineligible.
C. The twin who applies first receives the entire amount.
D. The pension is granted only to the male twin.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: When eligible children are twins, the family pension is explicitly meant to be paid to them in equal shares. When one twin ceases to be eligible (e.g., turns 25), their half share reverts to the other twin, who then draws the full pension.
Elimination: Option A is impossible since twins are legally considered equal in birth order for this specific purpose.

Q62. What does the term "Notional Pay" imply in the context of pension revisions?
A. It is the anticipated future pay the pensioner would have drawn if they hadn't retired.
B. It is the theoretical pay fixed in the revised pay matrix (e.g., during Pay Commissions) corresponding to the pay scale from which the employee explicitly retired.
C. It is the temporary pay allowed during suspension.
D. It is a fabricated pay to calculate provisional pension.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: During the implementation of Pay Commissions (like the 7th CPC), the pensions of past retirees are revised by "notionally fixing" their pay in the new pay matrix corresponding to the level/scale they were holding at the time of their retirement, ensuring parity with current employees.
Elimination: Notional pay is a revision mechanism, not an anticipatory or suspension-related formula.

Q63. If an unmarried Government servant nominates a friend for Death Gratuity, and subsequently gets married, what happens to the nomination?
A. The nomination remains valid until manually changed by the official.
B. The nomination automatically becomes invalid upon the official acquiring a "family".
C. The friend receives 50%, and the spouse receives 50%.
D. The nomination requires re-validation by the Head of Office.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: A fundamental rule of nomination states that if an employee has no family, they can nominate any person (or entity). However, if the employee subsequently acquires a family (e.g., getting married), the previous nomination made in favor of a non-family member instantly and automatically becomes invalid.
Elimination: The law protects the legal heirs/family members over prior non-family friends unquestioningly.

Q64. In the calculation of minimum pension, what is the current threshold set by the Government?
A. ₹3,500 per month
B. ₹9,000 per month
C. ₹18,000 per month
D. ₹5,000 per month
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: As per the implementation of the 7th Central Pay Commission (mapped into the operational guidelines of the 2021 rules), the absolute minimum pension and family pension payable to any pensioner is fixed at ₹9,000 per month (Basic), excluding Dearness Relief.
Elimination: ₹3,500 was the old 6th CPC limit. ₹18,000 is the minimum starting salary for a serving employee, not a pensioner.

Q65. Is "Foreign Service" inside India (e.g., deputation to a State Government) treated differently from Foreign Service outside India regarding pension contributions?
A. Yes, outside India services do not count for pension at all.
B. No, as long as the prescribed pension contributions are paid to the Consolidated Fund of India, both count as qualifying service.
C. Yes, inside India deputations do not require any contributions.
D. State Government deputations inherently forfeit central pension rights.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Whether the foreign service is to a State PSU, a domestic body, or an international organization, the underlying rule is identical: the period counts as qualifying service for central pension provided the continuous payment of pension and leave salary contributions is verified and remitted correctly.
Elimination: Location does not sever the overarching legal protection as long as the financial dues are settled.

Q66. Does a "Warning" or "Displeasure" issued to an employee count as a penalty that reduces their pension?
A. Yes, it causes a mandatory 5% cut.
B. No, warnings and letters of displeasure are not statutory penalties and do not impact pension eligibility or calculations.
C. Yes, it halts commutation of pension permanently.
D. Warnings prevent the grant of any death gratuity.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Under the CCS (CCA) Rules and mirrored in Pension Rules, adverse administrative remarks like "Warning", "Displeasure", or "Advisory" are not recognized as formal penalties (unlike Censure, Withholding of increments, or Dismissal). Therefore, they carry zero legal weight in withholding or altering pensionary benefits.
Elimination: Options implying arbitrary cuts for non-statutory penalties violate basic service law.

Q67. Consider the following regarding the "Commutation of Pension Flow":
1. Once a pensioner applies for commutation, they can withdraw the application any time before receiving the commuted value.
2. The commuted value of pension becomes absolute on the date the Medical Authority signs the medical certificate (if medical is required).
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: A pensioner has the discretionary right to withdraw their commutation application before the commuted value is actually credited. However, legally, the fractional reduction of pension is "absolute" (fixed) on the exact date they sign the medical certificate (or on the date of retirement if applied simultaneously without a medical). Both statements correctly reflect the phased legalities.

Q68. Under the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, if an official is absent from duty for a continuous period of more than 5 years (other than on foreign service or valid leave), they are deemed to have resigned. What is the impact on their pension?
A. They receive a pro-rata pension for the years served before the absence.
B. They receive a compassionate allowance.
C. The deemed resignation entails forfeiture of all past service, resulting in no pension.
D. The 5 years of absence are treated as Dies-non, but previous service is protected.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
Concept: The rules are strict: unauthorized continuous absence exceeding five years triggers a "deemed resignation" from Government service. As per the rules on resignation, any form of non-technical resignation causes immediate forfeiture of all past qualifying service, effectively zeroing out any pension capability.
Elimination: It is not a penalty like compulsory retirement (where a fraction is paid) nor a mere dies-non. It severs the employment entirely.

Q69. A deceased Government servant's son is legally adopted by another family after the employee's death. Does this adopted child lose their right to the biological parent's Family Pension?
A. Yes, adoption severs all ties automatically.
B. No, the child remains eligible for the family pension of the deceased biological parent as the right vested upon the employee's death.
C. Yes, but only if the adoptive parents are also Government servants.
D. The pension is placed in a trust until the child turns 18, then cancelled.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The rights to Family Pension vest in the eligible family members at the time of the employee's death. According to the established clarifications, a child who is given in adoption by the surviving widow/widower after the death of the Government servant continues to be eligible for family pension.
Elimination: The adoption occurs post-death; thus, the vested financial protection of the deceased's service is not disrupted.

Q70. Which formula correctly represents the calculation of "Family Pension" for the dependent family when an official dies after 10 years of service?
A. 30% of the last pay drawn from day one.
B. 50% of the last pay drawn for the first 10 years (Enhanced Rate), and 30% of the last pay drawn thereafter (Normal Rate).
C. 50% of the average emoluments, payable for life.
D. A predefined lump sum of ₹10 Lakhs, followed by 20% of basic pay.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The statutory framework for in-service deaths provides robust immediate relief: the family receives an "Enhanced Family Pension" equal to 50% of the deceased's last pay drawn for the first 10 years. After exactly 10 years, it shifts to the "Normal Family Pension" rate, which is 30% of the last pay drawn.
Elimination: 30% from day one completely ignores the enhanced period. 50% for life is incorrect as the enhancement is capped at 10 years.

Q71. Is an employee who is dismissed from service due to participation in a "Strike" eligible for an Invalid Pension?
A. Yes, if they can prove they sustained an injury during the strike.
B. No, dismissal for participating in a strike results in forfeiture of all past service, nullifying eligibility for Invalid Pension.
C. Yes, the Constitution explicitly protects pension rights during unionized strikes.
D. Yes, provided they have over 20 years of service.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Invalid Pension is strictly a medical retirement protocol. If an employee is dismissed for misconduct (including illegal strikes), their service is forfeited. Invalid pension cannot apply because they are legally severed from the roll via a disciplinary penalty, not a medical board recommendation.
Elimination: Forfeiture is absolute.

Q72. If a pensioner is incapacitated and cannot physically sign the claim forms or life certificates, what procedure is validated by the rules?
A. Only the spouse can sign on their behalf.
B. A thumb impression, attested by a Gazetted Officer, a bank manager, or a registered medical practitioner, is accepted as a valid signature.
C. The pension must be permanently halted and transferred to the family pension account.
D. The pensioner must be brought physically to the Head of Office on a stretcher.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: Pension rules are designed with deep welfare considerations for the elderly and infirm. A thumb impression (or toe impression) that is appropriately witnessed/attested by designated authorities (like Bank Branch Managers, Magistrates, or Gazetted Officers) holds identical legal weight to a signature for all pension matters, including Life Certificates.
Elimination: Halting the pension or demanding physical attendance ignores standard humanitarian administrative protocols explicitly baked into the system.

Q73. During the calculation of "Retirement Gratuity", a fraction of a rupee is resolved by:
A. Rounding it off to the nearest lower rupee.
B. Rounding it off to the next higher rupee.
C. Keeping it to two decimal places and depositing it.
D. Deducting the fraction from the final PPO.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: In all terminal benefits calculations (Pension, Gratuity, Commutation), the Government universally rounds off fractions of a rupee to the next higher rupee to benefit the employee.
Elimination: Lowering it or ignoring it contradicts the established Ministry of Finance rounding protocols for final pensionary disimbursements.

Q74. A pensioner passes away on the 15th of November. Up to what exact date is the standard Basic Pension payable to their account before the Family Pension kicks in?
A. Up to the 14th of November.
B. Up to the 15th of November (the date of death).
C. The entire month of November is paid as Basic Pension.
D. The pension stops on the 1st of November.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: By rule, the pension of a deceased pensioner is payable up to the actual date of death (inclusive). From the date following the date of death (i.e., 16th November), the Family Pension becomes operational.
Elimination: Paying the whole month is incorrect. Stopping on the 1st deprives the estate of earned pension for the 15 days the pensioner lived.

Q75. Which section/rule strictly mandates the "Preparation of a checklist" by the Head of Office to track the timely progression of pension papers?
A. The Head of Office is not required to maintain a checklist.
B. Rule 54 requires the establishment of an automated registry to monitor delays and affix responsibility on dealing hands.
C. Only the Accounts Officer maintains the checklist.
D. Checklists are optional measures dependent on the department's manual.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Concept: The modernized CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021 significantly tightened the timelines. The rules (often anchored around Rule 54 and the accompanying schedule) mandate a strict timeline checklist and a monitoring system to ensure cases are dispatched months in advance, specifically stating that accountability and responsibility must be fixed on the dealing staff for any unwarranted delays in issuing the PPO.
Elimination: It is not optional nor limited to the Accounts Officer. It is a mandatory statutory mechanism for the Head of Office to enforce.
