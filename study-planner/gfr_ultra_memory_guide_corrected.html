GFR 2017 — Chapter 2
General System of Financial Management (Rules 18 to 41)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
18 Remission of Revenue No revenue claim can be remitted or abandoned without the sanction of the competent authority.
19 Annual Revenue Remission Statement Departments submit an annual statement to the Audit & Accounts Officer on 1st of June. Individual remissions below ₹1,000 are excluded.
20 Power to make rules for Remission Departments may frame departmental rules for remission of revenue, subject to general rules issued by the Government.
21 Standards of financial propriety Every officer must ensure expenditure is not prima-facie more than the occasion demands — guided by the highest standards of propriety.
22 Expenditure from Public Funds No authority may incur any expenditure or enter into any liability involving expenditure from public funds unless sanctioned by a competent authority.
23 Delegations of Financial powers Financial powers not delegated to subordinate authorities vest exclusively in the Finance Ministry.
24 Consultation with Financial Advisers Before incurring any expenditure, Departments must consult their Financial Adviser.
25 Provision of funds for sanction No expenditure shall be incurred from public revenues without provision of funds — must ensure budget exists before spending.
26 Responsibility of Controlling Officer The Controlling Officer is personally responsible for ensuring expenditure does not exceed the budget allocation and guarding against waste.
27 Date of effect of sanction Rules, sanctions or orders come into force from the date of issue unless any other date is specified therein.
28 Required previous consent of the Finance Ministry Prior Finance Ministry consent mandatory for granting land, assigning revenue, concession/lease of mineral/forest rights, or relinquishment of revenue.
29 Procedure for communication of sanctions All financial sanctions go to Audit — except certain categories like advances, establishment (appointment/promotion), GPF, and routine contingencies.
30 Lapse of sanctions A sanction automatically lapses if no payment is made during a period of 12 months from the date of issue.
31 Sanction of an allowance A sanction respect of an allowance sanctioned for a post, but not drawn, shall not lapse within 12 months.
32 Remission of disallowances & writing off overpayment Disallowances by Audit on government servant payments can be remitted, and overpayments written off according to Delegation of Financial Power rules.
33 Report of Losses Any loss of public money/property must be reported to the next higher authority immediately. Petty losses up to ₹10,000 need not be reported.
34 Loss of Government Property due to fire, theft, fraud Losses above ₹50,000 due to suspected fire, theft, fraud must be invariably reported to the Police for investigation as early as possible.
35 Loss of immovable Property due to fire, flood etc. Loss of immovable property exceeding ₹50,000 caused by fire, flood, cyclone, earthquake must be reported at once.
36 Report to Audit and Accounts Officers After a full enquiry of the loss, a copy of the report must be forwarded to the Audit officer and Pay and Accounts Officer.
37 Responsibility of losses An officer shall be held personally responsible for any loss sustained by the Government through fraud or negligence on his part.
38 Prompt disposal of cases of loss Action at each stage of detection, reporting, write off, and remedial measures should be completed promptly.
39 Demand for information by Audit or Accounts Officer A subordinate authority is fully responsible to furnish any information required by Audit or PAO for official account or report preparation.
40 Submission of records and information A subordinate authority shall not withhold any information, books or other documents required by the Audit Officer.
41 Handling of Secret/Top Secret Files Secret/Top Secret files must be sent personally to the Head of the Audit Office specifying this fact.

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part A
Procurement of Goods (Rules 143 to 176)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
143 Definition of Goods Includes all tangible/intangible products, software, technology transfer, licenses. Excludes books, publications, periodicals for a library.
144 Fundamental principles of public buying All procurements must bring efficiency, economy, and transparency. Specs must be Objective, Functional, Generic, Measurable (OFGM).
145 Authorities competent to purchase goods An authority competent to incur expenditure may sanction purchase of goods in accordance with the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules.
146 Procurement of goods required on mobilization Special procedures apply for procurement during urgent or mobilization situations.
147 Powers for procurement of goods Common use Goods and Services available on GeM are required to be procured mandatorily as per Rule 149.
148 Deleted This rule has been deleted in GFR 2017.
149 Government e-Market place (GeM) Mandatory portal. Direct buy up to ₹50,000. L1 from 3+ sellers up to ₹10,00,000. Bidding/Reverse Auction above ₹10,00,000. (Limits revised 10.07.2024)
150 Registration of Suppliers For goods not on GeM, Ministry may register suppliers for 1 to 3 years after verifying credentials.
151 Debarment from bidding Debarred up to 3 years for corruption/BNS offences. Debarred up to 2 years for breach of code of integrity.
152 Enlistment of Indian Agents Compulsory for Indian agents who desire to quote directly on behalf of their foreign principals to get themselves enlisted.
153 Reserved Items and other Purchase/Price Preference Policy Mandatory procurement of at least 20% from handloom origin/KVIC and purchase preference policies for MSMEs.
154 Purchase of goods without quotation Direct purchase up to ₹50,000 on each occasion without inviting quotations or bids. (Revised from ₹25k on 10.07.2024)
155 Purchase of goods by Purchase Committee For goods costing above ₹50,000 and up to ₹5,00,000. Made on recommendations of a Local Purchase Committee of 3 members. (Revised limits on 10.07.2024)
156 Deleted This rule has been deleted.
157 Prohibition of piecemeal purchases A demand for goods should not be divided into small quantities to avoid procurement through L1 buying/bidding or sanction limits.
158 Purchase of goods by obtaining bids Standard methods include: Advertised Tender, Limited Tender, Two-Stage, Single Tender, and Electronic Reverse Auctions.
159 E-Publishing Mandatory for all Ministries/Departments to publish tender enquiries, corrigenda, and bid awards on the Central Public Procurement Portal (CPPP).
160 E-Procurement Mandatory to receive all bids through e-procurement portals in respect of all procurements.
161 Advertised Tender Enquiry For procurement of goods of estimated value ₹50 lakhs and above. Min time 3 weeks (4 weeks for global). Global Tender Enquiry (GTE) not allowed up to ₹200 crores. (Revised 10.07.2024)
162 Limited Tender Enquiry May be adopted when estimated value is up to ₹50 lakhs. Number of supplier firms should be more than three. (Revised 10.07.2024)
163 Two bid system For complex/technical machinery. Simultaneous receipt of separate technical and financial bids. Technical opened first.
164 Two-Stage Bidding Stage 1: technical bids without price. Stage 2: final bids with prices from those whose technical bids were acceptable.
165 Late Bids Bids received after the specified date and time for receipt of bids should not be considered.
166 Single Tender Enquiry Allowed if only a particular firm manufactures it, in emergencies, or for standardization. Requires a Proprietary Article Certificate (PAC).
167 Electronic Reverse Auctions An online real-time purchasing technique involving presentation by bidders of successively more favorable bids.
168 Contents of Bidding Document Must contain instructions, conditions, schedule of requirements, specifications, and price schedule.
169 Maintenance Contract Needed for sophisticated/costly equipment. Should commence only after the warranty period or extended warranty.
170 Bid security (Earnest Money) Normally ranges between 2% to 5%. Valid for a period of 45 days beyond the final bid validity period.
171 Performance Security Amount of 3% to 5%. Should remain valid for 60 days beyond the date of completion of all contractual obligations. (Revised from 3-10% to 3-5% w.e.f 01.01.2024)
172 Advance payment to Supplier Should not exceed 30% (private firms) or 40% (State/Central agency). For maintenance contracts, not exceeding 6 months.
173 Transparency, competition, fairness All purchases must be transparent and fair. Quotations with NIL charges/consideration will be treated as unresponsive.
174 Efficiency, Economy and Accountability Public procurement procedure should ensure efficiency, economy and accountability in the system.
175 Code of Integrity Prohibition of making offer, acceptance of bribe or reward. Disciplinary action or debarment if contravened.
176 Buy-Back Offer Department may trade the existing old item while purchasing the new one with approval of competent authority.

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part B
Consulting Services (Rules 177 to 196)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
177 Consulting Service Means any subject matter of procurement other than goods or works, including professional, intellectual, training and advisory services.
178 Hiring of Consultants Ministries may hire external professionals or consultancy firms for a specific job which is well defined in content and time frame.
183 Identification of likely sources Up to ₹50 lakhs: formal/informal enquiries. Above ₹50 lakhs: Expression of Interest (EoI) on CPPP. (Revised from ₹25L to ₹50L on 10.07.2024)
184 Short listing of consultants The number of short-listed consultants should not be less than three.
185 Preparation of Terms of Reference (TOR) TOR must include precise statement of objectives, outline of tasks, schedule for completion, and inputs provided by Ministry.
186 Preparation and Issue of Request for Proposal (RFP) RFP is the document to be used for obtaining offers from the shortlisted consultants.
187 Receipt and opening of proposals Asked for in Two bid system. Technical proposals should be opened first.
192 Quality and Cost Based Selection (QCBS) Weights applied to Quality and Cost (e.g. 70:30, 80:20). Non-financial parameters weightage cannot exceed 80%.
193 Least Cost System (LCS) Responsive technically qualified proposal with the lowest evaluated cost shall be selected (no weightage for technical score beyond qualification).
194 Single Source Selection/Consultancy by nomination Direct negotiation/nomination under exceptional circumstances (emergencies, continuation of work, only one consultant has expertise).

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part C
Non-Consulting Services (Rules 197 to 205)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
197 Non-Consulting Service Services with physical, measurable deliverables/outcomes (e.g. maintenance, hiring of vehicles, security, janitor, photocopying).
201 Invitation of Bids Up to ₹50 lakhs: Limited tender enquiry from identified contractors. Above ₹50 lakhs: Advertisement on CPPP. (Revised from ₹10L to ₹50L on 10.07.2024)
204 Procurement of Non-consulting services by nomination Allowed in exceptional situations with detailed justification and consultation with Financial Adviser.
