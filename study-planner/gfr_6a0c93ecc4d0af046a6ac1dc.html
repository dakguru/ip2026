GFR 2017 — Chapter 2
General System of Financial Management (Rules 18 to 41)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
18 Remission of Revenue No revenue claim can be remitted or abandoned without the sanction of the competent authority.
19 Annual Revenue Remission Statement Departments submit an annual statement to the Audit & Accounts Officer on 1st of June. Individual remissions below ₹1,000 are excluded.
20 Power to make rules for Remission Departments may frame departmental rules for remission of revenue, subject to general rules issued by the Government.
21 Standards of Financial Propriety Every officer must ensure expenditure is not prima-facie more than the occasion demands — guided by the highest standards of propriety.
22 Expenditure from Public Funds No authority may incur any expenditure or enter into any liability involving expenditure from public funds without the sanction of the competent authority.
23 Delegation of Financial Powers Financial powers not delegated to subordinates vest exclusively in the Finance Ministry.
24 Consultation with Financial Adviser Before incurring any expenditure, Departments must consult their Financial Adviser.
25 Provision of Funds No expenditure shall be incurred from public revenues without provision of funds — must ensure budget exists before spending.
26 Responsibility of Controlling Officer The Controlling Officer is personally responsible for ensuring funds under his control are spent for the purposes they were granted and no excess occurs.
27 Control of Expenditure against Budget Ministries must maintain a monthly expenditure register and continuously monitor actuals vs. budget to prevent excess or surrender.
28 Previous Consent of Finance Ministry Prior Finance Ministry consent mandatory for granting land, assigning revenue, concession/lease of mineral/forest rights, water/power rights, or any relinquishment of revenue.
29 Communication of Sanctions to Audit All sanctions go to Audit — except 7 categories: Advances, Establishment (Appointment/Promotion/Transfer), Posts (Creation/Continuation/Abolition), Charge handover, GPF withdrawals, Contingency, and Routine sanctions.
30 Lapse of Sanctions Every sanction to expenditure automatically lapses if not acted upon within 12 months from the date of issue, unless otherwise specified.
31 Date of Effect of Sanction A sanction takes effect from the date of issue, unless a specific date is mentioned in the sanction order itself.
32 Remission of Disallowances & Write-off of Overpayments Disallowances by Audit on government servant payments can be remitted by competent authority. Overpayments may be written off if recovery is impractical.
33 Losses and Defalcations Any loss of public money/property must be reported to the next higher authority immediately. Petty losses up to ₹10,000 exempt. Losses exceeding ₹50,000 (fire/theft/fraud) → Police investigation mandatory.
34 Detailed Rules on Losses Every loss must be subjected to investigation both departmentally and through Police (where criminal activity is suspected).
35 Losses — Detailed Procedure Losses due to fire, theft, fraud or negligence must be reported to next higher authority with a preliminary report within 24 hours.
36 Report of Loss to Audit/Accounts Officer All losses exceeding ₹10,000 must be reported to the Principal Accounts Officer and the Audit Officer without delay.
37 Responsibility for Losses The Head of Office is personally responsible for any loss occurring due to fraud, negligence or any laxity in internal controls.
38 Prompt Disposal of Loss Cases All cases of losses must be investigated and finalized promptly — delays in write-off or recovery proceedings are not permitted.
39 Demand for Information by Audit Every officer must comply with any demand for records, information or explanations made by the Comptroller & Auditor General or Audit Officer.
40 Submission of Records to Audit All records, documents, books, accounts must be made available to Audit when required — failure to produce is a serious irregularity.
41 Handling of Secret/Top Secret Files by Audit Secret/Top Secret files must be sent personally to the Head of the Audit Office. They are NOT exempt from audit — only the delivery method is different.

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part A
Procurement of Goods (Rules 143 to 176)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
143 Definition of Goods Goods = all tangible/intangible products including software, IP, services, transportation, installation, training. Excludes library books/periodicals.
144 Fundamental Principles of Procurement Description must be Objective, Functional, Generic, Measurable (O-F-G-M). Must NOT indicate a brand/trademark name.
145 Procurement for Defence/Security Procurement for defence mobilisation/security operations may be done under special powers delegated by the Government.
146 Powers of Procurement Delegated financial powers of Ministries/Departments for procurement of goods — head of department may further delegate subject to limits.
147 Purchase without Quotation Purchases up to ₹50,000 per occasion (for items not on GeM) can be made without any formal quotation.
148 Local Purchase Committee (LPC) For purchases ₹50,001 to ₹5,00,000 — a 3-member committee is constituted to handle local purchases.
149 Limited Tender Enquiry (LTE) For procurement up to ₹50 Lakhs. Tender sent directly to more than 3 eligible suppliers. No open advertisement needed.
150 Advertised Tender Enquiry (ATE) For procurement ₹50 Lakhs and above. Must be published openly. Minimum 3 weeks (4 weeks if foreign bidders) for submission.
151 Debarment from Bidding Corruption/BNS guilty → debarred up to 3 years. Code of Integrity breach → debarred up to 2 years.
152 Registration of Suppliers Suppliers may be registered by the Purchasing Department. Registration does not guarantee any order but facilitates participation in tenders.
153 Purchase Preference Policy Purchase preference to Central Public Sector Enterprises, Khadi/Village Industries, and MSEs as prescribed by Government orders.
154 Rate Contract (RC) & DGS&D Procurement of common-use goods through DGS&D Rate Contract or GeM — all Ministries must procure at the RC rate or below.
155 Two-Bid System For complex/technical goods — Technical & Financial bids submitted together in separate sealed envelopes. Technical opened first; Financial opened only for qualified bidders.
156 Single Tender Enquiry (STE) Buy from one source only if: (1) Sole manufacturer, (2) Emergency, or (3) Standardization requirement.
157 Contents of Bid/Tender Bid documents must contain complete specifications, evaluation criteria, terms, EMD requirements, delivery schedule, and penalty clauses.
158 Late Bids Bids received after the deadline shall NOT be considered — late bids must be returned unopened. No exceptions.
159 Mandatory E-Publishing (CPPP) All tender enquiries, corrigenda, and bid awards must be published on CPPP (eprocure.gov.in) — mandatory for all Ministries, departments, and autonomous bodies.
160 Mandatory E-Procurement All bids must be received electronically via NIC's portal or any other approved service provider. No offline/manual bid reception permitted.
161 Global Tender Enquiry (GTE) — Restriction No GTE permitted for procurements up to ₹200 Crores. GTE only allowed beyond ₹200 Cr with special approval.
162 Contents of Bidding Document The complete set of bidding documents should contain eligibility criteria, technical specs, evaluation methodology, contract terms, EMD/Performance Security details.
163 Maintenance Contract AMC/CMC contracts for equipment may be entered into only with the OEM or authorised service provider and on competitive basis.
164 Two-Stage Bidding For evolving requirements — Stage 1: technical bids only (no price). Stage 2: shortlisted bidders submit final bids with prices based on revised specs.
165 Receipt & Opening of Bids Bids must be opened publicly at the specified time/place in the presence of bidders or their authorised representatives.
166 Proprietary Article Certificate When procuring a proprietary article (one made by a single manufacturer), the competent authority must certify that the item is uniquely available from that source only.
167 Evaluation of Bids Bids must be evaluated based on pre-disclosed criteria only — no new criteria can be introduced post-tender. L1 (Lowest evaluated cost) principle applies.
168 Terms of Delivery Terms of delivery (FOR destination/ex-works) must be clearly stated in the tender. Default: FOR destination with transit insurance by supplier.
169 Negotiation with L1 Price negotiation is generally banned — only permitted in Single Tender (proprietary) or with prior approval of competent authority.
170 Award of Contract Contract awarded to the lowest evaluated responsive bidder (L1) who meets all qualification criteria. Award within bid validity period.
171 Integrity Pact For procurements above ₹5 Crore (as revised), an Integrity Pact must be signed between the buyer and bidders with an Independent External Monitor.
172 Buy-back Offer Old items may be offered for buy-back when procuring new similar goods — helps offset cost. Must be on "as-is-where-is" basis.
173 Energy Efficient Appliances (BEE) All electrical appliances procured must carry the notified threshold or higher BEE Star Rating. Lower-rated appliances cannot be procured.
174 Insurance of Goods All goods procured must be insured during transit, unless delivered FOR destination where supplier bears transit risk.
175 Bid Security (Earnest Money Deposit) 2% to 5% of estimated value. Valid for 45 days beyond bid validity. Refunded to unsuccessful bidders by the 30th day after contract award.
176 Performance Security 3% to 5% of contract value (revised w.e.f 01.01.2024). Valid for 60 days beyond completion of all obligations including warranty.

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part B
Procurement of Consulting Services (Rules 177 to 196)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
177 Definition of Consulting Services Professional, intellectual, training & advisory services. Does NOT include direct engagement of retired Government servants.
178 Principles of Engaging Consultants Consultant selection must follow principles of economy, transparency, fairness, and quality — avoid conflict of interest.
179 Preparation of Terms of Reference TOR defines the scope, objectives, tasks, deliverables and timeline — must be precise and comprehensive before floating RFP.
180 Preparation of Cost Estimate A realistic cost estimate must be prepared before seeking proposals — helps determine the budget and evaluate financial bids.
181 Short-listing and Expression of Interest For complex assignments, a two-stage process: first invite EOI for short-listing, then send RFP to shortlisted firms only.
182 Preparation of Request for Proposal (RFP) The RFP must contain: Letter of Invitation, TOR, Evaluation Criteria, Contract Conditions — financial and technical proposals in separate sealed envelopes.
183 Quality & Cost Based Selection (QCBS) Final score = Technical (up to 80%) + Financial (min 20%). Default split: 70:30. Technical weightage must never exceed 80%.
184 Quality Based Selection (QBS) Selection based on technical quality alone — financial bid of the highest ranked technical proposal is then negotiated. Used for highly complex/specialised assignments.
185 Terms of Reference (TOR) TOR must contain 5 elements in order: Objective → Tasks → Timeline → Support → Deliverables (Mnemonic: O-T-T-S-D).
186 Fixed Budget Selection (FBS) Budget is disclosed upfront — consultant offering the best quality within the fixed budget is selected. Financial proposals exceeding budget are rejected.
187 Single Source Selection (SSS) Engagement of a consultant without competition — permitted only when: (a) a natural extension of prior work, (b) only one firm is qualified, or (c) emergency.
188 Selection of Individual Consultants Individual consultants may be hired for tasks where teams or institutional support are not required — selection based on qualifications and experience.
189 Procedure for Selection Published EOI → short-list → issue RFP → receive proposals → evaluate → negotiate → award. Minimum 3 shortlisted firms needed.
190 Contract Negotiations Negotiations only with the first-ranked proposer — cover TOR, methodology, staff, and financial terms. If unsuccessful, proceed to second-ranked.
191 Publication of Awards All consulting assignments awarded must be published on the CPPP portal for transparency.
192 Monitoring & Evaluation Ministry must monitor contract performance through periodic progress reports and evaluate deliverables against the TOR.
193 Least Cost System (LCS) Zero weightage for technical score after qualifying. The responsive, technically qualified proposal with the lowest evaluated cost wins.
194 Bid Security for Consulting EMD of 2% to 3% of estimated cost — exempt for MSEs and firms registered with appropriate authorities.
195 Performance Security for Consulting 5% to 10% of contract value — valid for 60 days beyond completion. Forfeited for non-performance or breach.
196 Payment Terms for Consulting Payment to consultants in milestones linked to deliverables. Advance payment (if any) not exceeding 10% of contract value against bank guarantee.

GFR 2017 — Chapter 6, Part C
Procurement of Non-Consulting Services (Rules 197 to 205)
Rule No. Heading / Subject One-Line Meaning (Exam-Ready)
197 Definition of Non-Consulting Services Services with physical, measurable deliverables — security, maintenance, cleaning, vehicle hire, facilities management.
198 Powers for Procurement of Non-Consulting Services Financial powers for procurement of non-consulting services follow the same delegation as for Goods under Rule 146.
199 Modes of Procurement Procurement modes: Advertised Tender, Limited Tender, Single Tender — same as Goods procurement but adapted for service contracts.
200 Bid Document for Non-Consulting Services Bid documents must specify scope of work, service levels (SLAs), penalty clauses, and evaluation criteria clearly.
201 Bid Security for Non-Consulting Services EMD of 2% to 5% of estimated value, same as Goods procurement rules.
202 Performance Security for Non-Consulting 3% to 5% of contract value — valid until 60 days beyond completion of all contractual obligations.
203 Evaluation of Bids Non-consulting service bids evaluated on L1 basis (lowest cost) after meeting technical qualification criteria.
204 Contract Management Appointing authority must designate a Contract Management Officer to monitor service delivery and ensure compliance with SLAs.
205 Residual Provisions For any matter not specifically covered under Rules 197-204, the corresponding provisions for Goods procurement (Rules 143-176) shall apply.
