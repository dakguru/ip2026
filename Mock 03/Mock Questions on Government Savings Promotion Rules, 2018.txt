## SECTION 2: ANSWER KEY & EXPLANATIONS

Q1: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rule 3(1)(e) of GSPR 2018 defines a "Joint Account" as an account opened in the names of more than one and up to three individuals.
Elimination: Options A, C, and D are incorrect because the statutory limit is specifically capped at three.



Q2: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 3(1)(f) defines a "Matured account" as one that has completed its term or extended term and is due for payment.
 Elimination: "Dormant" and "Closed" are different stages of account status not matching this specific definition; "Eligible" refers to the balance amount.



Q3: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rule 4(4) states that if a depositor ceases to be a citizen of India, the account is closed or deemed closed from the last day of the month preceding such cessation.
Elimination: Unlike NRIs who can continue until maturity , non-citizens face immediate deemed closure.



Q4: Correct Answer A
Legal Concept: Rule 4(2) specifies that a minor who has attained the age of ten years may open and operate an account.
Elimination: 18 is the age of majority, but the rules allow operational rights at 10.



Q5: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 4(1) and its second proviso require that both the guardian and the minor (or person of unsound mind) shall be resident citizens of India.
Elimination: Residency and citizenship are required for both parties, making A and B incomplete.



Q6: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 6(1)(b) states that failure to submit the PAN when the balance exceeds Rs. 50,000 results in the account ceasing to be operational until the document is submitted.
Elimination: Closure (A) or penalties (B) are not the immediate prescribed actions; suspension of operations is the rule.



Q7: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rule 8(2)(ii) defines 'Joint B' type as an account to be operated by any of the depositors or surviving depositors severally.
Elimination: Joint operation (A) applies to 'Joint A' type accounts.



Q8: Correct Answer D 
Legal Concept: Rule 3(1)(h) lists officially valid documents including Passport, Driving Licence, Voter ID, MGNREGA job card, and NPR letter.Elimination: Ration Card is not mentioned in the exhaustive list provided in the rules.



Q9: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 6(1)(a) requires the Aadhaar number to be submitted within 6 months from the date of opening the account for linking.
Elimination: 2 and 3 months refer to PAN submission events or address updates, not the initial Aadhaar linking window.



Q10: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rules 4(3) and 9(4) specify that if a depositor becomes an NRI, the account can continue till maturity, but no interest shall be payable after the date of its maturity.
Elimination: Post-maturity interest (A) is for resident accounts that haven't been closed, not for NRI-held accounts.



Q11: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 8(3) states that a Single Account cannot be converted to Joint and vice-versa, with the sole exception that a Joint Account can become Single if there is only one surviving holder.
Elimination: General conversion is explicitly barred, making A and B incorrect.



Q12: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 14(1) allows a depositor to nominate one or more individuals, but not exceeding four individuals.
Elimination: The rules provide a specific cap of four.



Q13: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 15(6)(ii) mandates that for amounts above Rs. 5 lakh, payment shall be made on submission of probate, letters of administration, or a succession certificate.
Elimination: The discretion of the authorized officer to pay a "rightful claimant" (B) is limited to amounts not exceeding Rs. 5 lakh.



Q14: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 7(2) explicitly states that the date of realization of the cheque shall be the date of deposit.
Elimination: Handover or cheque dates are irrelevant for interest and record purposes; realization is key.



Q15: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rule 16(2) allows pledging of a minor's account provided the guardian certifies in writing that the minor is alive and the transfer is for their benefit.
Elimination: It is permitted without a court order (D) if the specific certificate is provided.



Q16: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Schedule II, item (c) specifies the fee for "Transfer of account" is Rs. 100.
Elimination: Rs. 50 (A) is for duplicate passbooks or nomination changes.



Q17: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Rule 27 identifies the Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance, as the final authority for interpretation.
Elimination: While RBI (A) approves electronic modes, the Ministry of Finance holds interpretive power over the rules.



Q18: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 15(6) allows the authorized officer to pay a claimant for amounts up to Rs. 5 lakh if no legal documents (probate, etc.) are produced within six months from the death of the depositor.
Elimination: The six-month window is the statutory waiting period before the officer can exercise this discretion.



Q19: Correct Answer B
Legal Concept: Schedule II, item (a)(i) sets the fee for the issue of a duplicate passbook at Rs. 50. SB Order No. 05/2025 (dated April 3, 2025) deleted the Rs. 50 fee for cancellation or change of nomination. However, the fee for a duplicate passbook remains Rs. 50.
Elimination: Rs. 20 (A) is for a statement of account or deposit receipt.



Q20: Correct Answer C
Legal Concept: Rule 14(9) states that an NRI is eligible to be nominated, provided the payment is on a non-repatriation basis.
Elimination: Both statements align perfectly with the provision in Rule 14.


Q21. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: Rule 3(1) of the Post Office Savings Account Scheme, 2019, stipulates that an individual can open only one account as a single adult.
 Elimination: While joint accounts can be multiple, single ownership is restricted to one account per individual across the entire POSB network.

Q22. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: Per the POSA 2019 rules, the minimum deposit to open is Rs. 500, but subsequent deposits can be as low as Rs. 10.
 Elimination: Rs. 500 is the minimum balance; Rs. 100 is for other schemes; Rs. 50 is a common distractor.

Q23. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 10th day and the last day of the month.
 Elimination: Deposits made after the 10th do not earn interest for that month.

Q24. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: If the balance falls below Rs. 500 at the end of the financial year, a maintenance fee of Rs. 50 (inclusive of GST) is deducted.
 Elimination: GST is not charged extra; it is built into the Rs. 50 fee.

Q25. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: An account is treated as silent/inoperative if no transaction occurs for three continuous financial years.
 Elimination: Two years is the standard for commercial banks, but POSB uses a three-year window.

Q26. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: The National Savings Recurring Deposit Scheme, 2019, specifies a fixed maturity of 5 years (60 monthly deposits).
 Elimination: 3 years is the premature closure eligibility; 10 years was an old provision no longer standard for RD.

Q27. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: For accounts opened between the 16th and the last day of a month, subsequent deposits must be made by the last working day of the month.
 Elimination: The 15th is the deadline only for accounts opened between the 1st and 15th.

Q28. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: An account is discontinued after 4 defaults.
Elimination: Up to 4 defaults can be regularized within the next two months.

Q29. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: For a Rs. 100 denomination, a 6-month advance yields Rs. 10 rebate; a 12-month advance yields Rs. 40.
 Elimination: Rs. 50 and Rs. 100 are incorrect math; rebates are calculated on fixed slabs per Rs. 100.

Q30. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: RD accounts can be closed prematurely only after the completion of 3 years from the date of opening.
 Elimination: One/Two years are for TD/MIS; RD requires a longer commitment for PMC.

Q31. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: TD Scheme 2019 recognizes 1, 2, 3, and 5-year accounts. There is no 4-year TD category.
 Elimination: 1, 2, and 5 are the most common; 3 is also valid, but 4 does not exist.

Q32. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: The minimum deposit for any TD account category is Rs. 1,000.
 Elimination: Rs. 500 is for POSA; Rs. 5,000/Rs. 10,000 are distractors based on older limits.

Q33. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: For a 2-year TD, the extension must be applied for within 12 months from the date of maturity.
 Elimination: 6 months is for 1-year TD; 18 months is for 3/5-year TD.

Q34. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: If an account matures and is not extended or closed, interest is paid at the Post Office Savings Account (POSA) rate for the period it remains in the office.
 Elimination: Original TD rates never apply post-maturity without a formal extension.

Q35. Correct Answer: D
Rule/Logic: TD interest is compounded quarterly but payable annually. If not withdrawn, the interest does not earn further interest (no interest on interest).
 Elimination: All three statements represent the statutory method of TD interest management.

Q36. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: No TD account can be closed before the expiry of 6 months from the date of deposit.
 Elimination: 3 months is too early; 1 year is the threshold for receiving TD-linked interest rather than POSA interest.

Q37. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: If closed after 1 year, interest is paid at 2% less than the rate for the completed years (e.g., 3-year TD rate - 2%).
 Elimination: Option B (minus 1%) is the old rule; the 2019 rules standardized the 2% penalty.

Q38. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: The maximum limit for a single MIS account was increased to Rs. 9,00,000.
 Elimination: Rs. 4.5 Lakh is the old limit; Rs. 15 Lakh is the Joint limit.

Q39. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: For ceiling purposes, each joint holder in an MIS account is deemed to have an equal share.
 Elimination: Ownership is always split equally among the 2 or 3 joint holders.

Q40. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: If the corresponding day does not exist in a month, interest is paid on the last day of that month.
 Elimination: Interest is never "forfeited" or pushed to the 1st of the next month.

Q41. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: MIS accounts have a lock-in period of one year. No premature closure is allowed before 1 year.
 Elimination: 6 months applies to TD; 3 years is for RD.

Q42. Correct Answer: B
 Rule/Logic: PMC between 1 and 3 years incurs a 2% penalty on the principal.
 Elimination: 1% penalty applies only if the account is closed after 3 years.

Q43. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: On death, the account is closed. Interest is paid up to the preceding month of the refund.
 Elimination: Nominees cannot "continue" the MIS account; it must be settled.

Q44. Correct Answer: A
Rule/Logic: If a holder has defaults (up to 4), they can extend the account tenure to complete 60 deposits. This is linked to the fact that it is not yet "discontinued."
 Elimination: Both statements accurately reflect the relationship between defaults and account status.

Q45. Correct Answer: D
Rule/Logic: The depositor must personally collect the ATM card; third-party authorization is strictly prohibited by POSB ATM rules.
 Elimination: A, B, and C are valid rules (Joint-A cannot have ATMs as it requires both signatures for transactions).

Q46. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: The standard daily cash withdrawal limit at ATMs for Post Office cards is Rs. 25,000.
 Elimination: Rs. 10,000 is the per-transaction limit; Rs. 40,000 is common for private banks but not POSB.

Q47. Correct Answer: C
Rule/Logic: The annual maintenance fee for the Debit card is Rs. 125 + GST.
 Elimination: Rs. 50 and Rs. 100 are incorrect; Rs. 125 is the current statutory charge.

Q48. Correct Answer: A
Rule/Logic: For a Rs. 100 denomination, a 6-month advance deposit gets a Rs. 10 rebate.
 Elimination: Rebate is only 0 if the deposit is not for a minimum of 6 months.

Q49. Correct Answer: A
Rule/Logic: RD can be extended for 5 years. During extension, it can be closed at any time. However, the interest rate is the rate applicable at the time of the original opening (not "always" the original if the extension happens under new notified rates).
 Elimination: Statement 3 is slightly nuanced but 1 and 2 are the firm primary rules.

Q50. Correct Answer: B
Rule/Logic: The "Protected Savings Scheme" (Life cover for RD) requires that the account was active for 24 months without any defaults.
 Elimination: 3 years is the PMC rule; age 21 is not a requirement (minor accounts also qualify through guardians).
