Q1. Which of the following statements regarding the definition of 'cash' under Rule 100 of FHB Vol-I is/are correct?

1. It includes legal-tender coins, currency notes, and cheques payable on demand.
2. It includes Government Securities and Bank deposit receipts accepted as security deposit.
3. It excludes postage stamps, stationery, and reply coupons.
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 100, FHB Vol-I, 'cash' includes legal tender and demand cheques and explicitly EXCLUDES Government Securities, Bank deposit receipts, and stamps (treated as inventory). Statements 1 and 3 are correct; statement 2 is wrong because securities/deposit receipts are specifically outside the definition.

Q2. According to Rules 24 and 27 of FHB Vol-I, what is the time-bar limit for preferring a general arrear claim without requiring authority from the Circle Accounts Officer?
A. 6 months
B. 1 year
C. 3 years
D. 2 years
Answer: D
Explanation: Under Rules 24 and 27, FHB Vol-I, a general arrear claim not preferred within 2 years of becoming due requires the authority of the Circle Accounts Officer for investigation before payment.

Q3. A Government servant in receipt of pay dies in harness. The Head of Office proposes to make an anticipatory payment of the dues to the legal heirs WITHOUT obtaining the usual legal authority (succession certificate). The gross claim works out to Rs. 6,40,000. Under Rule 189 of FHB Vol-I, which course of action is correct?
A. The Head of Office may pay the full Rs. 6,40,000 on a simple receipt.
B. The Head of Office may pay up to Rs. 50,000 on a receipt; the balance requires a duly stamped Indemnity Bond in Form T.R. 14 with sureties.
C. The entire amount requires a stamped Indemnity Bond in Form T.R. 14, since the gross claim exceeds Rs. 50,000.
D. No payment is permissible without a succession certificate, regardless of amount.
Answer: C
Explanation: Under Rule 189, FHB Vol-I, anticipatory payment WITHOUT legal authority and without an Indemnity Bond is permitted only up to Rs. 50,000. Where the GROSS claim exceeds Rs. 50,000, the payment of the whole amount is regulated by obtaining a duly stamped Indemnity Bond in Form T.R. 14 with sureties — the limit is not a "first slice free" device but a threshold that pulls the entire claim into the indemnity-bond procedure. Hence C, not B.

Q9. A cheque is drawn by a cheque-drawing DDO and dated 14 January 2026. Under Rule 156 of FHB Vol-I, up to which date is it payable, and on what principle?

Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 156 (mirroring the Central Government Account (Receipts and Payments) Rules) provides that a Government cheque is payable within three months AFTER the month of issue. Rules, Rule on currency of cheques; FHB Vol-V Sec. V.)

Q10. A Government servant's monthly emoluments, after deducting exempt allowances, come to Rs. 42,000. A Civil Court attaches the salary under a decree OTHER than maintenance (Rule 175, based on CPC Section 60). Compute the maximum amount attachable in a month.
A. Rs. 13,667
B. Rs. 14,000
C. Rs. 41,000
D. Rs. 21,000
Answer: A
Explanation: Under CPC Section 60(1)(i)/Rule 175, for a non-maintenance decree the first Rs. 1,000 plus two-thirds of the remainder is EXEMPT; only one-third of the remainder is attachable. Remainder = 42,000 − 1,000 = 41,000; attachable = 41,000 ÷ 3 = Rs. 13,666.67 ≈ Rs. 13,667. The near-miss distractors: Rs. 14,000 wrongly takes one-third of the whole emoluments; Rs. 41,000 treats almost the entire salary as attachable; Rs. 21,000 wrongly attaches one-half of the remainder.(i).)
