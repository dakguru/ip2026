
Q1. Under the Postal Manual Vol II, any representation made by a Government servant that is not technically an appeal is classified as what?
A. Memorial
B. Petition
C. Revision
D. Review
Answer: B
Explanation: Any representation made by a Government servant that is not technically an appeal is classified as a petition. An appeal is a formal challenge to a specific order under a defined rule, while a petition is the residuary category.

Q2. Which of the following statements regarding the submission of petitions under the Postal Manual Vol II is/are correct?

1. Joint petitions are strictly inadmissible.
2. Identical petitions are permitted only if the circumstances represented apply personally to each individual submitting them.
3. A petition to a higher authority must carry a copy of the order challenged and be routed through the officer who passed it.
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: D
Explanation: Every employee must petition separately, making joint petitions inadmissible. Identical petitions require circumstances to apply personally to each individual. A petition to a higher authority must carry a copy of the order challenged and be routed through the officer who passed it.



Q3. Consider the following statements regarding the withholding of petitions addressed to the Director General:

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Explanation: A petition can be withheld if it relates to transfers ordered in the interest of service. It can also be withheld if it is premature. A list of all withheld petitions is forwarded quarterly to the Director General.



Q4. An Inspector of Posts issued a penalty order to a Gramin Dak Sevak (GDS) on 15 January. The GDS submitted a petition against this order on 20 August of the same year without citing any reasonable cause for the delay. According to the Postal Manual Vol II, what is the most likely consequence?
A. The petition must be entertained because the limitation period is one year.
B. The petition is liable to be rejected without investigation as it is time-barred.
C. The petition will be automatically forwarded to the President for a decision.
D. The limitation period restarts because the GDS filed a fresh representation.
Answer: B
Explanation: A petition is liable to be rejected without investigation if submitted more than 6 months after the date the petitioner was informed of the order. The 6 months are counted from the original order date, and a fresh representation does not restart the clock.

Petitions merely addressed to the "Chairman" or a "Member" of the PSB are not petitions to the President and are returned. A review petition under Rule 29-A is submitted to the President.



Q6. Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, what is the maximum number of charges of the same kind, committed within 12 months, that an accused may be tried for at a single trial?
A. Three charges
B. Four charges
C. Five charges
D. Six charges
Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 138 originally mirrored the old CrPC limiting trials to three charges. Under the successor joinder-of-charges provision of the BNSS, 2023, the ceiling has been raised to five offences of the same kind within a twelve-month window.

Q14. Under Rule 387 of the Postal Manual Vol II, which of the following scenarios is expressly stated as NOT constituting an offence?

Q95. The GST Amnesty Scheme introduced by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 (Section 128A) provides a conditional waiver of interest and penalty for non-fraud demands under Section 73. This waiver applies to demands for which of the following financial years?

Q96. A principal sends raw materials (inputs) to a job worker without payment of tax. Due to unforeseen production delays, the inputs are not returned to the principal within 1 year. Under the CGST Act, what is the consequence?
A. A mandatory penalty of Rs. 10,000 is automatically levied on the principal.
B. The principal must write off the value of the goods and reverse ITC immediately.
C. The inputs are deemed to be a supply by the principal to the job worker on the sent date.
D. The job worker is prosecuted under Section 132 for illegal retention of goods.
Answer: C
Explanation: Under Section 143, inputs sent for job work must return within 1 year. If they fail to return within this period, it is deemed a supply from the principal to the job worker on the date they were originally sent out.

Q97. Which of the following statements regarding the cross-empowerment of SGST/UTGST officers under Section 6 of the CGST Act is/are correct?

B. 1 and 3 only

Explanation: SGST/UTGST officers are proper officers for CGST. A key safeguard against double jeopardy states that once one tax authority initiates proceedings on a subject matter, the other shall not initiate on the same subject matter.



Q98. Under Section 36 of the CGST Act, for how many months from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the year must a registered person generally retain accounts and records?

Q99. Consider the following statements regarding the Time of Supply under Sections 12 and 13 of the CGST Act:

1. For goods under forward charge, it is the earlier of the date of issue of invoice or the date of receipt of payment.
2. For services under reverse charge, it is the earlier of the date of payment or the 61st day from the supplier's invoice.
3. The special time-of-supply rule for vouchers remains unchanged after the Finance (No. 7) Act, 2025.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

Q100. Which of the following is explicitly included within the scope of "supply" under Section 7 of the CGST Act, even if made without consideration?
A. Import of services, whether or not it is executed in the regular course of business.
B. Activities specified in Schedule I, such as permanent transfer of business assets.
C. Activities specified in Schedule III, including services provided by a court or tribunal.
D. Any transfer of title in goods to an unrelated person for an agreed-upon consideration.
Answer: B
Explanation: Section 7 includes activities specified in Schedule I (such as supplies between related persons, or permanent transfer of business assets where ITC was availed) as supply, even if made without consideration. Import of services requires consideration to be a supply unless covered by Schedule I.
