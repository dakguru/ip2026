Q1. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the extent and applicability of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988?

1. It extends to the whole of India except the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir.
2. It applies to all citizens of India outside India.
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: B
Explanation:
Under Section 1(2), the Act extends to the whole of India and applies to all citizens of India outside India. The exclusion of Jammu and Kashmir was omitted by Act 34 of 2019, effective from 31 October 2019.

Q2. Consider the following statements regarding the definition of a "public servant" under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988:

1. Any person in the service or pay of a local authority is a public servant.
2. An office-bearer of an agricultural co-operative society receiving financial aid from the Government is a public servant.
3. A person is considered a public servant only if formally appointed by the Government.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation:
Sections 2(c)(ii) and 2(c)(ix) include local authority employees and specific co-operative society office-bearers as public servants. Explanation 1 clarifies that persons fall under these sub-clauses whether or not appointed by the Government, making statement 3 incorrect.

Q3. A public servant 'X' asks a citizen 'Y' to pay Rs. 2,000 to process a legitimate passport application on time. 'X' is eventually caught. Under which section of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 is 'X' guilty?
A. Section 7
B. Section 7A
C. Section 8
D. Section 11
Answer: A
Explanation:
Section 7 penalises a public servant obtaining an undue advantage intending to perform a public duty improperly or dishonestly. The statutory illustration in the Act specifically identifies taking money to process a routine application on time as an offence under this section.

Q4. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the trial of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988?

1. The trial shall endeavour to be concluded within two years.
2. The period of trial can be extended by a maximum of one year at a time for recorded reasons.
3. The total period of trial, including extensions, shall not ordinarily exceed four years in aggregate.
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: C
Explanation:
Under Section 4(4), the trial should ideally conclude within two years. Extensions may be granted for recorded reasons, not exceeding six months at a time (not one year), with an aggregate maximum of four years.

Q5. A private individual promises to pay Rs. 50,000 to a public servant to secure a tender but does so because he was compelled. He fails to report the matter to the investigating agency. Which of the following statements is correct regarding his liability?
A. He is completely exempt from prosecution as he was compelled.
B. He is liable for punishment under Section 8 of the Act.
C. He is liable only for a fine under Section 9 of the Act.
D. He is treated as an accomplice and granted automatic pardon under Section 5.
Answer: B
Explanation:
Section 8(1) criminalises bribe-giving. Proviso 1 and 2 state that a compelled person escapes liability only if they report the matter to the law-enforcement authority within seven days. Since he failed to report, he is liable under Section 8.

Q6. Consider the following statements regarding commercial organisations under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988:

1. A commercial organisation can be punished with imprisonment up to 7 years.
2. It is a defence for the organisation if it proves it had adequate procedures to prevent associated persons from bribing.
3. An officer of the organisation is guilty only if the offence was committed with their consent or connivance.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation:
Under Section 9, a commercial organisation is punishable with fine, not imprisonment. Statement 2 is correct per the proviso to Section 9(1). Statement 3 is correct per Section 10, which requires consent or connivance of the officer.

Q7. What is the maximum imprisonment prescribed for a public servant convicted of criminal misconduct by intentionally enriching himself illicitly during his term of office?
A. 5 years
B. 7 years
C. 10 years
D. 14 years
Answer: C
Explanation:
Under Section 13(2), the punishment for criminal misconduct (which includes intentional illicit enrichment or disproportionate assets under 13(1)(b)) is imprisonment of not less than 4 years, which may extend to 10 years, and also a fine.

Q8. Which of the following statements is/are not correct regarding prior approval for investigation under Section 17A?

1. Previous approval is mandatory before arresting a public servant on the spot on the charge of accepting a bribe.
2. The authority must convey its decision regarding approval within three months.
3. The time limit for conveying the decision can be extended by a maximum of one month.
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation:
Proviso 1 to Section 17A explicitly states that no approval is needed for an arrest on the spot on the charge of accepting or attempting to accept an undue advantage. Statements 2 and 3 correctly state the timelines (3 months + 1 month extension).

Q9. Consider the following statements regarding sanction for prosecution under Section 19:

1. Sanction is required for prosecuting a retired public servant for offences under Section 7 committed during his tenure.
2. A defect in sanction automatically vitiates the trial and mandates reversal of sentence.
3. The authority competent to remove the public servant at the time of the alleged offence grants the sanction.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation:
Section 19 expressly covers retired public servants. Section 19(2) defines the competent authority. However, Section 19(3) states that no finding or sentence is reversed merely for an error or irregularity in sanction unless a failure of justice has in fact resulted.

Q10. An inspector of the CBI wishes to inspect the bankers' books of a suspect under the Prevention of Corruption Act. Which of the following conditions must be met?

Answer: C
Explanation:
Section 18 empowers police officers to inspect bankers' books, but the proviso explicitly states this power must not be exercised by an officer below the rank of Superintendent of Police unless specially authorised by an officer of or above that rank.

Q11. Which of the following is/are correct regarding the summary trial of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988?

1. A Special Judge may try any offence under this Act summarily if the maximum punishment is less than 3 years.
2. The power to try summarily is confined to contraventions linked to the Essential Commodities Act, 1955.
3. On a summary conviction, the maximum sentence is imprisonment up to one year.
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation:
Section 6 restricts the power of a Special Judge to try cases summarily specifically to offences committed in relation to the contravention of an order under Section 12A of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955. The maximum sentence on such summary conviction is one year.

Q12. Consider the following statements regarding the presumption where a public servant accepts an undue advantage:

1. The statutory presumption applies only to offences under Section 7 and Section 11.
2. The presumption applies to all offences under the Act, including criminal misconduct.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation:
Following the 2018 Amendment, Section 20 restricts the statutory presumption exclusively to trials of offences under Section 7 or Section 11. The presumption requires the accused to prove the contrary once acceptance of undue advantage is established.

Q13. What is the minimum imprisonment prescribed for a habitual offender under Section 14 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988?
A. 3 years
B. 4 years
C. 5 years
D. 7 years
Answer: C
Explanation:
Section 14 prescribes that whoever, having been convicted of an offence under the Act, subsequently commits another offence punishable under the Act, shall be punished with imprisonment of not less than 5 years, which may extend to 10 years, and also fine.

Q14. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the rights of an accused as a witness under Section 21?

1. A person charged under the Act is a competent witness for the defence.
2. The accused can be called as a witness by the prosecution without his request.
3. Failure of the accused to give evidence can raise a presumption against him.
A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: A
Explanation:
Section 21 makes the accused a competent witness for the defence, but clause (a) states he shall not be called except at his own request, and clause (b) states his failure to give evidence cannot be commented on or raise any presumption against him.

Q15. Under Section 23 of the Act, in framing a charge for misappropriation under Section 13(1)(a), the period between the first and last date of the alleged offence shall not exceed:
A. Six months
B. One year
C. Two years
D. Three years
Answer: B
Explanation:
Section 23 allows the framing of a charge for misappropriation without specifying exact items or dates, provided the period between the first and last date does not exceed one year.

Q16. Consider the following statements about Section 17 (Persons authorised to investigate):

1. In Delhi, an Inspector of Police of the Delhi Special Police Establishment can investigate without a Magistrate's order.

Proviso 2 to Section 17 explicitly mandates that an offence under Section 13(1)(b) (disproportionate assets) shall not be investigated without the order of an officer not below the rank of a Superintendent of Police, making statement 3 incorrect.

Q17. A State Government employee is transferred to a Central Government post on deputation. There is no specific mutual agreement regarding joining time. Which rules will regulate his joining time for the inward journey?

Explanation:
Under Rule 2 of the CCS (Joining Time) Rules, 1979, when an employee of a State Government is appointed to a Central Government post on deputation, joining time for the inward journey is regulated by the CCS Rules unless different provisions are expressly agreed upon.

Q18. Which of the following categories of employees are covered under the CCS (Joining Time) Rules, 1979?

Rule 1 explicitly covers work-charged staff but specifically excludes Railway employees, Armed Forces personnel, contract employees, and those paid out of contingencies from the application of the CCS (Joining Time) Rules, 1979.

Q19. An official is temporarily transferred to a new station for a period of 150 days. Which of the following statements is correct regarding his entitlement?

Answer: C
Explanation:
Rule 4.1 stipulates that no joining time is admissible in cases of temporary transfer for a period not exceeding 180 days. In such cases, only the actual transit time, as admissible for journeys on tour, may be allowed.

Q20. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the crediting of unutilised joining time?

1. The unutilised joining time is credited to the leave account as earned leave.
2. A maximum of 15 days of unutilised joining time can be credited.

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: D
Explanation:
Under Rule 26 of the CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972 (referred to by Rule 6 of JT Rules), unavailed joining time up to a maximum of 15 days is credited as earned leave, subject to the condition that the total earned leave balance does not exceed the ceiling of 300 days.

Q21. An official is transferred to a station located 1,500 km away involving a change of residence. He travels by continuous road journey for 250 km. What is his joining time entitlement?
A. 10 days
B. 12 days
C. 15 days
D. 17 days
Answer: C
Explanation:
Under Rule 5(4), for distances "More than 1,000 km", the normal entitlement is 12 days. However, since the transfer necessarily involves continuous road travel of more than 200 km, the entitlement is enhanced to 15 days.

Q22. Consider the following statements regarding joining time pay and allowances:

2. House Rent Allowance (HRA) applicable to the new station is drawn during joining time.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer: B
Explanation:
Rule 7 states that the official draws HRA applicable to the old station from which he was transferred, not the new station. Statements 1 and 3 are correct; joining time pay equals old pay, and Conveyance Allowance is not admissible.

Q23. A Government servant is transferred at his own request. How is the intervening period between relinquishing the old charge and taking up the new charge regularised?
A. By granting half the normal joining time.
B. By granting maximum 10 days of joining time.
C. By grant of leave of the kind due and admissible.
D. It is treated as dies non.
Answer: C
Explanation:
According to DoPT OM dated 29 January 1983 (Rule 4.4 notes), joining time is admissible only when the transfer is in public interest. Transfer on own request carries no joining time; the gap is regularised by granting leave of the kind due and admissible.

Q24. An official is transferred to the Andaman & Nicobar Islands from Delhi. The distance exceeds 2,000 km, and he travels by air. What is his total joining time entitlement?
A. 12 days
B. 14 days
C. 15 days
D. 17 days
Answer: B
Explanation:
For >2,000 km by air, the base entitlement is capped at 12 days. Under Note 2 to Rule 5(4), transfer to remote regions like A&N Islands attracts 2 additional days. Therefore, total entitlement is 12 + 2 = 14 days.

Q25. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the commencement of joining time?

Answer: A
Explanation:
Rule 5.1 specifies that joining time commences from the date of relinquishment if made over in the forenoon, or from the following day if made over in the afternoon. The rules do not absolutely prohibit commencement on a Sunday/holiday.

Q26. An official in transit receives revised transfer orders directing him to proceed to a different station. How will his joining time be regulated?

Rule 6.4 allows an official diverted in transit to retain the joining time already availed up to the receipt of orders, plus a completely fresh spell of full joining time calculated from the day following the date of receipt of the revised orders.

Q27. Who bears the cost of the joining-time pay when a Government servant is transferred in public interest?

Note 2 inserted after Rule 7 by the 2015 amendment explicitly states that the joining-time pay shall be paid by the new administrative authority where such Government servant joins on transfer.

Q28. Which of the following leave types cannot be combined with joining time?

Rule 6 expressly provides that joining time may be combined with vacation and/or regular leave of any kind or duration, except casual leave.

Q32. Under the CCS (Recognition of Service Associations) Rules, 1993, what is the minimum membership required for an association to be recognised?

Rule 5(d)(i) mandates that the association must represent a minimum of 35% of the total number of a category of employees to be eligible for recognition.

Q33. Consider the following scenario: In a specific category, Association X has 40% membership, Association Y has 20% membership, and Association Z has 15% membership. Which association(s) can be recognised under the CCS (RSA) Rules, 1993?
A. Only X
B. X and Y
C. X, Y, and Z
D. None, as no single association holds a 50% majority
Answer: B
Explanation:
Rule 5(d)(i) proviso states that where only one association commands more than 35%, a second association with the next highest membership may be recognised provided it commands at least 15%. Association Y is the second-highest and meets the 15% threshold.

Q34. Consider the following scenario: Association A has 30% membership and Association B has 25% membership. Which association(s) will be recognised?
A. Only A, as it has the highest membership
B. Both A and B, as their combined total exceeds 50%
C. Neither A nor B
D. Association A will be granted provisional recognition for one year
Answer: C
Explanation:
Neither association reaches the mandatory 35% threshold required by Rule 5(d)(i). The 15% relaxation proviso is only triggered when there is exactly one association already exceeding 35%. Hence, neither qualifies.

Rule 6(c) strictly prohibits an association from maintaining any political fund or lending itself to propagating the views of any political party. This is a condition for continued recognition, not an objective.

Q46. Under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, what is the consequence of a person attempting to commit an offence of misappropriation under Section 13(1)(a)?

Q47. Which of the following sections was entirely omitted by the Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018 because bribe-giving was made a direct offence?

Q51. Which of the following actions constitutes an offence by a middleman under Section 7A of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988?
