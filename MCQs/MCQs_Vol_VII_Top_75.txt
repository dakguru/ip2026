TOPIC 1 — DEPARTMENTS OF A SET   (Q1-Q7)
================================================================

Q1. Which of the following correctly defines the "Set" of a Sorting Mail Office under the Railway Mail Service?
A. A group of railway officials managing mail transit.
B. A team of officials working a section or office together during a fixed period.
C. A collection of sorting bags assigned to a specific station.
D. The geographical jurisdiction of a Railway Mail Division.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Under the definitions in Rule 1, a 'set' refers to the team of officials working together in a section or office, divided into departments for the distribution of work and responsibility.

Q2. In a full-set Sorting Mail Office, how many distinct departments are established for the distribution of work?
A. Two
B. Three
C. Four
D. Six
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• As per Rule 1(1), each set of a Sorting Mail Office is divided into four departments: Mail, Sorting, Registration, and Parcel.

Q3. Who is the official in charge of the Mail Department in a Sorting Mail Office?
A. Sorting Assistant
B. Registration Sorting Assistant
C. Parcel Sorting Assistant
D. Head Sorting Assistant
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• According to Rule 1, the Head Sorting Assistant (HSA) is the official in charge of the Mail Department and is overall responsible for the set.

Q4. In a set where only two Sorting Assistants are employed, what is the prescribed arrangement for the distribution of work?
A. One Assistant handles Mail/Sorting while the other handles Registration/Parcel.
B. One Assistant handles all departments while the other remains in reserve.
C. The Head Sorting Assistant handles all four departments.
D. Both Assistants work jointly across all four departments.
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
• Rule 1(3) mandates that in a set of only two SAs, the HSA handles the sorting-department work in addition to his own, while the other SA handles both registration and parcel work.

Q5. Which official is designated as the "Mail Sorting Assistant" in sets headed by a selection-grade Head Sorting Assistant?
A. The most junior Sorting Assistant.
B. A senior Sorting Assistant responsible for receipt and dispatch of mails.
C. The Record Officer on special duty.
D. The Registration Sorting Assistant.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 1(4) specifies that in selection-grade sets, the receipt/dispatch of mails and opening/closing of bags is assigned to a senior SA designated as the Mail Sorting Assistant.

Q6. A Sorting Mail Office set has three sanctioned Sorting Assistants. Due to staff shortage, the separate Parcel Sorting Assistant is not employed. What is the correct procedural disposal of parcel duties?
A. The duties are kept in abeyance.
B. The HSA must handle the parcel work.
C. The parcel duties go to the Registration Sorting Assistant.
D. The parcel work is transferred to the nearest Head Post Office.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Under Rule 1(3), where no separate Parcel Sorting Assistant is employed, the parcel duties are assigned to the Registration Sorting Assistant.

Q7. Which of the following statements regarding the departmental structure of an RMS set is correct?
A. Running sorting sections currently perform sorting work inside trains.
B. The HSA handles the Registration Department directly in all sets.
C. Duties of two or more departments can be combined based on sanctioned establishment.
D. The Mail Sorting Assistant is always the junior-most official in a set.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 1(2) allows for the combination of department duties when the number of Sorting Assistants is less than the number of departments. Note that sorting work in trains has been abolished.

TOPIC 2 — LATE-FEE POSTING   (Q8-Q13)
================================================================

Q8. What is the mandatory requirement for an unregistered article posted in a train letter-box at any time?
A. It must carry double postage.
B. It must carry the prescribed late fee in addition to postage.
C. It must be registered.
D. No extra fee is required.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 2(1) states that unregistered articles posted in train letter-boxes must carry the prescribed late fee in addition to the postage.

Q9. Which specific stamp is impressed on an unregistered letter (other than a postcard) posted in a van letter-box without the prepayment of a late fee?
A. "Unpaid — Return to Sender"
B. "Postage Due"
C. "Detained — Late Fee Not Paid"
D. "Late Fee Collected"
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Under Rule 2(2), articles (except postcards) where the late fee has not been prepaid are impressed with the 'Detained — Late Fee Not Paid' stamp.

Q10. How are articles treated if they are posted in a van letter-box but do not have the late fee prepaid?
A. They are delivered to the addressee with a penalty.
B. They are returned to the sender immediately.
C. They are made over loose to the first mail office for disposal.
D. They are destroyed after 24 hours.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 2(2) stipulates that such articles are made over loose to the first mail office (or a Post Office acting as one) for disposal as though posted there.

Q11. Which of the following articles is EXEMPT from the application of the 'Detained — Late Fee Not Paid' stamp even if the late fee is not prepaid?
A. Inland Letter Cards
B. Envelopes
C. Packets
D. Postcards
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• Rule 2(2) explicitly excludes postcards from the detention rule when the late fee is not prepaid.

Q12. Under what specific condition may an RMS section receive an article by hand?
A. If the sender is a government official.
B. If the van has no letter-box or the article is too large for the box.
C. During the last 5 minutes before train departure.
D. Hand-to-hand receipt is strictly prohibited without exception.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 2(3) provides a narrow exception: articles are received by hand only if the van has no letter-box or if the article is too large for the existing box.

Q13. Which of the following statements about late-fee posting is correct?
A. Late-fee notices on mail vans are optional.
B. Stamps on articles in the van box are obliterated before the train leaves the station.
C. Articles with paid late fees are forwarded by the nearest route.
D. RMS offices do not have a prescribed late-fee period.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 2(2) confirms that articles with the late fee paid are forwarded by the nearest route. Rule 2(4) requires a painted notice on vans, and stamps are obliterated after departure.

TOPIC 3 — INTERRUPTIONS TO MAIL MOVEMENT   (Q14-Q18)
================================================================

Q14. In the event of an interruption due to floods or accidents, to whom does the HSA report if the detention is likely to be less than 6 hours?
A. Superintendent and Record Office.
B. Only the Superintendent.
C. Only the Record Office.
D. Head of the Circle.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 7 states that for detentions not exceeding 6 hours, the report (by fax/telegraph) goes only to the Record Office.

Q15. If a detention due to a railway breach is expected to exceed 6 hours, who must receive the immediate fax/telegraph report?
A. The Superintendent and the Record Office.
B. The Superintendent and the Head of Circle.
C. The Inspector and the Superintendent.
D. The Railway Guard and the Record Office.
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
• Rule 7 requires that if detention likely exceeds 6 hours, it is reported at once to the Superintendent and the Record Office.

Q16. Who is responsible for reporting interruptions to mail movement while a set is on duty?
A. The Mail Guard
B. The Record Officer
C. The Head Sorting Assistant
D. The Divisional Superintendent
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 7 assigns the responsibility of reporting interruptions to the Head Sorting Assistant of the set on duty.

Q17. Which of the following is NOT a mandatory recipient of a standard (non-telegraphic) interruption report by the HSA?
A. The Superintendent
B. The Inspector
C. The Record Office
D. The Director-General
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• Rule 7 specifies the Superintendent, Inspector, and Record Office as recipients of the report. The DG is not mentioned for standard interruptions.

Q18. Which of the following correctly distinguishes the reporting threshold for mail interruptions?
A. 3 hours: Report to Superintendent; 6 hours: Report to DG.
B. 6 hours: Threshold for reporting to the Superintendent in addition to the Record Office.
C. 12 hours: Threshold for reporting to the Head of Circle.
D. 24 hours: Threshold for cancelling the set.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• The critical distinction in Rule 7 is the 6-hour mark, which triggers the requirement to include the Superintendent in the immediate telegraphic/fax report.

================================================================

Q19. Which of the following correctly defines the status and purpose of an RMS Metal Token?
A. It is personal property used for identification at banks.
B. It is Government property used to authorize travel and platform entry.
C. It is a reward for senior Sorting Assistants.
D. It is a key used to open the mail van.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 14 defines tokens as Government property that must be shown to railway authorities and carried by all RMS officials on duty.

Q20. Before which event must an RMS official produce their metal token for inspection?
A. Before starting the out-trip.
B. Before monthly salary is disbursed.
C. Before entering the Head Record Office.
D. Before signing the Order Book.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 14(2) mandates that every holder must produce the token before his monthly salary is disbursed.

Q21. To whom must the loss of a metal token be reported immediately?
A. The Superintendent
B. The Railway Police
C. The Record Officer
D. The Head of the Circle
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 14(4) states that on loss, the holder reports at once to the Record Officer.

Q22. Which RMS official is EXEMPT from carrying a metal token?
A. A Sorting Assistant traveling in a mail van.
B. A Supervising Officer traveling in a reserved vehicle on duty.
C. A Mail Guard of a transit section.
D. A newly appointed MTS official.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 14(1) exempts Supervising Officers who travel on duty in a reserved vehicle/compartment.

Q23. A Sorting Assistant is transferring from one Record Office to another. What is the correct protocol regarding his metal token?
A. He carries the token to the new office.
B. He returns the token in exchange for his receipt.
C. He must destroy the token.
D. He hands it over to his successor in the set.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 14(3) requires the holder to return the token in exchange for his receipt when transferring or quitting service.

Q24. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of an RMS metal token?
A. It bears the Division name.
B. It bears a serial number.
C. It bears the name of the official.
D. It is treated as part of Record Office stock.
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 14(2) mentions the Division name and serial number but not the official's name.

Q31. In a Record Office with one or more Sorting Assistants, who are the joint custodians of the keys to the safe?
A. Record Officer and the Superintendent.
B. Record Officer and a nominated Sorting Assistant.
C. Two Sorting Assistants.
D. Record Officer and the local Postmaster.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(4) states that in a Record/Sub-Record office, keys are held by the Record/Sub-Record Officer and a nominated SA as joint custodian.

Q32. How many locks must secure the safe in a Head Record Office beyond working hours?
A. One lock
B. Two locks
C. Three locks
D. No locks, only a seal
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(6) requires the safe to be secured with both locks beyond working hours.

Q33. Who holds the keys of the safe in a Head Record Office?
A. Head Record Officer and the Inspector.
B. Head Record Officer and the Accountant.
C. Accountant and the Superintendent.
D. Head Record Officer and the senior-most SA.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(3) specifies that in an HRO, one key is held by the HRO and the other by the Accountant.

Q34. During working hours, how many locks are typically used to secure the safe in a Record Office?
A. One lock
B. Two locks
C. No locks
D. Four locks
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
• Rule 19(5) allows the safe to be secured with one lock during working hours.

Q35. An RMS office is required to keep cash in a safe embedded in a Police Station. What is the prescribed equipment for this purpose?
A. One large cash chest with three locks.
B. Two safes — one for working hours and one for after hours.
C. A portable cash box.
D. No safe is needed; the Police take responsibility.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(7) mandates two safes in such cases — one (single lock) for working hours and one (double locks) for after hours.

Q36. Which of the following is strictly prohibited from being kept in the Government cash safe?
A. Postage stamps
B. Valuables
C. Private money or property
D. Permanent advance vouchers
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 19(1) strictly prohibits private money or property from being kept in the cash safe.

Q37. In a single-handed Record Office, how are the keys to the cash safe managed?
A. One key is given to the local station master.
B. Both keys are held by the Record/Sub-Record Officer.
C. One key is sent to the Divisional office daily.
D. The office is not allowed to have a safe.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(4) specifies that in a single-handed office, both keys are held by the Record/Sub-Record Officer.

C. A first-aid box

B. Carbolic soap

Q69. In contrast to a section, what is the correct sequence of mail exchange for a Mail Office?
