Q1. Consider the following statements regarding "accountable items" in the postal system:
1.	They provide a receipt at the time of booking for track and trace purposes.
2.	They do not require the signature of the addressee upon delivery.
3.	Items with value-added services like Cash on Delivery (COD) and insurance are classified as accountable items.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct? A. 1 only B. 1 and 3 only C. 2 and 3 only D. 1, 2, and 3
Correct Answer: B Explanation:
•	Concept: Accountable items are specifically designed to provide evidence of delivery and track-and-trace capabilities. They strictly require the signature (in ink or digital) of the addressee or their authorized person. Examples include Speed Post, India Post Parcel, and items with COD or insurance.
•	Elimination: Statement 2 is incorrect because the signature of the addressee (or authorized person) is a mandatory requirement for these items to provide evidence of delivery.

Q164. Based on the Postal Infringement Penalty Matrix, if an item is posted as "Literature for the Blind" (which requires 0 postage) but is found to violate the conditions and is re-classified as a Parcel, how is the penalty calculated?

Q165. A customer books an item under "Gyan Post" (weighing 300g, paying Rs. 37). It is found to contain commercial business materials, causing an infringement. A. Rs. 16
B. Rs. 32
C. Rs. 69
D. Rs. 106
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
•	Concept: Gyan Post enjoys a unique penalty framework. Unlike ordinary Book Post, where the penalty doubles the gross target postage before subtracting the paid amount, Gyan Post directly doubles the net deficiency.
•	Elimination: Row 6 of the matrix specifies the exact mathematical formula for a Gyan Post infringement: 2(53 − 37). 53 − 37 = 16. 2 × 16 = 32. Therefore, exactly Rs. 32 is recovered.
