── Sub-Section 1.1: Scope, Definition of Goods & Fundamental Principles (R142–145) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q1–Q18 ──

Q1. The expression 'goods', for the purpose of the procurement rules under the General Financial Rules, 2017, includes —
A. Only tangible products physically delivered to a Government store
B. All tangible or intangible products such as software, technology transfer, licences, patents and other intellectual properties
C. Only manufactured items carrying a Bureau of Indian Standards mark
D. Only items appearing on the Government e-Marketplace catalogue
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 143 of the GFR, 2017, 'goods' covers all tangible or intangible products like software, technology transfer, licences, patents or other intellectual properties acquired for Government use. The definition is deliberately wide so that intellectual property and intangibles are brought within the procurement discipline.

Q2. Which of the following is expressly EXCLUDED from the definition of 'goods' under the General Financial Rules, 2017?
A. Installation and commissioning services
B. Insurance and transportation
C. Books, publications and periodicals for a library
D. Training and maintenance
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 143 states that 'goods' also covers works, services, transportation, insurance, installation, commissioning, training and maintenance, but specifically excludes books, publications, periodicals, etc. for a library, which are governed separately.

Q3. The coverage of the term 'goods' under the procurement rules extends to all of the following EXCEPT —
A. Transportation and insurance
B. Commissioning and training
C. Maintenance services
D. Periodicals procured for a departmental library
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Per Rule 143, library books, publications and periodicals are the carved-out exception; everything else listed — transportation, insurance, commissioning, training and maintenance — falls within 'goods'.

Q4. Consider the following statements regarding the definition of 'goods' under the GFR, 2017:
1. Intangible products such as licences and patents are included.
2. Works and services are excluded from the definition.
3. Maintenance is treated as part of 'goods'.
4. Books and periodicals for a library are excluded.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
A. 1 and 4 only
B. 1, 3 and 4 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3 only
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct under Rule 143. Statement 2 is wrong — 'goods' expressly COVERS works and services (along with transportation, insurance, installation, commissioning, training and maintenance); only library books/publications/periodicals are excluded.

Q5. Every authority delegated with financial powers of procuring goods in public interest is responsible and accountable to bring which of the following into public procurement?
A. Confidentiality, speed and discretion
B. Efficiency, economy and transparency
C. Flexibility, informality and convenience
D. Standardisation, secrecy and uniformity
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 144 lays down the fundamental principles of public buying — efficiency, economy and transparency — as the cornerstones of accountability for every authority exercising delegated procurement powers.

Q6. As per the fundamental principles of public buying, the description of the subject matter of procurement should, to the extent practicable, be —
A. Brand-specific to ensure quality
B. Objective, functional, generic and measurable
C. Restricted to a single approved manufacturer
D. Framed around a particular trade name
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 144, the description must be objective, functional, generic and measurable, specifying technical, qualitative and performance characteristics, so as to maximise competition and avoid favouritism.

Q7. The fundamental principles of public buying under Rule 144 require that the description of the subject matter of procurement should NOT —
A. Specify performance characteristics
B. Be measurable
C. Indicate a requirement for a particular trade mark, trade name or brand
D. Be functional and generic
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 144 prohibits indicating a requirement for a particular trade mark, trade name or brand, as this would distort competition. Specifications must remain generic and performance-based.

Q8. Under the General Financial Rules, 2017, every Ministry/Department is required to prepare an Annual Procurement Plan —
A. After the close of the financial year, for audit purposes
B. Before the commencement of the year, and place it on its website
C. Only when total procurement exceeds ₹50 crore
D. Once every three years
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 144 mandates that all Ministries/Departments prepare an Annual Procurement Plan before the commencement of the year and also place it on their website, promoting transparency and advance market awareness.

Q9. The fundamental principles of public buying contained in Rule 144 apply to —
A. Procurement of goods only
B. Procurement of consultancy services only
C. All procurements, including procurement of works
D. Only procurements routed through GeM
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 144 expressly states that the fundamental principles apply to all procurements including the procurement of works, making them the umbrella principles for the entire procurement framework.

Q10. An authority competent to sanction the purchase of goods required for use in public service derives that competence, under Rule 145, from —
A. The Central Vigilance Commission's manual
B. The Delegation of Financial Powers Rules
C. The Right to Information Act, 2005
D. The Indian Contract Act, 1872
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 145 provides that an authority competent to incur expenditure may sanction the purchase of goods in accordance with the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules, following the general procedure in the succeeding rules.

Q11. The competence to sanction the purchase of goods for public service is linked, under the GFR, 2017, to the authority's —
A. Seniority in service
B. Competence to incur expenditure under the DFPR
C. Membership of a tender committee
D. Designation as Head of Office
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 145, it is the authority competent to incur expenditure (as per the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules) that may sanction the purchase of goods. The power flows from expenditure competence, not merely from rank or office.

Q12. The requirement that procurement specifications be "objective, functional, generic and measurable" primarily serves to —
A. Reduce the paperwork involved in tendering
B. Ensure efficiency, economy and transparency by widening competition
C. Allow procurement from a pre-selected vendor
D. Permit the buyer to avoid e-procurement
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Generic, measurable specifications under Rule 144 prevent tailoring of tenders to a single brand, thereby widening competition and advancing the core principles of efficiency, economy and transparency.

Q13. Which one of the following correctly reflects the treatment of intangible products under the GFR procurement framework?
A. Software and licences are outside the scope of procurement rules
B. Software, technology transfer, licences and patents are 'goods'
C. Intangibles may be procured only by Scientific Departments
D. Patents are treated as 'works', not 'goods'
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 143 expressly classifies intangible products such as software, technology transfer, licences, patents and other intellectual properties as 'goods', subjecting their acquisition to the procurement rules.

Q14. A demand for procurement of works would be governed by the fundamental principles of public buying because —
A. Works are a sub-category of consultancy services
B. Rule 144 applies to all procurements including works
C. Works are always procured through GeM
D. The DFPR does not cover works
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 144 explicitly states its fundamental principles apply to all procurements including procurement of works, so the efficiency-economy-transparency mandate and the Annual Procurement Plan requirement extend to works.

Q15. Consider the following statements regarding the fundamental principles of public buying:
1. The principles apply to procurement of works as well as goods.
2. Specifications may indicate a particular brand to ensure quality.
3. An Annual Procurement Plan must be placed on the Department's website.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Statements 1 and 3 are correct under Rule 144. Statement 2 is wrong — the rule prohibits indicating a requirement for any particular trade mark, trade name or brand.

Q16. The procedure to be followed after an authority competent to incur expenditure sanctions the purchase of goods is —
A. Left entirely to the discretion of the purchasing officer
B. The general procedure contained in the succeeding rules of the chapter
C. Determined solely by the supplier
D. Governed by the Companies Act, 2013
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 145 provides that purchases are sanctioned in accordance with the DFPR, following the general procedure contained in the following rules of Chapter VI, ensuring uniformity in the purchase process.

Q17. The accountability for bringing efficiency, economy and transparency into public procurement rests with —
A. Only the Financial Adviser of the Ministry
B. Every authority delegated with the financial powers of procuring goods
C. Only the Secretary of the Department
D. Only the Government e-Marketplace
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 144 fixes responsibility and accountability on every authority delegated with the financial powers of procuring goods in public interest, not merely on senior functionaries.

Q18. Which of the following best describes the relationship between 'goods', 'works' and 'services' under Rule 143?
A. They are mutually exclusive categories with no overlap
B. 'Goods' is defined to also cover works and services, among other things
C. 'Services' alone are covered; goods are dealt with elsewhere
D. Only works are covered; goods and services are excluded
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 143 adopts an inclusive definition under which 'goods' also covers works and services, transportation, insurance, installation, commissioning, training and maintenance — a broad sweep with only library books/periodicals excluded.


── Sub-Section 1.2: GeM, Powers & Mandatory Procurement (R146–149) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q19–Q40 ──

Q19. Ministries or Departments have been delegated full powers to make their own arrangements for procurement of goods and services that —
A. Cost more than ₹50 crore
B. Are not available on the Government e-Marketplace (GeM)
C. Are required by Scientific Departments only
D. Are imported from abroad
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 147 delegates full powers to Ministries/Departments to make their own procurement arrangements for goods and services NOT available on GeM. Common-use goods and services available on GeM must be procured mandatorily through GeM.

Q20. Procurement of common-use goods and services available on the Government e-Marketplace is —
A. Optional, at the discretion of the buyer
B. Mandatory, as per Rule 149
C. Permitted only above ₹10,00,000
D. Prohibited for Central Government Departments
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 147 read with Rule 149 makes procurement of common-use goods and services available on GeM mandatory, to leverage aggregation, transparency and price discovery on the platform.

Q21. On the Government e-Marketplace, direct online purchase up to ₹50,000 may be made —
A. Only through the lowest-priced seller of three manufacturers
B. Through any available supplier meeting the requisite quality, specification and delivery period
C. Only after a reverse auction
D. Only with the prior approval of the Financial Adviser
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 149(i), purchases up to ₹50,000 on GeM may be made through any available supplier meeting the requisite quality, specification and delivery period — no comparison of sellers is mandated at this slab.

Q22. The monetary ceiling of ₹50,000 for direct purchase through any available GeM supplier was substituted (from the earlier ₹25,000) by —
A. The Finance Act, 2025
B. DoE O.M. dated 10.07.2024
C. The GeM SPV's own bye-laws
D. CVC guidelines dated 01.01.2024
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The enhanced ₹50,000 slab under Rule 149(i) was introduced by the Department of Expenditure O.M. No. F.1/3/2024-PPD dated 10.07.2024 (effective 10.07.2024), replacing the pre-amendment limit of ₹25,000.

Q23. On GeM, for procurement above ₹50,000 and up to ₹10,00,000, the purchase shall be made through the GeM Seller having the lowest price amongst available sellers of —
A. At least two different manufacturers
B. At least three different manufacturers
C. Any single manufacturer
D. At least five different manufacturers
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(ii) requires that, in the ₹50,000–₹10,00,000 slab, the purchase be from the lowest-priced GeM Seller among at least three different manufacturers meeting the requisite quality, specification and delivery period.

Q24. The upper limit of the middle GeM slab (lowest price among three manufacturers) is —
A. ₹5,00,000
B. ₹10,00,000
C. ₹25,00,000
D. ₹50,00,000
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under Rule 149(ii), as amended by DoE O.M. dated 10.07.2024, the slab runs above ₹50,000 and up to ₹10,00,000. The earlier upper limit was ₹5,00,000.

Q25. For procurement above ₹10,00,000 on GeM, the purchase shall be made through the supplier having the lowest price after mandatorily obtaining bids using —
A. Manual sealed tenders deposited at the Head Office
B. The online bidding or reverse auction tool provided on GeM
C. A single tender enquiry to the preferred vendor
D. Telephonic negotiations recorded in a file note
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(iii) requires that above ₹10,00,000, bids be obtained mandatorily through the online bidding or reverse auction tool on GeM, selecting the lowest-priced supplier meeting quality, specification and delivery requirements.

Q26. In the case of automobiles procured on GeM, the procurement under the first sub-rule (any available supplier) is permitted —
A. Only up to ₹50,000
B. Without any ceiling limit
C. Only through three manufacturers
D. Only after Cabinet approval
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The Note to Rule 149(i) provides that, in the case of automobiles, procurement under that sub-rule is permitted without any ceiling limit, a deliberate carve-out from the ₹50,000 cap.

Q27. For automobiles in the middle GeM slab, the existing monetary limit that continues to apply is —
A. ₹10 lakh
B. ₹30 lakh
C. ₹50 lakh
D. ₹2 crore
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(ii) preserves, for automobiles, the existing limit of ₹30 lakh (rather than ₹10,00,000), reflecting the higher unit cost of vehicles.

Q28. Ministries/Departments must project their Annual Procurement Plan of goods and services on the GeM portal within —
A. 15 days of Budget approval
B. 30 days of Budget approval
C. 60 days of Budget approval
D. 90 days of the start of the financial year
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(iv) requires the Annual Procurement Plan to be projected on the GeM portal within 30 days of Budget approval, after deciding on the OPEX or CAPEX mode at the Budget Estimates stage.

Q29. Under Rule 149(iv), Ministries/Departments are required to work out their procurement requirements on either —
A. "Lease" or "Buy" mode
B. "OPEX" mode or "CAPEX" model
C. "Cash" or "Credit" basis
D. "Domestic" or "Global" basis
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(iv) requires procurement requirements to be worked out on the "OPEX" (operating expenditure) mode or the "CAPEX" (capital expenditure) model, as per requirement/suitability, at the time of preparing Budget Estimates.

Q30. To ascertain the reasonableness of prices before placing an order on GeM, a Government Buyer may use the Business Analytics tools, which include —
A. The supplier's self-declared cost sheet
B. The Last Purchase Price on GeM and the Department's own Last Purchase Price
C. The retail price printed on the packaging
D. The price quoted in a foreign tender
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(v) permits the use of Business Analytics (BA) tools available on GeM — including the Last Purchase Price on GeM and the Department's own Last Purchase Price — to test price reasonableness before order placement.

Q31. A demand for goods shall NOT be divided into small quantities to make piecemeal purchases in order to —
A. Speed up delivery
B. Avoid L-1 buying / bidding / reverse auction on GeM, or the sanction of higher authorities
C. Distribute work among more suppliers
D. Reduce the storage burden
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(vi) prohibits splitting a demand into small quantities to evade L-1 buying/bidding/reverse auction on GeM or the necessity of obtaining higher authorities' sanction with reference to the total estimated value.

Q32. The Business Analytics tools for online bidding and online reverse auction available on GeM —
A. Can never be used below ₹10,00,000
B. Can be used by the Buyer even for procurements less than ₹10,00,000 if the competent authority so decides
C. Are mandatory for all purchases above ₹50,000
D. Are available only to Scientific Departments
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The proviso to Rule 149(ii) allows the BA tools for online bidding and reverse auction to be used even for procurements below ₹10,00,000, if decided by the competent authority — giving buyers flexibility to enhance price competition.

Q33. Consider the following slabs for direct online purchase on GeM under Rule 149:
1. Up to ₹50,000 — any available supplier.
2. Above ₹50,000 and up to ₹10,00,000 — lowest price among three manufacturers.
3. Above ₹10,00,000 — lowest price after mandatorily obtaining bids/reverse auction.
Which of the above is/are correctly stated?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: All three slabs are correctly stated as per Rule 149(i)–(iii), as amended by DoE O.M. dated 10.07.2024 (₹50,000 / ₹10,00,000 thresholds).

Q34. A Buyer wishes to purchase common-use stationery worth ₹45,000 that is listed on GeM. The correct course of action is to —
A. Float a limited tender enquiry to three firms
B. Purchase on GeM through any available supplier meeting quality, specification and delivery period
C. Constitute a Local Purchase Committee
D. Procure off-GeM under delegated full powers
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Since the item is a common-use good available on GeM and the value (₹45,000) is within the ₹50,000 slab, Rule 149(i) permits purchase through any available GeM supplier meeting the requisite parameters. Mandatory GeM procurement applies (Rule 147).

Q35. A Department intends to buy machinery worth ₹8,00,000 listed on GeM. Under Rule 149, the purchase must be made —
A. Through any available supplier without comparison
B. Through the GeM Seller having the lowest price among at least three different manufacturers
C. Only after a Global Tender Enquiry
D. By a Purchase Committee of three members
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: ₹8,00,000 falls in the ₹50,000–₹10,00,000 slab, so Rule 149(ii) requires selecting the lowest-priced GeM Seller from among at least three different manufacturers meeting quality, specification and delivery requirements.

Q36. A purchase of goods worth ₹14,00,000 on GeM must be made —
A. Through any available supplier of one manufacturer
B. By obtaining bids mandatorily through the online bidding/reverse auction tool and selecting the lowest price
C. Without quotations, by recording a certificate
D. By referring the case to the Cabinet
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: ₹14,00,000 exceeds ₹10,00,000, so Rule 149(iii) applies — bids must be obtained mandatorily via the GeM online bidding or reverse auction tool, and the lowest-priced compliant supplier selected.

Q37. The OPEX/CAPEX decision under Rule 149(iv) must be taken —
A. After the goods are delivered
B. At the time of preparation of Budget Estimates
C. Only when total value exceeds ₹50 lakh
D. At the time of payment to the supplier
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(iv) requires Ministries/Departments to decide between OPEX mode and CAPEX model as per requirement/suitability at the time of preparing Budget Estimates (BE), enabling proper budgetary planning.

Q38. Which of the following statements regarding procurement powers is correct?
A. Ministries have no powers to procure off-GeM goods
B. Ministries have full powers to procure goods/services not available on GeM, while GeM-available common-use items must be procured through GeM
C. All goods, whether on GeM or not, must be procured off-GeM
D. GeM procurement is optional in every case
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 147 grants full powers to make own arrangements for goods/services not on GeM, while making procurement of common-use goods/services that ARE on GeM mandatory (Rule 149). The two limbs operate together.

Q39. The principal purpose of permitting Business Analytics tools (including Last Purchase Price) on GeM is to —
A. Increase the number of suppliers on the portal
B. Enable the buyer to ascertain the reasonableness of prices before order placement
C. Replace the need for any quality check
D. Avoid preparing an Annual Procurement Plan
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 149(v) provides BA tools so that Government Buyers can satisfy themselves about price reasonableness — comparing the GeM Last Purchase Price and the Department's own Last Purchase Price — before placing the order.

Q40. Consider the following regarding automobiles on GeM:
1. Under the first sub-rule, automobile procurement is permitted without any ceiling.
2. In the middle slab, the existing limit of ₹30 lakh continues for automobiles.
3. Automobiles are entirely barred from procurement on GeM.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct (Note to Rule 149(i) and the carve-out in Rule 149(ii)). Statement 3 is wrong — automobiles are procured on GeM, subject to the special limits, not barred.


── Sub-Section 1.3: Registration of Suppliers (R150) · 🟡 HIGH · MCQs Q41–Q46 ──

Q41. For goods and services not available on GeM, the authority who may register suppliers periodically is the —
A. Financial Adviser
B. Head of the Ministry/Department
C. Comptroller and Auditor General
D. GeM Chief Executive Officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 150(1) empowers the Head of the Ministry/Department to register suppliers of goods and services specifically required by that Department or Office, for items not available on GeM, following a fair and transparent procedure.

Q42. Registration of suppliers under Rule 150 must be done following a procedure that is —
A. Quick and informal
B. Fair, transparent and reasonable, after due publicity
C. Confidential and restricted to known firms
D. Based solely on the lowest quoted price
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 150(1) requires registration to follow a fair, transparent and reasonable procedure after giving due publicity, so that all eligible suppliers have an opportunity to seek registration.

Q43. Before registering a supplier, the Department must carefully verify all of the following EXCEPT —
A. Manufacturing capability and quality-control system
B. Past performance and after-sales service
C. Financial background and credentials
D. The supplier's political affiliations
Correct Answer: D

 PART 2: PROCUREMENT OF SERVICES — A. CONSULTING SERVICES
 Source Rules: 177–196
 Importance Tags Inside: 🔴 ×6 · 🟡 ×7 · 🟢 ×5
 Total MCQs in this Part: 37

── Sub-Section 2.1: Definition, Hiring & Principles (R177–182) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q163–Q172 ──

Q163. "Consulting Service" under Rule 177 means any subject matter of procurement other than goods or works (except incidentals), and includes —
A. Only legal services
B. Professional, intellectual, training and advisory services
C. Only IT services
D. Only audit services
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 177 defines Consulting Service to include professional, intellectual, training and advisory services, or any other service so classified by a procuring entity, other than goods or works (save incidentals or consequentials).

Q164. The definition of "Consulting Service" under Rule 177 expressly does NOT include —
A. Advisory services
B. Training services
C. Direct engagement of a retired Government servant
D. Intellectual services
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 177 specifically excludes the direct engagement of a retired Government servant from the definition of Consulting Service.

Q165. Ministries/Departments may hire external professionals, consultancy firms or consultants under Rule 178 for —
A. Any indefinite ongoing task
B. A specific job well defined in terms of content and time frame for its completion
C. Routine clerical work
D. Permanent staffing needs
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 178 permits hiring of external professionals/consultancy firms/consultants for a specific job which is well defined in terms of content and time frame for its completion.

Q166. The term used throughout the rules to refer collectively to external professionals, consultancy firms or consultants is —
A. "Vendor"
B. "Consultant"
C. "Contractor"
D. "Agent"
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 178 provides that external professionals, consultancy firms or consultants are referred to as 'consultant' for the purposes of the rules.

Q167. The fundamental principles for engagement of consultants under Rule 179 apply to —
A. Only the Ministry of Finance
B. All Ministries or Departments
C. Only Scientific Departments
D. Only consultancy firms above ₹50 lakh
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 179 states that the fundamental principles regarding engagement of consultant(s) apply to all Ministries or Departments.

Q168. Which of the following is the correct sequence of preparatory steps for engaging a consultant under Rules 180–182?
A. Estimating expenditure → Identification of services → Scope
B. Identification of services required → Preparation of scope → Estimating reasonable expenditure
C. Scope → Estimating expenditure → Identification of services
D. RFP → TOR → Scope
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The rules proceed in order: identification of the services to be performed by consultants (Rule 180), preparation of the scope of the required consultant(s) (Rule 181), and estimating reasonable expenditure (Rule 182).

Q169. A retired Government servant engaged directly would NOT be treated as a "consultant" because —
A. Rule 177 excludes direct engagement of a retired Government servant from "Consulting Service"
B. Retired servants cannot be paid
C. Such engagement is always through GeM
D. The DFPR bars it
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 177 carves out the direct engagement of a retired Government servant from the definition of Consulting Service, so such engagement is governed by other instructions, not the consultancy-procurement rules.

Q170. Consider the following statements regarding consulting services:
1. Consulting Service excludes goods and works, save incidentals.
2. Consulting Service includes the direct engagement of a retired Government servant.
3. Consultants are hired for a specific job, well defined in content and time frame.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Statements 1 and 3 are correct (Rules 177 and 178). Statement 2 is wrong — Rule 177 EXCLUDES direct engagement of a retired Government servant.

Q171. The phrase "well defined in terms of content and time frame" in Rule 178 underscores that consultancy engagements should be —
A. Open-ended and indefinite
B. Specific, with a defined scope and completion timeline
C. Confined to retired Government servants
D. Limited to amounts below ₹50,000
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 178 requires the job for which a consultant is hired to be well defined in content and time frame, ensuring the engagement is specific and outcome-oriented rather than open-ended.

Q172. The step of "estimating reasonable expenditure" before engaging a consultant (Rule 182) is important primarily because it —
A. Determines the consultant's profit margin
B. Helps benchmark cost and informs the threshold-based identification of sources
C. Replaces the need for a Terms of Reference
D. Exempts the engagement from evaluation
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Estimating reasonable expenditure (Rule 182) provides a cost benchmark that, among other things, feeds into the threshold-based source-identification under Rule 183 (₹50 lakh) and supports economy and accountability.


── Sub-Section 2.2: Source Identification, Short-listing, TOR & RFP (R183–187) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q173–Q186 ──

Q173. Where the estimated cost of a consulting service is up to ₹50 lakh, a long list of potential consultants may be prepared on the basis of —
A. A Global Tender Enquiry only
B. Formal or informal enquiries from other Ministries/Departments/organisations, Chambers of Commerce & Industry, associations of consultancy firms, etc.
C. The single recommendation of the user department
D. An Expression of Interest on the CPPP
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 183(i) provides that, up to ₹50 lakh, the long list may be prepared from formal/informal enquiries from other Ministries/Departments/organisations involved in similar activities, Chambers of Commerce & Industry, associations of consultancy firms, etc.

Q174. Where the estimated cost of consulting services exceeds ₹50 lakh, in addition to the usual enquiries, the Department must publish —
A. A Single Tender Enquiry
B. An enquiry seeking 'Expression of Interest' from consultants on the Central Public Procurement Portal (CPPP)
C. A buy-back offer
D. A reverse auction notice
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 183(ii) requires that, above ₹50 lakh, an enquiry seeking 'Expression of Interest' from consultants be published on the CPPP (www.eprocure.gov.in), in addition to the enquiries permitted under sub-rule (i).

Q175. The ₹50 lakh threshold in both sub-rules of Rule 183 was substituted, in place of the earlier ₹25 lakh, by —
A. The Finance Act, 2025
B. DoE O.M. dated 10.07.2024
C. CVC guidelines
D. The GeM bye-laws
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The ₹50 lakh thresholds in Rule 183(i) and (ii) were substituted for the earlier ₹25 lakh by the Department of Expenditure O.M. dated 10.07.2024.

Q176. The number of short-listed consultants under Rule 184 should be —
A. Not less than three
B. Exactly two
C. Not more than three
D. At least five
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 184 requires that the number of short-listed consultants should not be less than three, to ensure adequate competition in the selection.

Q177. The Terms of Reference (TOR) under Rule 185 should include all of the following EXCEPT —
A. A precise statement of objectives and an outline of tasks
B. A schedule for completion of tasks
C. The support/inputs to be provided by the Ministry/Department
D. The names of the evaluation committee members
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 185 lists five contents of the TOR: precise statement of objectives; outline of tasks; schedule for completion; support/inputs by the Ministry/Department; and the final outputs required. The names of the evaluation committee members are not part of the TOR.

Q178. The Request for Proposal (RFP) under Rule 186 is the document used by the Ministry/Department to —
A. Pre-qualify suppliers of goods
B. Obtain offers (technical and financial proposals) from the shortlisted consultants
C. Publish bid awards
D. Register suppliers under Rule 150
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 186 provides that the RFP is the document used to obtain offers from consultants for the required service, and is issued to the shortlisted consultants to seek their technical and financial proposals.

Q179. Proposals from consultants under Rule 187 should ordinarily be obtained in a —
A. Single-bid system
B. 'Two bid' system with technical and financial bids sealed separately
C. Reverse-auction format
D. Walk-in negotiation
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 187 requires proposals to be asked for in a 'Two bid' system, with technical and financial bids sealed separately, placed within a larger sealed envelope and submitted by the specified date/time at the specified place.

Q180. On receipt of consultancy proposals under the Two-bid system, the bids opened first are the —
A. Financial bids
B. Technical proposals
C. Both bids together
D. Only the lowest-priced bid
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 187 provides that, on receipt, the technical proposals should be opened first by the Ministry/Department at the specified date, time and place — the financial bids being opened only later, for technically qualified bidders.

Q181. The minimum number of short-listed consultants (not less than three) under Rule 184 is intended to —
A. Limit competition to three firms
B. Ensure a competitive field while keeping evaluation manageable
C. Match the three-member Local Purchase Committee
D. Exclude small consultancy firms
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 184's floor of three short-listed consultants ensures a genuinely competitive field for technical and financial evaluation, while keeping the evaluation exercise manageable.

Q182. A consultancy assignment is estimated at ₹40 lakh. Under Rule 183, the Department should prepare the long list —
A. Only by publishing an EOI on the CPPP
B. From formal/informal enquiries (other Ministries, Chambers of Commerce, associations of consultancy firms, etc.)
C. By Global Tender Enquiry
D. By Single Source Selection
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: At ₹40 lakh (up to ₹50 lakh), Rule 183(i) applies — the long list is prepared from formal or informal enquiries; the EOI-on-CPPP requirement under Rule 183(ii) is triggered only above ₹50 lakh.

Q183. A consultancy assignment is estimated at ₹70 lakh. Under Rule 183, the Department must, in addition to the usual enquiries —
A. Constitute a Local Purchase Committee
B. Publish an enquiry seeking Expression of Interest on the CPPP
C. Use a reverse auction
D. Limit the field to two consultants
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: At ₹70 lakh (above ₹50 lakh), Rule 183(ii) requires publication of an EOI on the CPPP in addition to the enquiries under sub-rule (i).

Q184. Consider the following statements regarding consultancy source identification and short-listing:
1. Up to ₹50 lakh, the long list may be prepared from formal/informal enquiries.
2. Above ₹50 lakh, an EOI must be published on the CPPP.
3. The number of short-listed consultants should not be less than three.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: All three are correct: Rule 183(i) and (ii) (₹50 lakh threshold) and Rule 184 (not less than three short-listed consultants).

Q185. The TOR content "the final outputs that will be required of the Consultant" under Rule 185 serves to —
A. Fix the consultant's fee
B. Define the deliverables against which performance is judged
C. List the evaluation committee
D. Replace the RFP
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 185(v) requires the TOR to state the final outputs required of the Consultant, clearly defining the deliverables and providing the benchmark for assessing performance.

Q186. Under Rule 187, the two sealed envelopes (technical and financial) are to be —
A. Submitted to two different offices
B. Put inside a bigger, duly sealed envelope and submitted by the specified date and time at the specified place
C. Emailed to the evaluation committee
D. Opened together on receipt
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 187 requires the bidder to place the two separately sealed envelopes inside a bigger, duly sealed envelope and submit it by the specified date/time at the specified place; the technical proposals are opened first on receipt.


── Sub-Section 2.3: Evaluation, QCBS, LCS, Single Source & Monitoring (R188–196) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q187–Q199 ──

Q187. Technical bids for consultancy are analysed and evaluated by a —
A. Local Purchase Committee
B. Consultancy Evaluation Committee (CEC) constituted by the Ministry/Department
C. Tender Awarding Authority
D. Single nominated officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 189 requires technical bids to be analysed and evaluated by a Consultancy Evaluation Committee (CEC) constituted by the Ministry/Department, which records in detail the reasons for acceptance or rejection of the proposals.

Q188. The Consultancy Evaluation Committee under Rule 189 is required to record —
A. Only the names of the bidders
B. In detail, the reasons for acceptance or rejection of the technical proposals it evaluates
C. Only the financial scores
D. Nothing, as evaluation is automatic
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 189 obliges the CEC to record in detail the reasons for acceptance or rejection of the technical proposals analysed and evaluated by it, ensuring a transparent and auditable evaluation.

Q189. Under Rule 190, financial bids are opened of only those bidders who —
A. Quoted the lowest price
B. Have been declared technically qualified by the CEC
C. Are registered with GeM
D. Submitted bids first
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 190 provides that the Ministry/Department shall open the financial bids of only those bidders declared technically qualified by the Consultancy Evaluation Committee (per Rule 189), for further evaluation, ranking and selection.

Q190. In Quality and Cost Based Selection (QCBS), the proposal selected is the one with the —
A. Lowest financial bid, irrespective of quality
B. Highest weighted combined score of quality and cost
C. Highest technical score alone
D. Lowest technical score
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 192(i) provides that, in QCBS, the proposal with the highest weighted combined score (quality and cost) shall be selected, after specifying the minimum qualifying technical score and the relative weightages.

Q191. In QCBS, the weightage of the technical (non-financial) parameters should in no case exceed —
A. 50 percent
B. 60 percent
C. 70 percent
D. 80 percent
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 192(ii) provides that the weightage of the technical (i.e., non-financial) parameters should in no case exceed 80 percent.

Q192. Under the QCBS illustration, a bidder is rejected at the technical stage because the eligible technical score must be —
A. 50 and above
B. 60 and above
C. 70 and above
D. 80 and above
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: In the QCBS illustration, Bidder 3 (technical mark 55) is rejected because the eligible technical score should be 70 and above — only bidders meeting this threshold proceed to financial evaluation.

Q193. In the QCBS illustration, the financial score is computed using the formula —
A. F / LFB × 100
B. LFB / F × 100 (Lowest Financial Bid ÷ Quoted Amount × 100)
C. F − LFB
D. LFB × F
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The QCBS illustration converts the financial bid to a score as LFB / F × 100, where LFB is the Lowest Financial Bid and F is the quoted amount — so the lowest bidder scores 100 and higher quotes score proportionately less.

Q194. In the Least Cost System (LCS) under Rule 193, the proposal selected is the —
A. Highest technically scored proposal
B. Responsive, technically qualified proposal with the lowest evaluated cost (no weightage to technical score)
C. Proposal with the highest combined score
D. Proposal nominated by the user department
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 193 provides that, in LCS, there is no weightage for technical score in the final evaluation; the responsive technically qualified proposal with the lowest evaluated cost is selected.

Q195. Single Source Selection / consultancy by nomination is considered appropriate only under exceptional circumstances such as —
A. The estimated cost being below ₹50 lakh
B. Continuation of previous work, an emergency/disaster situation, or only one consultant having the requisite expertise
C. The presence of three or more eligible consultants
D. A routine annual assignment
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 194 limits Single Source Selection/nomination to exceptional circumstances: continuation of previous work by the firm; an emergency or post-disaster situation (or where timely completion is of utmost importance); or where only one consultant has the requisite expertise.

Q196. Which of the following is NOT a ground for Single Source Selection of a consultant under Rule 194?
A. Continuation of previous work carried out by the firm
B. An emergency situation after a natural disaster
C. Only one consultant has the requisite expertise
D. The consultant quoting the lowest fee in open competition
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 194 lists three exceptional grounds (continuation of previous work; emergency/disaster/time-critical; sole expertise). Quoting the lowest fee in open competition is the antithesis of single-source nomination and is not a ground.

Q197. A design competition for symbols/logos under Rule 196 must be conducted in a manner that is —
A. Confidential and restricted
B. Transparent, fair and objective, with wide publicity including on the CPPP
C. Limited to Government employees
D. Decided solely by the Secretary
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 196 requires design competitions to be conducted in a transparent, fair and objective manner, with wide publicity so that the information is accessible to all possible participants, including on the Central Public Procurement Portal.

Q198. Consider the following methods of consultancy selection:
1. QCBS selects the proposal with the highest weighted combined score.
2. In LCS, technical score carries no weightage in the final evaluation.
3. Single Source Selection is permissible where only one consultant has the requisite expertise.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: All three are correct: Rule 192 (QCBS — highest weighted combined score), Rule 193 (LCS — no technical weightage), and Rule 194 (sole-expertise ground for single source).

Q199. In a QCBS evaluation with 70:30 weightage, a bidder with a technical score of 100 and a financial score of 76.92 obtains a Combined Technical & Financial Score of —
A. 86.00
B. 87.99
C. 93.07
D. 100.00
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Per the QCBS illustration, CTFS = 100 × (70/100) + 76.92 × (30/100) = 70 + 23.07 = 93.07, ranking that bidder L1. The combined score weights quality and cost in the specified ratio.

── Sub-Section 3.1: Definition, Tender Enquiry, Bids & Nomination (R197–206) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q200–Q214 ──

Q200. "Non-Consulting Service" under Rule 197 means any procurement involving physical, measurable deliverables/outcomes (other than goods or works, save incidentals) where performance standards can be clearly identified and consistently applied, and includes —
A. Professional and advisory services
B. Maintenance, hiring of vehicles, facilities management, security, photocopier service, janitorial and office errand services, etc.
C. Direct engagement of a retired Government servant
D. Intellectual and training services
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 197 defines Non-Consulting Service by reference to physical, measurable deliverables with clear performance standards, and lists examples such as maintenance, hiring of vehicles, building facilities management, security, photocopier service, janitorial and office errand services, drilling, aerial photography, satellite imagery and mapping.

Q201. The essential distinguishing feature of a Non-Consulting Service (as against a Consulting Service) is that it involves —
A. Intellectual and advisory inputs
B. Physical, measurable deliverables/outcomes with clearly identifiable performance standards
C. Engagement of retired Government servants
D. Only IT advisory work
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 197 hinges the definition on physical, measurable deliverables/outcomes where performance standards can be clearly identified and consistently applied — distinguishing it from the intellectual/advisory character of Consulting Services under Rule 177.

Q202. For a non-consulting service of estimated value up to ₹50 lakh, the Ministry/Department should issue a —
A. Global Tender Enquiry
B. Limited tender enquiry to prima facie eligible and capable contractors identified under Rule 199
C. Single Tender Enquiry as a matter of course
D. Buy-back offer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 201(i) provides that, for non-consulting services up to ₹50 lakh, the Department scrutinises the preliminary list of likely contractors (Rule 199), decides the prima facie eligible and capable contractors, and issues a limited tender enquiry to them.

Q203. The ₹50 lakh threshold for the limited-tender route for non-consulting services was substituted, in place of the earlier ₹10 lakh, by —
A. The Finance Act, 2025
B. DoE O.M. dated 10.07.2024
C. CVC guidelines
D. The GeM bye-laws
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The ₹50 lakh threshold in Rule 201 (for both the limited-tender and advertisement limbs) was substituted for the earlier ₹10 lakh by the Department of Expenditure O.M. dated 10.07.2024.

Q204. In a limited tender enquiry for non-consulting services up to ₹50 lakh, the number of contractors identified should be —
A. Exactly three
B. More than three
C. At least two
D. Not more than three
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 201(i) requires the number of contractors identified for issuing the limited tender enquiry to be more than three, ensuring adequate competition.

Q205. For a non-consulting service of estimated value above ₹50 lakh, the Ministry/Department should issue an advertisement on —
A. The Department's notice board only
B. The Central Public Procurement Portal (CPPP) at www.eprocure.gov.in and on GeM
C. A single newspaper only
D. The supplier's website only
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 201(ii) requires that, above ₹50 lakh, an advertisement be given on the CPPP (www.eprocure.gov.in) and on GeM; an organisation with its own website should also publish the advertised tender enquiries there.

Q206. The contents of the tender enquiry for a non-consulting service under Rule 200 include all of the following EXCEPT —
A. Details of the work/service to be performed by the contractor
B. The facilities and inputs to be provided by the Ministry/Department
C. Eligibility and qualification criteria, and statutory/contractual obligations
D. The internal cost estimate of the Department
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 200 lists four contents: details of the work/service; facilities/inputs provided by the Department; eligibility and qualification criteria; and statutory and contractual obligations to be complied with. The Department's internal cost estimate is not among them.

Q207. Likely contractors for a non-consulting service are identified under Rule 199 by means such as —
A. A reverse auction
B. Scrutiny of 'Yellow Pages', trade journals and websites, and organisations involved in similar activities
C. A Global Tender Enquiry only
D. The single recommendation of the user department
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 199 contemplates identification of likely contractors by reference to organisations involved in similar activities and scrutiny of 'Yellow Pages', trade journals (if available), websites, etc.

Q208. Late bids in the procurement of non-consulting services are governed by Rule 202, the effect of which (consistent with the scheme of the chapter) is that bids received after the specified date and time —
A. Are considered if the price is lowest
B. Should not be considered
C. Are condoned automatically
D. Are referred to GeM
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 202 deals with late bids; consistent with the chapter's scheme (e.g., Rules 165 and 188), bids received after the specified date and time are not to be considered, preserving the finality of the deadline.

Q209. Procurement of a non-consulting service from a specifically chosen contractor by nomination (Rule 204) may be done, in an exceptional situation, by the Competent Authority —
A. Without any consultation
B. In consultation with the Financial Adviser
C. Only with Cabinet approval
D. Only through GeM
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 204 provides that, should it become necessary in an exceptional situation to procure a non-consulting service from a specifically chosen contractor, the Competent Authority may do so in consultation with the Financial Adviser.

Q210. When procuring a non-consulting service by nomination under Rule 204, an integral part of the proposal must be —
A. A buy-back clause
B. The detailed justification, the circumstances leading to the choice, and the special interest/purpose it serves
C. The names of three rejected contractors
D. A reverse-auction record
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 204 requires that the detailed justification, the circumstances leading to procurement by choice, and the special interest or purpose it shall serve form an integral part of the proposal, ensuring accountability for the departure from competition.

Q211. For any circumstances not covered in Rules 198 to 205 for non-consulting services, the procuring entity may refer to —
A. Rules 177 to 196 (consulting services)
B. Rules 135 to 176 (procurement of goods), and not the rules for consulting services
C. The DFPR alone
D. The Companies Act, 2013
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 206 provides that, for circumstances not covered in Rules 198–205, the procuring entity may refer to Rules 135–176 pertaining to procurement of goods — and expressly not to the rules for consulting services.

Q212. A Department needs to outsource security services estimated at ₹35 lakh. The correct approach under Rule 201 is to —
A. Issue an advertisement on the CPPP and GeM
B. Issue a limited tender enquiry to more than three prima facie eligible and capable contractors
C. Constitute a Consultancy Evaluation Committee
D. Resort to Single Source Selection
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: At ₹35 lakh (up to ₹50 lakh), Rule 201(i) applies — the Department identifies likely contractors (Rule 199), decides the prima facie eligible and capable ones, and issues a limited tender enquiry to more than three of them.

Q213. A Department needs to outsource facilities management estimated at ₹80 lakh. Under Rule 201, it must —
A. Issue a limited tender enquiry to three firms
B. Issue an advertisement on the CPPP (www.eprocure.gov.in) and on GeM
C. Procure without quotation
D. Use the Two-Bid System for goods
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: At ₹80 lakh (above ₹50 lakh), Rule 201(ii) requires an advertisement on the CPPP and on GeM; if the organisation has its own website, the advertised tender enquiry should also be published there.

Q214. Consider the following statements regarding non-consulting services:
1. The definition turns on physical, measurable deliverables with clear performance standards.
2. Up to ₹50 lakh, a limited tender enquiry to more than three contractors is issued.
3. For circumstances not covered in Rules 198–205, reference is made to the rules for consulting services.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct (Rules 197 and 201(i)). Statement 3 is wrong — Rule 206 directs reference to Rules 135–176 (procurement of goods), expressly NOT to the rules for consulting services.
