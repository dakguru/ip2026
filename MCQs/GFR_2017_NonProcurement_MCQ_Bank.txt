── Sub-Section 1.1: Rule 1 — Short Title & Commencement · 🟢 STANDARD ──

Q1. The General Financial Rules, 2017 apply to Autonomous Bodies of the Central Government in which of the following ways?
A. They never apply to Autonomous Bodies
B. They apply only to bodies created after 2017
C. They apply, except where the body's own Government-approved Financial Rules provide otherwise
D. They apply only with the prior approval of the C&AG in each case
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Under Rule 1, the GFR 2017 apply to all Central Government Ministries/Departments and their attached and subordinate offices, and also to Autonomous Bodies — but the latter are governed by their own Financial Rules where those have been approved by Government and provide otherwise.

Q2. The General Financial Rules, 2017 came into force —
A. On 1st April 2017
B. On 1st April of the financial year following notification
C. Three months after publication in the Gazette
D. At once (immediately on notification)
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 1 provides that the Rules are called the General Financial Rules, 2017 and come into force at once, applying to all Central Government Ministries/Departments and their attached and subordinate bodies.

── Sub-Section 1.2: Rule 2 — Definitions · 🔴 CRITICAL · Q3–Q14 ──

Q3. 'Appropriation' under the GFR 2017 means —
A. The total budget of a Ministry for the year
B. The assignment of funds in a primary unit of appropriation to meet specified expenditure
C. The transfer of funds from one grant to another grant
D. The surrender of unspent funds at year-end
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: 'Appropriation' is defined as the assignment of funds, from a primary unit of appropriation, to meet specified expenditure included in a primary unit. It is distinct from re-appropriation, which is a transfer between primary units.

Q4. Which authority is the 'Competent Authority' under the GFR 2017?
A. Only the Secretary of the Department
B. Only the Finance Minister
C. Only the C&AG
D. The President, or the authority to whom the power is delegated under the Rules, DFPR or other orders
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: 'Competent Authority' means the President or the authority to which the relevant power has been delegated under these Rules, the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules, or by other general or special orders.

Q5. The Consolidated Fund of India is constituted under which Article of the Constitution?
A. Article 266(2)
B. Article 266(1)
C. Article 267(1)
D. Article 283(1)
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Under the GFR definitions, the Consolidated Fund corresponds to the Consolidated Fund of India under Article 266(1); the Public Account is under Article 266(2); and the Contingency Fund under Article 267(1).

Q6. The Contingency Fund of India was set up under —
A. Article 266(1) of the Constitution
B. The Government Accounting Rules, 1990
C. The Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950 (Article 267(1))
D. Article 283(2) of the Constitution
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The GFR definition ties the Contingency Fund to the Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950, which gives effect to Article 267(1) of the Constitution.

Q7. The Public Account of India is referable to which constitutional provision?
A. Article 266(1)
B. Article 267(2)
C. Article 266(2)
D. Article 284
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The Public Account corresponds to Article 266(2). Article 266(1) covers the Consolidated Fund, and Article 267(1) the Contingency Fund.

Q8. A 'Controlling Officer' under the GFR 2017 is an officer responsible for —
A. Controlling expenditure and/or collecting revenue, and includes a Head of Department and an Administrator
B. Only controlling expenditure
C. Only collecting revenue
D. Maintaining the cash book at the Sub-Treasury
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: A Controlling Officer is one entrusted with controlling expenditure and/or collecting revenue; the definition expressly includes a Head of Department and an Administrator.

Q9. A 'Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO)' is —
A. A Head of Office or other Gazetted Officer designated to draw bills and make payments
B. Any clerk authorised to write the cash book
C. Only the Pay and Accounts Officer
D. Any officer of and above the rank of Deputy Secretary
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: The DDO is defined as a Head of Office (or any other Gazetted Officer) so designated to draw bills and make payments on behalf of the Central Government.

Q10. To qualify as a 'Head of Department' under the GFR 2017, an authority must not be below the rank of —
A. Under Secretary
B. Director
C. Deputy Secretary
D. Joint Secretary
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: 'Head of Department' means an authority not below the rank of Deputy Secretary, declared as such, to exercise delegated financial powers.

Q11. Under the GFR 2017, the 'Financial Year' runs from —
A. 1st January to 31st December
B. 1st October to 30th September
C. 1st July to 30th June
D. 1st April to 31st March
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: The Financial Year is defined as the year beginning on 1st April and ending on 31st March of the following year — consistent with Rules 42 and 75.

Q12. 'Re-appropriation' is best described as —
A. The assignment of funds within a primary unit of appropriation
B. The lapse of unspent funds at year-end
C. The transfer of funds from one primary unit of appropriation to another
D. An advance from the Contingency Fund
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Re-appropriation is the transfer of funds from one primary unit of appropriation to another — to be contrasted with 'appropriation', which is an assignment within a primary unit.

Q13. Expenditure incurred at periodical intervals for the same purpose is classified as —
A. Non-recurring expenditure
B. Charged expenditure
C. Capital expenditure
D. Recurring expenditure
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Recurring expenditure is that incurred at periodical intervals for the same continuing purpose; all expenditure that is not recurring is non-recurring.

Q14. Under the two procurement models defined in the GFR, the model in which the seller supplies, maintains and takes back the asset while the buyer pays in a staggered manner is —
A. CAPEX
B. OPEX
C. Turnkey
D. Build-Operate-Transfer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: OPEX is the model where the seller supplies and maintains the asset and ultimately takes it back, with the buyer paying in a staggered manner. Under CAPEX, the buyer purchases, maintains and then disposes of the asset.

── Sub-Section 1.3: Rules 3–6 — Consultation, Regulation, Doubts, Modifications · 🟢 STANDARD · Q15–Q19 ──

Q15. Where a financial case concerns more than one Department and the Departments fail to concur, the matter is decided —
A. By the senior-most Secretary
B. By the C&AG
C. By or under the Cabinet
D. By the President directly in every case
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 3 provides that where a case concerns more than one Department, no order is issued until all concur; failing concurrence, the decision is taken by, or under the authority of, the Cabinet.

Q16. A departmental regulation of a financial character must be made by, or with the approval of —
A. The Cabinet Secretariat
B. The Ministry of Finance
C. The C&AG
D. The Department of Personnel & Training
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 4 requires that any departmental regulation of a financial character, or one with important financial bearing, be made by or with the approval of the Ministry of Finance.

Q17. Doubts on the interpretation of the GFR 2017 are referred for decision to —
A. The Ministry of Finance
B. The Cabinet
C. The C&AG
D. The Department of Legal Affairs
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Under Rule 5 (Removal of Doubts), any doubt regarding interpretation of the Rules is referred to the Ministry of Finance, whose decision is final.

Q18. The systems and procedures in the GFR 2017 may be modified by another authority only with —
A. The express approval of the Ministry of Finance
B. The concurrence of the C&AG
C. A resolution of Parliament
D. The approval of the administrative Secretary
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 6 provides that the Rules are subject to general or special instructions of the Ministry of Finance and may be modified by any other authority only with the express approval of that Ministry.

Q19. Consider the following statements regarding the introductory Rules of the GFR 2017:
1. The Rules apply to attached and subordinate offices of Central Ministries.
2. Doubts on interpretation are settled by the C&AG.
3. The Ministry of Finance may issue general or special instructions modifying the systems and procedures.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Statements 1 (Rule 1) and 3 (Rule 6) are correct. Statement 2 is wrong — doubts on interpretation are referred to the Ministry of Finance under Rule 5, not the C&AG.

── Sub-Section 2A.1: Rules 7–9 — Receipts into Government Account · 🟢 STANDARD · Q20–Q23 ──

Q20. All moneys received by or on behalf of Government must be brought into the Government Account —
A. Within thirty days
B. By the end of the financial year
C. Without delay
D. Only after assessment by the Accounts Officer
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 7 requires that all moneys received by or for Government — dues, deposits and remittances — be brought into the Government Account without delay, in accordance with rules made under Articles 150 and 283(1).

Q21. Moneys received by a Union officer (other than Government revenue), and moneys with a court other than a High Court in a Union Territory, are paid into the —
A. Public Account
B. Contingency Fund
C. Consolidated Fund
D. Personal Deposit Account of the officer
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 8(1), giving effect to Article 284, directs that such moneys — received by a Union officer otherwise than as Government revenue, or held by the Supreme Court or a non-High-Court court in a UT — be paid into the Public Account.

Q22. The head of account for credit and withdrawal of moneys under Article 284 is governed by the Government Accounting Rules 1990 and which other set of rules?
A. The Fundamental Rules
B. The Treasury Rules, 1934
C. The Central Government Account (Receipts and Payments) Rules, 1983
D. The Delegation of Financial Powers Rules
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 8(2) provides that the head of account is governed by the Government Accounting Rules 1990 and the Central Government Account (Receipts and Payments) Rules, 1983.

Q23. The duty to ensure that receipts and dues are correctly and promptly assessed, collected and credited to the Consolidated Fund or Public Account rests with —
A. The Reserve Bank of India
B. The concerned Department
C. The Pay and Accounts Officer alone
D. The C&AG
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 9 places on the Department the responsibility to ensure that all receipts and dues are correctly and promptly assessed, collected and duly credited.

── Sub-Section 2A.2: Rules 10–18 — Reconciliation, Receipt Books, Dues, Fines, Remission · 🟡/🟢 · Q24–Q33 ──

Q24. Under Rule 10, reconciliation of receipts requires the Controlling Officer to —
A. Obtain monthly accounts from subordinates and compare them with the Accounts Officer's credit statements
B. Conduct an annual physical verification of cash
C. Send quarterly returns to the Finance Ministry
D. Audit the accounts of subordinate offices
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 10 requires the Controlling Officer to obtain monthly accounts from subordinates and reconcile them with the credit statements of the Accounts Officer, who in turn sends a monthly extract of credits to the Controlling Officer.

Q25. Officers who receive money on behalf of Government issue receipts in which form?
A. Form GAR-6
B. Form GAR-7
C. Form GFR-19
D. Form GFR-12A
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 11(2) refers to receipts issued in Form GAR-6, and requires departmental rules to provide for proper accounting of receipt books and a check of used books.

Q26. Sums due to Government shall not be credited as revenue by debit to a suspense head unless specially authorised, because —
A. Suspense heads are not part of Government accounts
B. Revenue can only be credited at year-end
C. The Accounts Officer prohibits all suspense entries
D. The credit must follow, not precede, the actual realization
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 13 forbids crediting revenue by debit to a suspense head (absent special authorisation) — the credit to revenue must follow the actual realization, not anticipate it.

Q27. A claim to revenue shall not be remitted or abandoned except with —
A. The approval of the C&AG
B. The sanction of the competent authority
C. A resolution of Parliament
D. The concurrence of the Pay and Accounts Officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 18 provides that a claim to revenue is not to be remitted or abandoned save with the sanction of the competent authority.

Q28. Responsibility for the due assessment and realization of rent of a rentable building maintained by a civil department (other than CPWD) lies with —
A. The CPWD Engineer
B. The Head of Department
C. The Estate Officer of the Ministry of Urban Development
D. The Pay and Accounts Officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 15 makes the Head of Department responsible for due assessment and realization of rent of rentable buildings/lands maintained by his civil department, following CPWD-aligned rules.

Q29. Every authority empowered to impose or realize a fine must, among other things —
A. Remit the fine if recovery proves difficult
B. Obtain the C&AG's prior approval for every fine
C. Credit the fine to its own departmental fund
D. Ensure the money is realized, checked and deposited, and guard against double refunds
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 16 requires the empowered authority to ensure realization, check realizations against demand, and guard against double refunds of fines.

Q30. The annual statement of remissions and abandonments of revenue is submitted by Departments/Heads of Departments — but which Department is expressly excepted?
A. Department of Telecommunications
B. Department of Revenue
C. Department of Posts
D. Ministry of Railways
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 19(1) requires submission of the annual statement of remissions by 1st June each year, but expressly excepts the Department of Posts.

Q31. The annual statement of remissions of revenue must be submitted by —
A. 1st June each year
B. 1st April each year
C. 31st March each year
D. 30th September each year
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 19(1) fixes 1st June each year as the date by which the statement of remissions and abandonments is submitted to the Audit and Accounts Officers.

Q32. Individual remissions of revenue need NOT be included in the annual statement if they are below —
A. Rs.500
B. Rs.5,000
C. Rs.1,000
D. Rs.10,000
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 19(1) provides that individual remissions below Rs.1,000 need not be shown in the annual statement of remissions.

Q33. Accounts Officers watch the realization of miscellaneous demands not falling under ordinary revenue administration. Which of the following is such a demand?
A. Income tax on salaries
B. Contributions recoverable from State Governments, Local Funds and contractors
C. Customs duty on imports
D. GST on departmental purchases
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 17 covers miscellaneous demands — e.g. contributions from State Governments, Local Funds and contractors — which fall outside ordinary revenue administration and are watched by Accounts Officers.

── Sub-Section 2B.1: Rule 21 — Standards of Financial Propriety · 🔴 CRITICAL · Q34–Q41 ──

Q34. The standard of vigilance a Government officer must exercise over public money is that of —
A. A person of ordinary prudence exercising over his own money
B. A chartered accountant auditing a company
C. A trustee of a charitable endowment
D. A banker handling depositors' funds
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 21(i) embodies the cardinal canon of financial propriety: every officer should exercise the same vigilance over public money as a person of ordinary prudence would exercise over his own money.

Q35. Under the standards of financial propriety, expenditure sanctioned should not be —
A. Prima facie more than the occasion demands
B. Met from the Consolidated Fund
C. Classified as recurring
D. Subject to audit
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 21(ii) requires that expenditure not be prima facie more than the occasion demands — a check against extravagance.

Q36. The principle that no authority should sanction expenditure that benefits itself directly or indirectly is contained in —
A. The lapse-of-sanction rule (Rule 30)
B. The definition of Competent Authority (Rule 2)
C. The Standards of Financial Propriety (Rule 21)
D. The re-appropriation bar (Rule 65)
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 21(iii) provides that no authority should exercise its power of sanctioning expenditure to pass an order that will be, directly or indirectly, to its own advantage.

Q37. Public money may be spent for the benefit of a particular person or section only if the claim is enforceable in a Court of Law OR —
A. The expenditure is below Rs.10,000
B. The Accounts Officer certifies it
C. It is in pursuance of a recognised policy or custom
D. It is approved by the Internal Finance Wing
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 21(iv) permits expenditure for a particular person/section only where (a) the amount is claimable as a legal right, or (b) the expenditure is in pursuance of a recognised policy or custom.

Q38. Consider the following principles of financial propriety under Rule 21:
1. Exercise the same vigilance as over one's own money.
2. Expenditure should not be more than the occasion demands.
3. An authority may sanction expenditure to its own indirect advantage if economical.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 3 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Principles 1 and 2 are correct. Principle 3 is the negation of Rule 21(iii), which forbids sanctioning expenditure that benefits the sanctioning authority directly or indirectly.

Q39. The duty to enforce financial order and strict economy and to see that all financial rules are observed by one's own office and subordinate DDOs falls on —
A. Only the Pay and Accounts Officer
B. Every officer incurring or authorising expenditure
C. Only the Head of Department
D. Only the Financial Adviser
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 21 casts on every officer authorising or incurring expenditure the duty to observe high standards of financial propriety, enforce financial order and strict economy, and ensure observance of financial rules by his office and subordinate DDOs.

Q40. A Drawing & Disbursing Officer sanctions a costly farewell function from office contingencies that primarily benefits himself on retirement. This most directly violates —
A. Rule 21(iii) — no self-benefit
B. Rule 21(i) — ordinary prudence
C. Rule 26 — control of expenditure
D. Rule 30 — lapse of sanction
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: The scenario engages Rule 21(iii) — the prohibition on an authority sanctioning expenditure that is, directly or indirectly, to its own advantage.

Q41. Which of the following is NOT one of the enumerated standards of financial propriety under Rule 21?
A. Spending only within the financial year
B. Expenditure not more than the occasion demands
C. Vigilance equal to that over one's own money
D. No spending for a particular section unless legally enforceable or per recognised policy/custom
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: "Spending only within the financial year" is not a Rule 21 canon (that relates to lapse of grants/sanctions). The other three are the enumerated propriety standards.

── Sub-Section 2B.2: Rules 22–28 — Expenditure, Delegation, Sanctions · 🟢/🟡 · Q42–Q50 ──

Q42. No authority may incur expenditure or transfer money from public funds without —
A. The RBI's clearance
B. The C&AG's audit certificate
C. A Parliamentary resolution in each case
D. Competent-authority sanction
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 22 prohibits incurring expenditure, entering into a liability, or transferring moneys from the Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund or Public Account without the sanction of the competent authority.

Q43. Financial powers of Government not delegated to any subordinate authority vest in —
A. The President
B. The Cabinet
C. The Finance Ministry
D. The administrative Secretary
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 23 provides that financial powers are delegated through the DFPR; powers not so delegated continue to vest in the Finance Ministry.

Q44. All sanctions to expenditure must indicate the provision in the relevant grant or appropriation from which the expenditure is to be met. A sanction issued before funds are communicated must be —
A. Treated as void
B. Limited to Rs.1 lakh
C. Referred to the C&AG
D. Made subject to budget provision
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 25 requires every sanction to indicate the relevant provision; where a sanction is issued before funds are communicated, it is to be made expressly subject to budget provision.

Q45. Under Rule 26, the Controlling Officer must ensure that expenditure does NOT —
A. Get incurred before 1st April
B. Get audited by the C&AG
C. Exceed Rs.1 lakh without re-appropriation
D. Exceed the budget allocation, is for the sanctioned purpose, and is in the public interest
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 26 requires the Controlling Officer to ensure expenditure does not exceed the budget allocation, is incurred for the sanctioned purpose, is in the public interest, and has a control mechanism against errors, waste and loss.

Q46. Unless otherwise specified, a rule, sanction or order takes effect from —
A. The first day of the financial year
B. The date of the C&AG's approval
C. The date of issue
D. The date it is endorsed to the Accounts Officer
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 27(1) provides that rules, sanctions and orders take effect from the date of issue unless a different date is specified.

Q47. An order creating a temporary post must specify —
A. Only the recruiting authority
B. Only the pay scale
C. The date of creation and the sanctioned duration
D. The pension liability
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 27(2) requires temporary-post creation orders to state the date of creation and the period for which the post is sanctioned.

Q48. Without the prior consent of the Finance Ministry, a subordinate authority shall NOT —
A. Sanction TA to staff
B. Maintain a cash book
C. Pay salaries
D. Grant land, assign or relinquish revenue, or grant a concession/lease of mineral, forest, water or power rights
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 28 (amended vide DoE OM No.14(37)/2015-E.II.A dated 12.07.2024) bars a subordinate authority, without prior Finance Ministry consent, from granting land, assigning/relinquishing revenue, or granting concessions/leases/licences of mineral, forest, water or power rights.

Q49. Rule 28 of the GFR 2017 was amended by which Office Memorandum?
A. DoE OM No.F.1/3/2024-PPD dated 10.07.2024
B. DoE OM No.14(37)/2015-E.II.A dated 12.07.2024
C. DEA OM dated 23.02.2024
D. DoE OM dated 01.04.2024
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 28 (Powers in Regard to Certain Special Matters) was amended vide Department of Expenditure OM No.14(37)/2015-E.II.A dated 12.07.2024 — a non-procurement amendment, distinct from the 10.07.2024 procurement OM.

Q50. All draft EFC/PIB/CCEA/Cabinet memoranda are circulated after consultation with —
A. The C&AG
B. The concerned Financial Adviser, with a confirmation to that effect
C. The Cabinet Secretary
D. The Pay and Accounts Officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 24 requires that EFC/PIB/CEE/CCEA/Cabinet memoranda be circulated only after consultation with the concerned Financial Adviser, and that a confirmation to that effect be included.

── Sub-Section 2B.3: Rule 29 — Communication of Sanctions · 🟡 HIGH · Q51–Q55 ──

Q51. Financial sanctions on which the Accounts Officer makes or authorises payment are —
A. Issued as an Order addressed to no one
B. Addressed to the Accounts Officer
C. Endorsed only to the C&AG
D. Communicated orally
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 29(i) provides that sanctions on which the Accounts Officer is to make or authorise payment are addressed to him; all other sanctions (clause ii) are issued as an Order with a copy endorsed to the Accounts Officer.

Q52. For non-recurring expenditure, sanction may be accorded by —
A. The RBI's endorsement
B. A Parliamentary resolution
C. Signing or countersigning the bill or voucher, instead of a separate sanction
D. A press notification
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 29(iii) allows sanction for non-recurring expenditure to be accorded by signing or countersigning the bill or voucher (before or after drawal), dispensing with a separate sanction.

Q53. A sanction issued with the concurrence of the C&AG, MHA or DoPT must —
A. State so, citing the number and date of that concurrence
B. Be kept confidential
C. Be limited to Rs.1 lakh
D. Be issued only by the Secretary
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 29(v) requires sanctions issued with the concurrence of MHA, C&AG or DoPT to say so, quoting the number and date of the concurrence.

Q54. Orders sanctioning a definite amount or limit must express it —
A. In figures only
B. In both words and figures
C. In words only
D. In Roman numerals
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 29(vi) requires that orders sanctioning a definite amount or limit express the amount both in words and in figures.

Q55. Which of the following routine sanctions need NOT be endorsed to Audit?
A. GPF advances and contingent expenditure under Head-of-Office powers
B. Sanction for a New Service
C. Re-appropriation to a New Instrument of Service
D. Advance from the Contingency Fund
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 29(xi) lists routine sanctions exempt from endorsement to Audit — employee advances, appointment/promotion/transfer, creation/continuation/abolition of posts, handing/taking over charge, GPF advances, contingent expenditure under Head-of-Office powers, and routine sanctions of subordinate heads.

── Sub-Section 2B.4: Rules 30–32 — Lapse of Sanctions · 🔴 CRITICAL · Q56–Q63 ──

Q56. A sanction for a fresh charge lapses, unless specifically renewed, if no payment is made within —
A. Six months from the date of issue
B. Three years from the date of issue
C. The close of the financial year in all cases
D. Twelve months from the date of issue
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 30 provides that a sanction for a fresh charge lapses if no payment, in whole or part, is made within twelve months from the date of issue, unless specifically renewed.

Q57. Where a sanction specifies that the expenditure is to be met from a particular financial year's budget, it lapses —
A. At the close of that financial year
B. After twelve months in every case
C. After three months
D. On the date of issue of the next year's budget
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Under the second proviso to Rule 30, if the sanction states the expenditure is met from a specified financial year's budget, it lapses at the close of that year (not on the general twelve-month rule).

Q58. For the purchase of stores, a sanction does NOT lapse if, within one year of issue —
A. The bill is passed by the Accounts Officer

Explanation: Rule 287 provides that at appointment every person declares the date of birth in the Christian era with documentary proof.

Q327. Under Rule 287, where the prescribed minimum qualification is Matriculation or above, the acceptable proof of date of birth is the —
A. Municipal Birth Certificate only
B. Matriculation Certificate
C. Affidavit of the candidate
D. Certificate of the Head of Office
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 287 provides that a Matriculation Certificate is the proof where the prescribed qualification is Matriculation or above; otherwise a Municipal Birth Certificate or a certificate from the recognised school last attended is valid.

Q328. The Service Book is verified —
A. Once at entry into service
B. Only at retirement
C. Every year by the Head of Office
D. Every five years
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 288(1) provides that the Service Book is verified every year by the Head of Office, who records a 'Service verified from… up to…' certificate.

Q329. The Service Book is maintained in —
A. Duplicate — the office keeps the first copy and gives the second to the servant
B. A single copy held by the office
C. Triplicate
D. A single copy held by the servant
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 288(2) provides that the Service Book is maintained in duplicate — the office keeps the first copy and gives the second to the servant for safe custody.

Q330. Under Rule 288(2), the second copy of the Service Book is given to existing staff within —
A. One month
B. Three months
C. One year
D. Six months
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 288(2) provides that the second copy is given to existing staff within 6 months (and to new appointees within 1 month).

Q331. Under Rule 288(2), the second copy of the Service Book is given to new appointees within —
A. One month
B. Three months
C. Six months
D. Fifteen days
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 288(2) provides that the second copy is given to new appointees within 1 month (and to existing staff within 6 months).

Q332. In January each year, the servant hands his copy of the Service Book to the office for updation; it is returned within —
A. 15 days
B. 30 days
C. 45 days
D. 60 days
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 288(3) provides that in January each year the servant hands his copy to the office for updation, and it is returned within 30 days.

Q333. A lost servant's copy of the Service Book is replaced on payment of —
A. ₹500
B. ₹250
C. ₹100
D. ₹1,000
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 288(4) provides that a lost servant's copy is replaced on payment of ₹500.

── Sub-Section 9.2: Limitation for Claims — TA, LTC, OTA, Arrears (Rules 289–298) ──

Q334. A TA claim falls due for payment on —
A. The day after the journey is completed
B. The day the journey commences
C. The first day of the next month
D. The date the bill is presented
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 290 provides that a TA claim falls due for payment on the day after the journey is completed.

Q335. A TA claim must be submitted within how many days of becoming due, failing which it stands forfeited?
A. 30 days
B. 45 days
C. 60 days
D. 90 days
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 290 provides that a TA claim must be submitted within 60 days of becoming due, failing which it stands forfeited.

Q336. An LTC claim falls due —
A. The day the outward journey begins
B. The day after the return journey ends
C. On the first day of the next financial year
D. On the date the advance is sanctioned
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 292 provides that an LTC claim falls due the day after the return journey ends, with prescribed time-limits for submission depending on whether an advance is drawn.

Q337. An overtime-allowance (OTA) claim falls due on —
A. The first day of the month following the month it relates to
B. The last day of the month it relates to
C. The day the overtime is worked
D. The first day of the next financial year
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 293 provides that an OTA claim falls due on the first day of the month following the month it relates to, and stands forfeited if not submitted within 60 days.

Q338. An OTA claim stands forfeited if not submitted within —
A. 60 days
B. 30 days
C. 90 days
D. One year
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 293 provides that an OTA claim stands forfeited if not submitted within 60 days of falling due.

Q339. An arrear claim preferred within how many years of becoming due is settled by the DDO/Accounts Officer after usual checks?
A. Two years
B. One year
C. Three years
D. Five years
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 295(1) provides that an arrear claim preferred within two years of becoming due is settled by the DDO/Accounts Officer after the usual checks.

Q340. Under Rule 295(2), the date a claim is 'preferred' is —
A. The date it falls due
B. The date it is presented at the disbursing office
C. The date it is sanctioned
D. The date it is passed for payment
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 295(2) provides that the date the claim is presented at the disbursing office is the date it is 'preferred'.

Q341. An arrear claim in abeyance beyond two years is investigated by the —
A. DDO
B. Internal Finance Wing
C. Accounts Officer alone
D. Head of Department (who may delegate to the appointing authority)
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 295(3) provides that a claim in abeyance beyond two years is investigated by the HoD; if satisfied about genuineness and the reasons for delay, it is paid after usual checks (the HoD may delegate this to the appointing authority).

Q342. A time-barred claim is paid with the express sanction of Government, issued with the prior consent of the —
A. Internal Finance Wing
B. Comptroller and Auditor General
C. Department of Expenditure
D. Controller General of Accounts
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 296(2) provides that a time-barred claim is paid with the express sanction of Government, issued with the prior consent of the Internal Finance Wing.

Q343. Under Rule 296(1), a time-barred claim is still entertained where the authority is satisfied that the claimant —
A. Was prevented from claiming in time by causes beyond his control
B. Has retired from service
C. Has obtained legal opinion
D. Belongs to a reserved category
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 296(1) provides that a time-barred claim is entertained where the authority is satisfied the claimant was prevented from claiming in time by causes beyond his control.

Q344. Retrospective effect is not given to sanctions revising pay or granting concessions, except in very special circumstances with the prior consent of the —
A. Department of Personnel and Training
B. Finance Ministry
C. Cabinet
D. Comptroller and Auditor General
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 298 provides that retrospective effect is not given to sanctions revising pay or granting concessions, except in very special circumstances with prior Finance Ministry consent.

── Sub-Section 9.3: PF Sanctions, Compensation, Public Debt & PLI (Rules 299–305) ──

Q345. A sanction to a Provident Fund advance or non-refundable withdrawal lapses, unless renewed, on the expiry of —
A. One month
B. Twelve months
C. Six months
D. Three months
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 299 provides that a sanction to a PF advance/non-refundable withdrawal lapses, unless renewed, on the expiry of three months (with stated exceptions).

Q346. Compensation for accidental loss of an officer's property is paid —
A. Automatically up to ₹50,000
B. Only with Finance Ministry approval, and not ordinarily for loss unconnected with official duty
C. By the Head of Office without reference
D. Only to Gazetted officers
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 302 provides that no compensation for accidental loss of an officer's property is paid except with Finance Ministry approval, and not ordinarily for loss not connected with official duty.

Q347. Public debt raised by issue of securities is managed by the —
A. Department of Economic Affairs
B. Reserve Bank
C. Controller General of Accounts
D. State Bank of India
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 303 provides that public debt raised by issue of securities is managed by the Reserve Bank, which also manages securities under other laws that so provide.

Q348. Recovery of subscriptions to, and withdrawals from, Provident Funds follows the —
A. General Financial Rules exclusively
B. Civil Accounts Manual alone
C. Income Tax Act
D. Respective PF Rules and the prescribed instructions
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 304(1) provides that recovery of subscriptions to, and withdrawals from, Provident Funds follows the respective PF Rules and the prescribed instructions.

Q349. Drawing officers maintain a record of PLI policy holders in which Form?
A. Form GFR 14
B. Form GFR 16
C. Form GFR 17
D. Form GFR 20
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 305(1) provides that drawing officers maintain a record of PLI policy holders in Form GFR 20, kept up to date with policy details and recoveries.

── Sub-Section 9.4: Security Deposits (Rules 306–308) ──

Q350. Every Government servant who handles cash or stores furnishes security in the amount and form prescribed by the competent authority and executes a —
A. Personal bond only
B. Bank guarantee
C. Letter of undertaking
D. Security bond
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 306(1) provides that every Government servant who handles cash or stores furnishes security, in the amount and form the competent authority prescribes, and executes a security bond.

Q351. Under Rule 306(2), the amount of security is based on the actual cash handled, excluding —
A. Cash in transit
B. Receipts below ₹1,000
C. Petty cash imprest
D. Account-payee cheques and drafts
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 306(2) provides that the amount is based on actual cash handled, excluding account-payee cheques and drafts.

Q352. Under Rule 306(3), security is furnished as a Fidelity Bond in which Form?
A. Form GFR 14
B. Form GFR 16
C. Form GFR 17
D. Form GFR 19
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 306(3) provides that security is furnished as a Fidelity Bond in Form GFR 17, with the security bond in Form GFR 14.

Q353. Under Rule 306(3), the security bond accompanying the Fidelity Bond is in which Form?
A. Form GFR 16
B. Form GFR 14
C. Form GFR 17
D. Form GFR 20
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 306(3) provides that the security bond is in Form GFR 14, with the Fidelity Bond in Form GFR 17.

Q354. Under Rule 306(4), a permanent servant officiating against a cash/store-handling post in a short-term vacancy may be exempted from furnishing security if the vacancy does not exceed —
A. Two months
B. Three months
C. Four months
D. Six months
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 306(4) provides that a permanent servant in a short-term vacancy not exceeding four months may be exempted if there is no risk, though a servant officiating otherwise furnishes the full prescribed security.

Q355. Under Rule 308, a security deposit taken from a Government servant is retained for at least —
A. Three months from the date he vacates the post
B. One year from the date he vacates the post
C. Six months from the date he vacates the post
D. Until audit is completed
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 308 provides that a security deposit is retained for at least six months from the date the servant vacates the post.

Q356. Under Rule 308, a security bond is retained —
A. For six months after the servant vacates
B. Until the next audit
C. For three years
D. Permanently, or until it is certain there is no further need for it
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 308 provides that a security bond is retained permanently, or until it is certain there is no further need for it (whereas the security deposit is retained for at least six months after vacating).

── Sub-Section 9.5: Land, Local Bodies & Government Companies (Rules 309–319) ──

Q357. Save as otherwise provided by law or rule, no Government land is sold without —
A. Approval of the local body
B. Prior Government sanction
C. A public auction
D. C&AG concurrence
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 309 provides that, save as otherwise provided by law/rule, no Government land (including that of autonomous bodies and PSUs) is sold without prior Government sanction.

Q358. Transfer of land between a Union Territory and a Central Government Department is on a —
A. Market-value basis
B. Cost-plus-10% basis
C. 'No profit no loss' basis
D. Concessional 50% basis
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 310(1) provides that transfer of land between a UT and a Central Government Department (or vice versa) is on a 'no profit no loss' basis.

Q359. Detailed instructions on Charitable Endowments and other Trusts are contained in which Appendix?
A. Appendix-9
B. Appendix-8
C. Appendix-12
D. Appendix-6
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 311 provides that detailed instructions on Charitable Endowments and other Trusts are in Appendix-8.

Q360. Under Rule 312, a local body ordinarily pays for services/works —
A. After completion
B. In equal half-yearly instalments
C. In advance, the estimated charges/cost, unless a specific Government order authorises otherwise
D. Only on audit certification
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 312(1) provides that a local body ordinarily pays in advance the estimated charges/cost, unless a specific Government order authorises another arrangement.

Q361. Any amount or loan a local body fails to pay on the due date may be adjusted from —
A. Its statutory grant
B. Its tax collections
C. Any non-statutory grant sanctioned to it
D. The Consolidated Fund
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 313 provides that any amount or loan a local body fails to pay on the due date may be adjusted from any non-statutory grant sanctioned to it.

Q362. Taxes, fines or revenues collected for local bodies are not appropriated directly to a local fund without first passing through the —
A. Public Account
B. Reserve Fund
C. Contingency Fund
D. Consolidated Fund
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 314 provides that taxes/fines/revenues collected for local bodies are not appropriated directly to a local fund without first passing through the Consolidated Fund.

Q363. Subject to which Article are the accounts of local and other non-Government bodies audited by the Indian Audit & Accounts Department?
A. Article 148
B. Article 151
C. Article 150
D. Article 149
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 316 provides that, subject to Article 149, accounts of local and other non-Government bodies are audited by the IA&AD on prescribed terms.

Q364. For Government Companies, the cost of Supplementary Audit under Section 143(6) of the Companies Act 2013 is waived where —
A. The company's turnover is below ₹1 crore
B. A private auditor is appointed
C. The C&AG audits through its own staff
D. The company is loss-making
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 318 provides that, for Government Companies, the cost of Supplementary Audit under Section 143(6) of the Companies Act 2013 is waived where the C&AG audits through its own staff.

Q365. Financial transactions between Government and local bodies are rounded off to the —
A. Nearest ten rupees
B. Nearest hundred rupees
C. Nearest rupee
D. Nearest paisa
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 319 provides that financial transactions between Government and local bodies are rounded off to the nearest rupee.

── Sub-Section 9.6: Records, Contingent & Misc. Expenditure (Rules 320–324) ──

Q366. No account-connected Government record is destroyed except as per which Appendix (and departmental rules)?
A. Appendix-9
B. Appendix-8
C. Appendix-12
D. Appendix-6
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 320(1) provides that no account-connected Government record is destroyed except per Appendix-9 (and departmental rules); records prescribed for permanent retention are not destroyed.

Q367. Rules on incurring contingent expenditure are contained in Section III of the Subsidiary Instructions to which Rules?
A. The General Financial Rules 2017
B. The Treasury Rules
C. The Delegation of Financial Powers Rules
D. The Central Government Account (Receipt & Payment) Rules, 2022
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 321 provides that rules on incurring contingent expenditure are in Section III of the Subsidiary Instructions to the Central Government Account (Receipt & Payment) Rules, 2022.

Q368. A permanent advance / imprest is granted to a servant by the Head of Department in consultation with the —
A. Department of Expenditure
B. Internal Finance Wing
C. Accounts Officer
D. Comptroller and Auditor General
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 322 provides that a permanent advance/imprest may be granted by the Head of Department in consultation with the Internal Finance Wing, kept to the minimum needed for smooth functioning.

Q369. A permanent advance/imprest is to be kept —
A. At a fixed statutory amount
B. At the discretion of the servant
C. Equal to one month's contingent expenditure
D. To the minimum needed for smooth functioning
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 322 provides that the permanent advance/imprest is kept to the minimum needed for smooth functioning, with maintenance per para 10.12 of the Civil Accounts Manual.

Q370. Under Rule 323(2), the adjustment bill for an advance for contingent/miscellaneous purposes, with any balance, is submitted within —
A. Seven days of drawal
B. Fifteen days of drawal
C. Thirty days of drawal
D. Sixty days of drawal
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Rule 323(2) provides that the adjustment bill, with any balance, is submitted within fifteen days of drawal, failing which it is recovered from the servant's next salary.

Q371. If the adjustment bill for an advance under Rule 323 is not submitted within the prescribed period, the amount is —
A. Written off
B. Carried forward to the next year
C. Recovered from the servant's next salary
D. Referred to the Internal Finance Wing
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Rule 323(2) provides that, failing submission of the adjustment bill within fifteen days of drawal, the amount is recovered from the servant's next salary.

Q372. A Ministry/Department may sanction an advance to a Government Pleader in law suits to which Government is a party, up to a maximum at a time of —
A. ₹10,000
B. ₹15,000
C. ₹50,000
D. ₹25,000
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Rule 324 provides that an advance to a Government Pleader may be sanctioned up to a maximum of ₹25,000 at a time, adjusted when the counsel's fee bill is settled. (This ₹25,000 figure is the current post-amendment ceiling set by DoE OM No. 14(37)/2015-E.II.A dated 12.07.2024.)

Q373. The advance to a Government Pleader under Rule 324 is adjusted —
A. When the counsel's fee bill is settled
B. Within fifteen days of drawal
C. At the end of the financial year
D. On disposal of the suit only
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rule 324 provides that the advance to a Government Pleader, up to ₹25,000 at a time, is adjusted when the counsel's fee bill is settled.

── Sub-Section 9.7: Multi-Statement & Synthesis (Chapter 12) ──

Q374. Consider the following statements about the Service Book under Rule 288:
1. It is verified every year by the Head of Office.
2. It is maintained in duplicate.
3. A lost servant's copy is replaced on payment of ₹1,000.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct (Rule 288(1) and (2)). Statement 3 is false — a lost servant's copy is replaced on payment of ₹500, not ₹1,000 (Rule 288(4)).

Q375. Consider the following time-limits under the GFR:
1. A TA claim must be submitted within 60 days of becoming due.
2. An arrear claim is settled if preferred within two years of becoming due.
3. A PF advance sanction lapses on the expiry of six months unless renewed.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
A. 2 and 3 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct (Rules 290 and 295(1)). Statement 3 is false — a PF advance sanction lapses on the expiry of three months, not six (Rule 299).

Q376. Match the time-limit to its provision — which pairing is INCORRECT?
A. Adjustment bill for contingent advance — 30 days (Rule 323)
B. OTA claim forfeiture — 60 days (Rule 293)
C. Service Book returned after January updation — 30 days (Rule 288)
D. TA claim submission — 60 days (Rule 290)
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: The incorrect pairing is D — the adjustment bill for a contingent advance is submitted within fifteen days of drawal, not 30 days (Rule 323(2)). The other three pairings are correct.

Q377. Consider the following statements about security under Rules 306–308:
1. The Fidelity Bond is in Form GFR 17.
2. The security bond is in Form GFR 14.
3. A security deposit is retained for at least one year after the servant vacates the post.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
A. 2 and 3 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct (Rule 306(3)). Statement 3 is false — a security deposit is retained for at least six months after the servant vacates the post, not one year (Rule 308).

Q378. Which of the following correctly states the position on the ₹25,000 Government Pleader advance under Rule 324?
A. It is a superseded figure doubled to ₹50,000 by the 10.07.2024 procurement OM
B. It applies only to suits before the Supreme Court
C. It is the current ceiling set by the non-procurement amendment of 12.07.2024 and was not affected by the procurement-limit OM
D. It is the per-annum aggregate limit, not a per-advance limit
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The ₹25,000 ceiling is the current post-amendment figure set by DoE OM No. 14(37)/2015-E.II.A dated 12.07.2024 (a non-procurement amendment). It is separate from, and was not affected by, the 10.07.2024 procurement-limit OM (F.1/3/2024-PPD). The "all limits doubled" note circulating online does not apply to Rule 324.
