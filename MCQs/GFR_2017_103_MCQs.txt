── Sub-Section 1.1: Rule 1-6 (Definitions & Basics) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q1–Q5 ──

Q1. Under Rule 2 of the GFR 2017, which of the following best defines "Appropriation"?
A. The transfer of funds from one minor head to another
B. The assignment of funds in a primary unit of appropriation to meet specified expenditure
C. An advance granted from the Contingency Fund of India
D. The expenditure charged directly on the Consolidated Fund
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 2 defines Appropriation strictly as the assignment of funds in a primary unit of appropriation to meet specified expenditure.

Q2. As per GFR 2017, an authority declared as a "Head of Department" to exercise delegated financial powers must NOT be below the rank of:
A. Under Secretary
B. Deputy Secretary
C. Director
D. Joint Secretary
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 2 stipulates that a Head of Department is an authority not below the rank of Deputy Secretary, declared as such to exercise delegated financial powers.

Q3. Consider the following statements regarding procurement models defined under GFR 2017:
1. Under the CAPEX model, the seller supplies and maintains the asset while the buyer pays in a staggered manner.
2. Under the OPEX model, the buyer purchases, maintains, and disposes of the asset.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• Both definitions are reversed. Rule 2 defines CAPEX as the model where the buyer purchases, maintains, and disposes of the asset. OPEX is where the seller supplies, maintains, and takes back the asset against staggered payments.

Q4. Who among the following is designated as a "Drawing & Disbursing Officer (DDO)"?
A. Any Group A officer in the Ministry
B. The Principal Accounts Officer
C. A Head of Office or other Gazetted Officer designated to draw bills and make payments
D. The Controlling Officer responsible for budget allocation
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 2 defines a DDO as a Head of Office or other Gazetted Officer specifically designated to draw bills and make payments.

Q5. If any doubts arise regarding the interpretation of the General Financial Rules, to whom are they referred for a final decision?
A. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India
B. The Ministry of Law and Justice
C. The Ministry of Finance
D. The Department of Personnel and Training
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 5 specifies that doubts on the interpretation of these Rules are referred directly to the Ministry of Finance for decision.

── Sub-Section 2.1: Receipts (Rules 7-20) · 🟡 HIGH · MCQs Q6–Q11 ──

Q6. Moneys received by any Union officer (other than Government revenue), such as deposits or remittances, are paid into which of the following accounts under Article 284?
A. The Consolidated Fund of India
B. The Contingency Fund of India
C. The Public Account
D. The Personal Deposit Account
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 8 states that moneys received by any Union officer (other than Government revenue) and moneys with courts (other than a High Court in a UT) are paid into the Public Account.

Q7. Under Rule 19, Departments must submit an annual statement of remissions and abandonments of revenue to the Audit & Accounts Officers. Individual remissions below what monetary threshold need NOT be included in this statement?
A. ₹500
B. ₹1,000
C. ₹5,000
D. ₹10,000
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 19(1) specifies that individual remissions below ₹1,000 need not be included in the annual statement of remissions.

Q8. Which of the following practices regarding the crediting of revenue is strictly prohibited under Rule 13?
A. Crediting revenue directly into the Public Account
B. Crediting sums as revenue by debit to a suspense head before actual realization
C. Remitting revenue through online e-Receipts portals
D. Consolidating miscellaneous demands into a single minor head
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 13 states that sums shall not be credited as revenue by debit to a suspense head (unless specially authorised); the credit must follow, not precede, actual realization.

Q9. Where a civil department (other than CPWD) maintains a rentable building, who is responsible for the due assessment and realization of rent?
A. The Principal Accounts Officer
B. The Head of Department
C. The Central Public Works Department
D. The Drawing and Disbursing Officer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 15 designates the Head of Department as the authority responsible for the due assessment and realization of rent per CPWD-aligned rules.

Q10. By which date must the annual statement of remissions and abandonments of revenue be submitted to the Audit & Accounts Officers each year?
A. 1st April
B. 30th April
C. 1st June
D. 31st December
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 19(1) states that Departments/Administrators/Heads of Departments submit this statement by 1st June each year.

Q11. A claim to revenue shall not be remitted or abandoned except with the sanction of:
A. The Finance Ministry exclusively
B. The Competent Authority
C. The Head of Office
D. The Comptroller and Auditor General
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 18 mandates that a claim to revenue shall not be remitted or abandoned except with the sanction of the competent authority.

── Sub-Section 2.2: General Principles of Expenditure (Rules 21-32) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q12–Q20 ──

Q12. Every officer incurring expenditure must observe the standards of financial propriety. Which of the following violates these standards?
A. Exercising the same vigilance as a person of ordinary prudence
B. Sanctioning expenditure that is prima facie no more than the occasion demands
C. Authorizing expenditure to fulfill a claim enforceable in a Court of Law
D. Sanctioning an allowance that directly benefits the sanctioning authority itself
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• Rule 21(iii) explicitly prohibits any authority from sanctioning expenditure that benefits itself directly or indirectly.

Q13. An order sanctioning the creation of a temporary post must specifically state:
A. The name of the person appointed to the post
B. The date of creation and the sanctioned duration
C. The exact object head from which the salary will be drawn
D. The prior approval of the Parliament
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 27(2) requires that temporary-post creation orders specify the date of creation and the sanctioned duration.

Q14. As a general rule under Rule 30, a sanction for a fresh charge lapses if no payment is made within what period from the date of issue?
A. 6 months
B. 12 months
C. 24 months
D. At the close of the financial year
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 30 states that a sanction for a fresh charge lapses, unless specifically renewed, if no payment (whole or part) is made within twelve months from the date of issue.

Q15. Under which of the following circumstances does a sanction NOT lapse even if no payment is made within 12 months?
A. A sanction for the grant of a Personal Pay allowance
B. A sanction for the purchase of stores where tenders have been accepted within one year of issue
C. A sanction for the construction of a minor work
D. A sanction endorsed to the Internal Finance Wing
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 30, Proviso (iii), explicitly states that for the purchase of stores, the sanction does not lapse if tenders are accepted or the indent is placed on the Central Purchase Organization within one year of issue.

Q16. A sanction issued on 10th August specifies that the expenditure is to be met from the budget provision of the current financial year. If no payment is made, when will this sanction lapse?
A. 9th August of the next year
B. 31st March of the current financial year
C. 31st December of the current year
D. It will not lapse until payment is made
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 30, Proviso (ii), states that if a sanction specifies the expenditure is to be met from a particular financial year's budget, it lapses at the close of that year.

Q17. Which of the following routine sanctions must STILL be endorsed to the Audit Officer?
A. Creation or continuation of temporary posts
B. Grant of GPF advances to employees
C. Handing and taking over charge reports
D. Grants of land or alienation of land revenue
Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
• Under Rule 29(xi), routine staff sanctions are exempt. However, Rule 29(xii) requires that grants of land/alienation of land revenue must be reported to Audit/Accounts in a consolidated monthly return.

Q18. Consider the following statements regarding the communication of sanctions:
1. Sanctions on which the Accounts Officer is to make payment are addressed directly to him.
2. Orders sanctioning a definite amount must express it only in figures to avoid clerical errors.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
• Statement 1 is correct per Rule 29(i). Statement 2 is incorrect because Rule 29(vi) strictly requires that orders sanctioning a definite amount express it in BOTH words and figures.

Q19. Without the prior consent of the Finance Ministry, a subordinate authority shall NOT:
A. Sanction contingent expenditure under Head of Office powers
B. Grant a concession, lease, or licence of mineral, forest, or water rights
C. Communicate a sanction directly to the Principal Accounts Officer
D. Process scheme-related estimates
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 28 strictly bars subordinate authorities from granting land, assigning revenue, or granting leases/licences of mineral, forest, water, or power rights without prior Finance Ministry consent.

Q20. Which of the following types of sanctions do NOT lapse under any general time limits?
A. Sanctions for non-recurring capital expenditure
B. Sanctions for fresh contingent charges
C. Year-to-year additions to a permanent establishment under a general scheme
D. Sanctions for advances to Government Pleaders
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 31 explicitly lists year-to-year additions to a permanent establishment under a general scheme, and allowances sanctioned but not drawn, as sanctions that do not lapse.

── Sub-Section 2.3: Defalcation & Losses (Rules 33-38) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q21–Q25 ──

Q21. Any loss or shortage of public money or departmental revenue must be reported immediately. However, petty losses are exempt. What is the monetary threshold for a petty loss?
A. Up to ₹2,000
B. Up to ₹5,000
C. Up to ₹10,000
D. Up to ₹25,000
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 33(1) exempts petty losses up to ₹10,000 from the mandatory immediate reporting requirement.

Q22. Which of the following revenue losses must be reported to the next higher authority and the Statutory Audit Officer?
A. A loss arising from an assessment mistake discovered too late for a supplementary claim
B. A refund allowed on the grounds that the claim was time-barred
C. A loss arising from the embezzlement of stamp duties by a clerk
D. An under-assessment where the law's interpretation was overruled after the time-limit
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 33(1) explicitly exempts assessment mistakes, overruled interpretations, and time-barred refunds. Embezzlement/defalcation, however, must be reported regardless of the amount.

Q23. The reporting of a severe loss due to fraud involves a two-stage process. When must the initial report be submitted?
A. Only after the police have filed an FIR
B. As soon as a loss is suspected
C. After the departmental inquiry is fully concluded
D. At the close of the financial year
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 33(3) mandates that the initial report be submitted as soon as a loss is suspected, followed by a final report after investigation.

Q24. In the event of a material loss of immovable property (such as buildings) by fire, flood, or cyclone, the loss must be reported if it exceeds:
A. ₹10,000
B. ₹25,000
C. ₹50,000
D. ₹1,00,000
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 35 states that all loss of immovable property exceeding ₹50,000 by natural cause must be reported as prescribed.

Q25. An amount lost by misappropriation or embezzlement may be redrawn on a simple receipt pending investigation or recovery. Whose prior approval is required for this redrawal?
A. The Comptroller and Auditor General
B. The Principal Accounts Officer
C. The authority competent to write-off the loss
D. The Drawing and Disbursing Officer
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 33(5) allows an amount lost by misappropriation to be redrawn on a simple receipt with the approval of the write-off authority.

── Sub-Section 3.1: Budget Preparation (Rules 42-56) · 🟡 HIGH · MCQs Q26–Q31 ──

Q26. Under which Article of the Constitution does the Finance Minister lay the Annual Financial Statement (Budget) before Parliament?
A. Article 110
B. Article 112
C. Article 114
D. Article 266
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 43(1) states that the Annual Financial Statement is laid under Article 112.

Q27. When fixing User Charges for Government services, which of the following principles must be followed?
A. They must be fixed exclusively by Acts of Parliament.
B. They are linked to appropriate price indices and reviewed at least every three years.
C. Any deviation resulting in charges below cost-recovery requires no special justification.
D. They must be reviewed monthly.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 47 specifies that user charges must be linked to appropriate price indices and reviewed at least every three years, and any deviation must be specifically recorded with justifying reasons.

Q28. Expenditure that is NOT subject to the vote of the legislature is known as:
A. Voted expenditure
B. Capital expenditure
C. Charged expenditure
D. Contingency expenditure
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 50(1) and Rule 83 define expenditure under Article 112(3) as Charged on the Consolidated Fund, which is not subject to a vote.

Q29. Detailed Demands for Grants of a Ministry are laid before which of the following bodies?
A. The Comptroller and Auditor General
B. The Departmentally-Related Standing Committee (DRSC)
C. The Public Accounts Committee
D. The NITI Aayog
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 51(3) specifies that Detailed Demands for Grants are laid before the Departmentally-Related Standing Committee (DRSC).

Q30. To meet expenditure before the Appropriation Bill is passed after the year begins, an interim grant is taken. What is this grant called and under which Article is it provided?
A. Supplementary Grant under Article 115
B. Vote on Account under Article 116
C. Contingency Advance under Article 267
D. Charged Appropriation under Article 112
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 55 states that an interim grant (Vote on Account) under Article 116 meets expenditure until the Appropriation Bill passes.

Q31. The Department of Expenditure, in consultation with NITI Aayog, prepares a specific budget linking scheme outlays to measurable outputs. What is this budget called?
A. The Performance Budget
B. The Outcome Budget
C. The Zero-Based Budget
D. The Capital Budget
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 54 defines the Outcome Budget, which links scheme outlays to measurable outputs and outcomes.

── Sub-Section 3.2: Control & Re-appropriation (Rules 57-70) · 🔴 CRITICAL · MCQs Q32–Q40 ──

Q32. To control expenditure and avoid a rush in March, the Controlling Officer obtains monthly liability statements (Form GFR 3A) from spending authorities starting from which month each year?
A. April
B. July
C. October
D. January
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 58 specifies that the liability statements are obtained from October each year.

Q33. Which of the following best defines a "New Service" expenditure under Rule 63?
A. Expenditure on an existing scheme exceeding the budget by 10%
B. Expenditure not contemplated in the Annual Budget, requiring a supplementary grant or Contingency Fund advance
C. Expenditure met through re-appropriation of anticipated savings
D. Any capital expenditure on infrastructure
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 63 states that no expenditure on a 'New Service' not contemplated in the Annual Budget can be incurred without a supplementary grant or CF advance.

Q34. Re-appropriation is the transfer of funds between primary units of appropriation. Which of the following is an ABSOLUTE bar on re-appropriation?
A. Transferring funds from a Revenue head to a Capital head
B. Transferring funds between two Minor Heads within the same grant
C. Transferring funds from Voted expenditure to Charged expenditure
D. Transferring funds in the last month of the financial year
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 65 explicitly bars re-appropriation from Voted to Charged (or vice versa), to a New Service, or to unsanctioned expenditure.

Q35. A re-appropriation order must explicitly state the reasons for any saving and excess if the amount transferred is:
A. ₹10,000 or more
B. ₹50,000 or more
C. ₹1,00,000 or more
D. ₹5,00,000 or more
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 65(4) dictates that the order must state reasons for saving and excess of ₹1 lakh or more.

Q36. Consider the following statements regarding re-appropriation:
1. Funds can be re-appropriated from a unit even if there is an intention to restore the amount later from other savings.
2. The application for re-appropriation must be supported by a statement in Form GFR 1.
Which of the above is/are correct?
A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Statement 1 is strictly prohibited under Rule 65(3). Statement 2 is correct per Rule 65(4).

Q37. If savings are NOT available within the grant, and expenditure on a New Service must be incurred, what must be obtained before authorizing payment?
A. An intra-departmental re-appropriation order
B. A Supplementary Grant or Appropriation under Article 115(1)
C. A Vote on Account under Article 116
D. A special waiver from the C&AG
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 66 requires a Supplementary Grant or Appropriation under Article 115(1) if savings are unavailable or for a New Service.

Q38. When there is not enough time to pass a supplementary appropriation before year-end for unforeseen expenditure, an advance is taken from the Contingency Fund. When must this advance be taken?
A. BEFORE incurring the expenditure
B. SIMULTANEOUSLY while paying the bill
C. AFTER the expenditure is incurred but before the year closes
D. In the next financial year as a regularizing measure
Correct Answer: A
Explanation:
• Rule 67(1) mandates that the advance from the Contingency Fund is taken BEFORE incurring the expenditure.

Q39. Who is designated as the Chief Accounting Authority of a Ministry/Department, responsible for its financial management?
A. The Financial Adviser
B. The Principal Accounts Officer
C. The Secretary of the Ministry/Department
D. The Head of Department
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 70 declares the Secretary of a Ministry/Department as its Chief Accounting Authority.

Q40. A Disbursing Officer realizes he does not have sufficient allotted funds to pass a bill. Under Rule 64, what action is he authorized to take on his own authority?
A. Pass the bill and adjust it against next year's budget
B. Pay the bill by diverting funds from a suspense head
C. He may not pay in excess of funds on his own authority; he must take the administrative authority's orders
D. Write directly to the Ministry of Finance for an immediate grant
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 64(2) explicitly states a Disbursing Officer may not, on his own authority, pay in excess of funds; he must take the administrative authority's orders to provide funds.

Q48. Who prepares the Annual Finance Accounts of the Government of India, showing receipts and disbursements?

• Rule 101 specifies that capital receipts accruing during construction are used to reduce capital expenditure.

Q51. By convention, what is the accepted time period for the re-audit of past transactions involving classification errors?
A. One year
B. Three years
C. Five years
D. Ten years
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 109 establishes that a period of three years is accepted for the re-audit of past transactions.

Q52. To facilitate inter-Governmental adjustments before the financial year closes, the RBI books for March remain open up to a crucial date. What is this date as per the amended Rule 117?
A. 31st March
B. 10th April
C. 30th April
D. 31st May

Explanation:
• The amended Rule 117 specifies that inter-Governmental adjustments may be carried out up to 10th April (or the date specified by the CGA).

Q53. Are Service Departments permitted to charge other Departments for services rendered within their normal duties?

C. No, except as expressly provided otherwise
D. No, unless the service is rendered to a Commercial Department

Q54. Inter-departmental claims must ordinarily be preferred within the same financial year. What is the absolute maximum time-limit from the date of transaction beyond which they cannot be preferred?
A. One year
B. Two years
C. Three years
D. Five years
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Rule 125 states that inter-departmental claims are not preferred beyond three years from the date of the transaction.

Q55. The pensionary liability of commercial departments (that maintain proforma accounts) is adjusted by charging what rate?
A. A flat fee of ₹10,000 per employee
B. The average or prescribed contribution rate
C. 10% of the departmental revenue
D. It is not charged to the commercial department at all
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 128 & 129 state that pensionary liability is assessed and adjusted on a contribution basis at the average/prescribed rate.

A. Original works
B. Minor works
C. Repair works
D. Maintenance works
Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
• Rule 130 defines minor works as those that add capital value but do not create a new asset. Original works create new assets or involve special repairs adding value.

Q57. A Ministry wishes to execute a repair work directly under its own arrangements. Following the 2024 amendment to Rule 133, what is the maximum estimated value of repair work it can execute directly?

B. Up to ₹30 lakh
C. Up to ₹60 lakh
D. Up to ₹5 crore
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• Amended Rule 133(1) allows a Ministry/Department to directly execute repair works estimated up to ₹60 lakh.

Q58. A Scientific Ministry needs to urgently assign a repair work on a nomination basis to a Public Works Organisation. Until 31.03.2025, it may do so for repair works up to what value?
A. ₹60 lakh
B. ₹1 crore
C. ₹5 crore
D. ₹10 crore
Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
• The notes to Rule 133 highlight a special provision allowing Scientific Ministries to assign repair works up to ₹5 crore on a nomination basis.

Q59. If a Ministry has an original civil work of ₹20 lakh to execute, how MUST it be handled under Rule 133?

D. It must be split into two minor works of ₹10 lakh each.

Explanation:
• Rule 133 allows direct execution ONLY for repair works up to ₹60 lakh. Original works of ANY value must be assigned to a PWO or eligible PSU.

Q60. When executing works directly under its own arrangements, a Ministry must call for open tenders if the work costs between:

Explanation:
• Rule 139(iv) sets the threshold for open tenders for works executed directly at ₹10 lakh to ₹60 lakh.

Q61. For works executed directly by a Ministry costing less than ₹10 lakh, what method of tendering is prescribed?
A. Open tenders
B. Limited tenders
C. Single tender (nomination)
D. Global tenders
